ORIGINAL USMLE-STYLE PRACTICE

Propranolol: 2

A patient with reactive airways develops wheezing after taking propranolol. The drug blocks a receptor that normally supports bronchial smooth-muscle relaxation. Which receptor-level effect best explains this respiratory adverse reaction?

  1. Intraoperative floppy iris syndrome
  2. Establish alpha blockade before adding beta blockade
  3. Beta1 blockade reducing heart rate
  4. Beta2 blockade causing bronchoconstriction
  5. Orthostatic hypotension
Answer and explanation

Beta2 blockade causing bronchoconstriction

Nonselective beta blockers can aggravate bronchospasm in susceptible patients.

Option explanations

Beta2 blockade causing bronchoconstriction: Nonselective beta blockers can aggravate bronchospasm in susceptible patients.

Orthostatic hypotension: Alpha1 blockade reduces the vasoconstrictor response to standing. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Intraoperative floppy iris syndrome: Alpha1 antagonists, especially tamsulosin, are associated with floppy iris during cataract surgery. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Establish alpha blockade before adding beta blockade: In pheochromocytoma, beta blockade before adequate alpha blockade can worsen vasoconstriction. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Beta1 blockade reducing heart rate: Cardiac beta1 blockade slows rate and can worsen bradycardia or conduction disease. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
