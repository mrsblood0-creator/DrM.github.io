Inflammation and renal protein loss

A 52-year-old woman with poorly controlled rheumatoid arthritis develops edema and heavy proteinuria. Renal biopsy confirms amyloid, and the deposits are typed as AA. Which circulating protein is the direct precursor of these fibrils?

  1. Serum amyloid A
  2. Immunoglobulin heavy chain
  3. Transthyretin
  4. Beta-2 microglobulin
  5. C-reactive protein
Answer and explanation

Serum amyloid A

Chronic inflammation increases hepatic production of the acute-phase protein SAA, from which AA fibrils are derived.

Option explanations

Serum amyloid A: Chronic inflammation increases hepatic production of the acute-phase protein SAA, from which AA fibrils are derived.

Immunoglobulin heavy chain: Heavy-chain amyloid is a rare separate entity and does not form AA fibrils.

Transthyretin: TTR is the precursor of ATTR, not AA, amyloid.

Beta-2 microglobulin: Beta-2 microglobulin causes dialysis-related amyloid.

C-reactive protein: CRP is an acute-phase reactant but is not the AA fibril precursor.
