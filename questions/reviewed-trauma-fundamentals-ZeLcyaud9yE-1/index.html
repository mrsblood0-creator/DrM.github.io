Acute compartment syndrome: 1

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

After a tibial fracture, a patient develops escalating pain despite analgesia, a tense leg compartment and severe pain with passive toe movement. Which urgent syndrome should be suspected rather than attributing all discomfort to the fracture?

  1. Acute compartment syndrome
  2. Preserved pulses do not exclude compartment syndrome
  3. Compartment-pressure assessment when needed
  4. Urgent surgical decompression
  5. Ischemic muscle contracture
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Acute compartment syndrome

Escalating pain, pain on passive stretch and a tense compartment after injury are concerning for pressure-related ischemia.

Understanding the options

Acute compartment syndrome
Escalating pain, pain on passive stretch and a tense compartment after injury are concerning for pressure-related ischemia.
Preserved pulses do not exclude compartment syndrome
Major arterial flow can remain present while microvascular tissue perfusion is compromised. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
Compartment-pressure assessment when needed
Pressure measurement can support diagnosis when the examination is unreliable; it must not delay obvious emergency treatment. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
Urgent surgical decompression
Established acute compartment syndrome requires urgent specialist treatment, usually fasciotomy. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
Ischemic muscle contracture
Untreated ischemia can cause muscle necrosis, fibrosis and permanent contracture. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
