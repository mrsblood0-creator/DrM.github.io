A clinician considers intravenous vancomycin for a serious susceptible MRSA infection. Another drug would be needed if Gram-negative coverage were required. Which broad bacterial group is the principal target of this glycopeptide in systemic practice?

  1. Anaerobic bacteria
  2. Atypical respiratory pathogens
  3. Aerobic Gram-negative bacilli
  4. Selected protozoal infections
  5. Resistant Gram-positive bacteria
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Resistant Gram-positive bacteria

Linezolid and vancomycin are used for selected resistant Gram-positive infections, depending on susceptibility and site.

Understanding the options

Resistant Gram-positive bacteria
Linezolid and vancomycin are used for selected resistant Gram-positive infections, depending on susceptibility and site.
Anaerobic bacteria
Metronidazole is active against many susceptible anaerobes; it does not replace aerobic coverage in a mixed infection. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
Atypical respiratory pathogens
Macrolides and tetracyclines can cover organisms such as Mycoplasma that are not treated by cell-wall inhibitors. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
Aerobic Gram-negative bacilli
Aminoglycosides are useful against selected aerobic Gram-negative organisms; oxygen-dependent uptake limits anaerobic activity. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
Selected protozoal infections
Metronidazole has activity against organisms such as Giardia and Trichomonas. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
