Oxygen therapy: 4

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A patient receiving supplemental oxygen becomes drowsy despite a reassuring saturation reading. The clinician suspects carbon dioxide retention and respiratory acidosis. Which investigation directly assesses these concerns rather than relying on oxygen saturation alone?

  1. Blood co-oximetry for carboxyhemoglobin
  2. Pulse oximetry
  3. Peak expiratory flow
  4. Chest radiography
  5. Arterial blood gas analysis
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Arterial blood gas analysis

Blood gases assess carbon dioxide and acid-base status as well as oxygen tension; saturation alone cannot assess ventilation.

Understanding the options

Arterial blood gas analysis
Blood gases assess carbon dioxide and acid-base status as well as oxygen tension; saturation alone cannot assess ventilation.
Blood co-oximetry for carboxyhemoglobin
Standard pulse oximetry can appear reassuring in carbon monoxide poisoning; co-oximetry measures abnormal hemoglobin species. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
Pulse oximetry
Pulse oximetry estimates peripheral oxygen saturation but does not directly measure carbon dioxide or reliably exclude dyshemoglobinemia. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
Peak expiratory flow
Peak flow helps assess expiratory airflow limitation and change over time, not carbon monoxide binding. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
Chest radiography
Radiographs show structural thoracic abnormalities but do not directly quantify blood gases or abnormal hemoglobin species. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
