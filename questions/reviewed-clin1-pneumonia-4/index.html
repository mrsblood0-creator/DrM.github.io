Atypical pneumonia in a school-aged child

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A 12-year-old develops gradually worsening dry cough, low-grade fever and headache during a school respiratory outbreak. Several classmates have similar symptoms. Mycoplasma pneumoniae is identified. Why would a beta-lactam antibiotic fail to directly target this organism?

  1. Absence of a peptidoglycan cell-wall target
  2. Production of a beta-lactamase that cleaves the drug
  3. Alteration of penicillin-binding proteins in a rigid cell wall
  4. Loss of outer-membrane porins preventing drug entry
  5. Active efflux of beta-lactams through a multidrug pump
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Absence of a peptidoglycan cell-wall target

Beta-lactams target cell-wall synthesis, and Mycoplasma lacks the relevant cell-wall structure.

Understanding the options

Absence of a peptidoglycan cell-wall target
Beta-lactams target cell-wall synthesis, and Mycoplasma lacks the relevant cell-wall structure.
Production of a beta-lactamase that cleaves the drug
Mycoplasma resistance to beta-lactams is intrinsic because the target is absent, rather than primarily due to beta-lactamase.
Alteration of penicillin-binding proteins in a rigid cell wall
This explains resistance in some wall-bearing bacteria; Mycoplasma lacks the conventional peptidoglycan wall.
Loss of outer-membrane porins preventing drug entry
Porin-mediated resistance occurs in some gram-negative bacteria, not as the defining reason for Mycoplasma beta-lactam resistance.
Active efflux of beta-lactams through a multidrug pump
Efflux is a resistance mechanism in other contexts but does not account for the absent beta-lactam target here.
