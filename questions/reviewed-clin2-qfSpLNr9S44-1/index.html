Hypercapnic respiratory failure

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A 69-year-old with COPD remains tachypneic after initial bronchodilator treatment. He is alert, cooperative and handling secretions. Arterial blood gas shows pH 7.28 and PaCO2 68 mm Hg. Blood pressure is stable, and there is no vomiting. Which respiratory support is most appropriate while closely monitoring for failure?

  1. A trial of bilevel noninvasive ventilation
  2. Continue low-flow oxygen alone without ventilatory reassessment
  3. Immediate intubation solely because PaCO2 exceeds 45 mm Hg
  4. CPAP as an identical substitute for inspiratory pressure support
  5. Withhold support until pH normalizes spontaneously
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

A trial of bilevel noninvasive ventilation

Acute respiratory acidosis in an appropriate COPD patient is a well-supported indication for bilevel NIV, with rapid access to intubation if needed.

Understanding the options

A trial of bilevel noninvasive ventilation
Acute respiratory acidosis in an appropriate COPD patient is a well-supported indication for bilevel NIV, with rapid access to intubation if needed.
Continue low-flow oxygen alone without ventilatory reassessment
Oxygen alone does not address the persistent hypercapnic ventilatory failure.
Immediate intubation solely because PaCO2 exceeds 45 mm Hg
Hypercapnia alone is not an automatic requirement for intubation in a cooperative stable candidate.
CPAP as an identical substitute for inspiratory pressure support
CPAP does not provide the same separate inspiratory assistance as bilevel NIV.
Withhold support until pH normalizes spontaneously
Acidosis and respiratory distress warrant active treatment.
