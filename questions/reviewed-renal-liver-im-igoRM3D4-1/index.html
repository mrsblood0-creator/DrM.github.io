Membranous nephropathy: 1

An adult with nephrotic syndrome has diffuse capillary-wall thickening and subepithelial immune deposits. Which glomerular disease best matches this biopsy pattern rather than a lesion with nearly normal light microscopy and absent immune deposits?

  1. Membranous nephropathy
  2. Minimal change disease
  3. Focal segmental glomerulosclerosis
  4. IgA nephropathy
  5. Infection-related glomerulonephritis
Answer and explanation

Membranous nephropathy

Subepithelial immune deposits and capillary-wall thickening characterize membranous nephropathy.

Option explanations

Membranous nephropathy: Subepithelial immune deposits and capillary-wall thickening characterize membranous nephropathy.

Minimal change disease: Childhood nephrotic syndrome with little change on light microscopy and diffuse foot-process effacement suggests minimal change disease. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Focal segmental glomerulosclerosis: FSGS is a pattern of segmental scarring affecting only some glomeruli; primary, adaptive and genetic causes must be distinguished. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

IgA nephropathy: Mesangial IgA deposition commonly presents with hematuria rather than the specified classic nephrotic patterns. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Infection-related glomerulonephritis: An inflammatory nephritic presentation after infection differs from a purely nephrotic podocyte pattern. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
