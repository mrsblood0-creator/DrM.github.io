ORIGINAL USMLE-STYLE PRACTICE

A missing degradative enzyme

A child has progressive enlargement of several organs. Biopsy shows lysosomes distended by a substrate, and testing identifies absence of the enzyme normally responsible for degrading it. Which general mechanism best explains the intracellular accumulation?

  1. Excessive removal of substrate by exocytosis
  2. Acute osmotic swelling from sodium pump failure
  3. Inherited failure of intracellular catabolism
  4. Physiologic hypertrophy from increased workload
  5. Replacement of one mature epithelial type by another
Answer and explanation

Inherited failure of intracellular catabolism

Lysosomal storage diseases result when deficient catabolic enzymes permit their substrates to accumulate.

Option explanations

Inherited failure of intracellular catabolism: Lysosomal storage diseases result when deficient catabolic enzymes permit their substrates to accumulate.

Physiologic hypertrophy from increased workload: Hypertrophy increases functional cellular components rather than undegraded lysosomal substrate.

Replacement of one mature epithelial type by another: Metaplasia changes cell identity rather than degrading capacity.

Excessive removal of substrate by exocytosis: Increased removal would reduce rather than expand the stored substrate.

Acute osmotic swelling from sodium pump failure: Pump failure causes water accumulation rather than substrate-specific lysosomal storage.
