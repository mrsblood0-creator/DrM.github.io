Inability to protect the airway

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A 58-year-old with respiratory failure becomes obtunded and repeatedly vomits. Oxygen saturation is falling despite supplemental oxygen. A clinician considers starting tight-mask bilevel NIV. Which feature most strongly favors an invasive airway strategy instead of a routine NIV trial?

  1. A history of COPD alone
  2. An elevated PaCO2 alone
  3. The need for supplemental oxygen alone
  4. Inability to protect the airway with active emesis
  5. A respiratory rate above 24/min alone
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Inability to protect the airway with active emesis

NIV does not seal the trachea and is unsuitable when airway protection is failing with active vomiting.

Understanding the options

Inability to protect the airway with active emesis
NIV does not seal the trachea and is unsuitable when airway protection is failing with active vomiting.
A respiratory rate above 24/min alone
Tachypnea can be a reason to consider NIV rather than an absolute exclusion.
A history of COPD alone
COPD exacerbations with respiratory acidosis are an important NIV indication.
An elevated PaCO2 alone
Hypercapnia alone does not exclude NIV.
The need for supplemental oxygen alone
Oxygen need does not by itself determine the airway strategy.
