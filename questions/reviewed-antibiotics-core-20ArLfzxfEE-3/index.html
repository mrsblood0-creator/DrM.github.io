ORIGINAL USMLE-STYLE PRACTICE

Clindamycin: 3

A staphylococcal isolate appears clindamycin susceptible but shows inducible resistance on a D-test performed because erythromycin resistance is present. The laboratory identifies an inducible methylase affecting ribosomal binding. Which mechanism explains the finding?

  1. High oral bioavailability
  2. Oxygen-dependent bacterial uptake
  3. Increased bacterial drug efflux
  4. Alteration of the ribosomal binding site
  5. Poor systemic absorption after oral administration
Answer and explanation

Alteration of the ribosomal binding site

Ribosomal methylation or other target changes can reduce binding of some protein-synthesis inhibitors.

Option explanations

Alteration of the ribosomal binding site: Ribosomal methylation or other target changes can reduce binding of some protein-synthesis inhibitors.

High oral bioavailability: Linezolid has high oral bioavailability, permitting an appropriate IV-to-oral switch in selected stable patients. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Oxygen-dependent bacterial uptake: Aminoglycosides require active oxygen-dependent transport into susceptible bacteria. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Increased bacterial drug efflux: Efflux can lower intracellular tetracycline concentrations and cause resistance. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
