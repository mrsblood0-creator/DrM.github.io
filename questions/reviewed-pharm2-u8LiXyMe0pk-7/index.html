Recognizing an airway complication

A patient receiving alteplase develops swelling of the tongue and lips with difficulty speaking. The patient also takes an ACE inhibitor. Which drug-associated complication best fits this abrupt change and requires immediate airway assessment?

  1. Orolingual angioedema
  2. Heparin-induced thrombocytopenia
  3. Warfarin-related skin necrosis
  4. Chronic pulmonary fibrosis
  5. Delayed iron-deficiency glossitis
Answer and explanation

Orolingual angioedema

Thrombolytic-associated angioedema can involve the tongue and threaten the airway.

Option explanations

Orolingual angioedema: Thrombolytic-associated angioedema can involve the tongue and threaten the airway.

Heparin-induced thrombocytopenia: HIT requires heparin-related immune platelet activation and does not explain tongue swelling.

Warfarin-related skin necrosis: Skin necrosis is a different anticoagulant complication.

Chronic pulmonary fibrosis: Fibrosis does not cause immediate orolingual swelling.

Delayed iron-deficiency glossitis: Iron deficiency develops gradually and has a different appearance.
