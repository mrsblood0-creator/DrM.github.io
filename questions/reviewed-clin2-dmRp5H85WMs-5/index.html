Reassurance after assessment

A healthy 31-year-old has occasional PACs during stressful weeks. He has no syncope, structural heart disease or persistent tachycardia, and symptoms are mild. After an appropriate evaluation, which initial plan is most reasonable?

  1. Prescribe lifelong amiodarone for every premature beat
  2. Explain the benign findings and address relevant triggers
  3. Implant a pacemaker immediately
  4. Begin anticoagulation solely for isolated PACs
  5. Perform emergency synchronized cardioversion
Answer and explanation

Explain the benign findings and address relevant triggers

Reassurance and individualized trigger management are reasonable for low-risk, mild ectopy.

Option explanations

Explain the benign findings and address relevant triggers: Reassurance and individualized trigger management are reasonable for low-risk, mild ectopy.

Implant a pacemaker immediately: There is no pacing indication in this presentation.

Begin anticoagulation solely for isolated PACs: Isolated PACs do not establish an anticoagulation indication.

Perform emergency synchronized cardioversion: There is no sustained unstable tachyarrhythmia.

Prescribe lifelong amiodarone for every premature beat: The risk of potent antiarrhythmics is disproportionate here.
