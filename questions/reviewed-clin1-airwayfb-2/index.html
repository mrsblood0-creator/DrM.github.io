Normal imaging with a convincing history

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A toddler choked on a small piece of carrot yesterday and has continued coughing. Examination now shows subtle unilateral wheeze. Chest radiography is normal. The child is stable. Which next step is most appropriate?

  1. Specialist referral for possible bronchoscopy
  2. A trial of asthma treatment before any airway referral
  3. Repeat a plain radiograph in several weeks if cough persists
  4. Oral antibiotics without further airway evaluation
  5. Upper gastrointestinal endoscopy as the next investigation
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Specialist referral for possible bronchoscopy

A convincing history and persistent focal symptoms warrant airway specialist assessment even when radiographs are normal.

Understanding the options

Specialist referral for possible bronchoscopy
A convincing history and persistent focal symptoms warrant airway specialist assessment even when radiographs are normal.
A trial of asthma treatment before any airway referral
A convincing choking history with persistent focal signs warrants prompt specialist assessment rather than delaying for an empiric asthma trial.
Repeat a plain radiograph in several weeks if cough persists
A normal film does not exclude a radiolucent object, and waiting can permit further complications.
Oral antibiotics without further airway evaluation
Antibiotics do not remove an aspirated object; no bacterial syndrome is described.
Upper gastrointestinal endoscopy as the next investigation
The focal wheeze and choking history indicate a respiratory rather than esophageal location.
