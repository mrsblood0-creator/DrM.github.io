Bradycardia during respiratory failure

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A 10-month-old with severe lower respiratory illness becomes cyanotic and bradycardic. Respirations are shallow and ineffective, and oxygen saturation falls to 76%. While the team assesses pulses and perfusion, which reversible problem requires immediate correction?

  1. Inadequate oxygenation and ventilation
  2. Hyperkalemia impairing cardiac conduction
  3. Hypoglycemia impairing neurologic function
  4. Hypothermia depressing sinus-node activity
  5. Increased vagal tone during airway suctioning
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Inadequate oxygenation and ventilation

Hypoxemia is an important cause of pediatric bradycardia; effective ventilation and oxygenation are priorities.

Understanding the options

Inadequate oxygenation and ventilation
Hypoxemia is an important cause of pediatric bradycardia; effective ventilation and oxygenation are priorities.
Hyperkalemia impairing cardiac conduction
Hyperkalemia can cause bradycardia, but the immediate observed failure is ineffective breathing with severe desaturation.
Hypoglycemia impairing neurologic function
Glucose should be assessed during resuscitation, but it does not replace correction of clearly inadequate ventilation.
Hypothermia depressing sinus-node activity
No cold exposure or hypothermia is described; the respiratory deterioration directly explains this change.
Increased vagal tone during airway suctioning
Vagal stimulation can slow heart rate, but no suctioning or other vagal trigger is described.
