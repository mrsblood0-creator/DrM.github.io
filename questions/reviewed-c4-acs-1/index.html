Normal initial ECG

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A 63-year-old man presents with ongoing pressure-like chest discomfort and diaphoresis. The first ECG does not show diagnostic ST elevation. His symptoms and risk profile remain concerning for acute coronary syndrome. Which conclusion is most appropriate?

  1. The patient cannot have myocardial ischemia
  2. Troponin testing is unnecessary in every such case
  3. Symptoms must be musculoskeletal
  4. Discharge is mandatory regardless of the clinical picture
  5. A nondiagnostic initial ECG does not exclude ACS
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

A nondiagnostic initial ECG does not exclude ACS

Further clinical evaluation, serial ECGs and cardiac troponin testing may be needed.

Understanding the options

A nondiagnostic initial ECG does not exclude ACS
Further clinical evaluation, serial ECGs and cardiac troponin testing may be needed.
The patient cannot have myocardial ischemia
A normal or nondiagnostic ECG cannot exclude ACS.
Troponin testing is unnecessary in every such case
Biomarkers contribute to evaluation when ACS is suspected.
Symptoms must be musculoskeletal
The ECG alone cannot establish an alternative diagnosis.
Discharge is mandatory regardless of the clinical picture
Disposition depends on the full risk assessment.
