Disproportionate concentration increase

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A patient with epilepsy has a small increase in the daily phenytoin dose. At follow-up, the concentration has risen disproportionately and he has nystagmus and ataxia. Adherence is confirmed. Which pharmacokinetic property best explains this result?

  1. Exclusively unchanged renal excretion
  2. Progressively faster clearance at high concentrations
  3. Absence of protein binding
  4. Saturable hepatic metabolism
  5. Constant fractional elimination at every concentration
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Saturable hepatic metabolism

Near metabolic saturation, a small dose increase can cause a large rise in phenytoin concentration.

Understanding the options

Saturable hepatic metabolism
Near metabolic saturation, a small dose increase can cause a large rise in phenytoin concentration.
Constant fractional elimination at every concentration
Phenytoin elimination becomes nonlinear as metabolism saturates.
Exclusively unchanged renal excretion
Phenytoin is mainly metabolized in the liver.
Progressively faster clearance at high concentrations
Saturation limits rather than accelerates clearance.
Absence of protein binding
Phenytoin is substantially protein bound.
