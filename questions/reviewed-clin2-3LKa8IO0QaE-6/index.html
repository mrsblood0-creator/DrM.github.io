Structural evaluation

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A 62-year-old with exertional dyspnea is found to have frequent PVCs. Examination suggests a displaced apical impulse. Before labeling the rhythm benign, the clinician wants to assess chamber size, valve function and ventricular systolic function. Which investigation is most appropriate?

  1. Transthoracic echocardiography
  2. An isolated D-dimer measurement
  3. Routine carotid massage
  4. A single fasting glucose measurement
  5. A chest CT solely to count PVCs
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Transthoracic echocardiography

Echocardiography evaluates structural disease and ventricular function.

Understanding the options

Transthoracic echocardiography
Echocardiography evaluates structural disease and ventricular function.
An isolated D-dimer measurement
D-dimer addresses selected thromboembolic questions.
Routine carotid massage
Carotid massage neither images the heart nor establishes structural safety.
A single fasting glucose measurement
Glucose does not assess chamber size or contractility.
A chest CT solely to count PVCs
CT is not a method of counting ectopic beats.
