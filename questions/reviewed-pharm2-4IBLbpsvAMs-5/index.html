Nonstimulant does not mean cardiovascular neutrality

A 35-year-old man chooses atomoxetine because he believes a nonstimulant cannot raise pulse or blood pressure. At follow-up both are elevated compared with baseline. Which mechanism makes this adverse effect pharmacologically plausible?

  1. Selective norepinephrine reuptake inhibition
  2. Selective serotonin reuptake inhibition
  3. Central alpha-2 agonism
  4. Dopamine D2 receptor antagonism
  5. Peripheral beta-1 receptor blockade
Answer and explanation

Selective norepinephrine reuptake inhibition

Increased noradrenergic signaling can affect cardiovascular measurements even though atomoxetine is not an amphetamine stimulant.

Option explanations

Selective norepinephrine reuptake inhibition: Increased noradrenergic signaling can affect cardiovascular measurements even though atomoxetine is not an amphetamine stimulant.

Selective serotonin reuptake inhibition: This is not atomoxetine's principal transporter action.

Central alpha-2 agonism: This describes drugs such as clonidine rather than atomoxetine.

Dopamine D2 receptor antagonism: This is not the principal action of atomoxetine.

Peripheral beta-1 receptor blockade: This would tend to lower pulse rather than explain the increase.
