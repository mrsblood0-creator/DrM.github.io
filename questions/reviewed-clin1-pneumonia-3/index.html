Imaging in uncomplicated outpatient pneumonia

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A healthy 6-year-old has fever and focal crackles consistent with uncomplicated community-acquired pneumonia. He has normal oxygen saturation, no significant work of breathing and can take oral fluids. Which investigation strategy is most appropriate?

  1. Clinical management without a routine chest radiograph
  2. Obtain chest CT to define the affected segment
  3. Obtain a chest radiograph before prescribing any antibiotic
  4. Obtain serial radiographs to establish progression
  5. Perform bronchoscopy to identify the pathogen
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Clinical management without a routine chest radiograph

A chest radiograph is not routinely required for a well child with a straightforward outpatient presentation.

Understanding the options

Clinical management without a routine chest radiograph
A chest radiograph is not routinely required for a well child with a straightforward outpatient presentation.
Obtain chest CT to define the affected segment
CT is disproportionate for uncomplicated clinically diagnosed pneumonia and adds radiation.
Obtain a chest radiograph before prescribing any antibiotic
Routine imaging is unnecessary in a stable child with an uncomplicated clinical presentation.
Obtain serial radiographs to establish progression
Serial imaging is not needed when disease is uncomplicated and the child can be followed clinically.
Perform bronchoscopy to identify the pathogen
Bronchoscopy is invasive and is not the routine diagnostic approach for uncomplicated outpatient pneumonia.
