Apnea without marked wheeze

A 4-week-old is brought to the emergency department after repeated pauses in breathing following several days of nasal congestion and reduced feeding. There is little wheeze on examination between episodes. Which interpretation is most appropriate?

  1. Bronchiolitis may present with apnea in a young infant
  2. Physiologic periodic breathing is the most likely explanation without further assessment
  3. Gastroesophageal reflux is established by the absence of wheeze
  4. Bronchiolitis requires audible wheeze at the time of assessment
  5. An upper respiratory infection cannot account for central pauses
Answer and explanation

Bronchiolitis may present with apnea in a young infant

Young infants may present with apnea before obvious lower-airway signs; the symptom warrants emergency assessment.

Option explanations

Bronchiolitis may present with apnea in a young infant: Young infants may present with apnea before obvious lower-airway signs; the symptom warrants emergency assessment.

Physiologic periodic breathing is the most likely explanation without further assessment: Recurrent apnea during an acute respiratory illness with reduced feeding requires urgent assessment rather than assuming normal periodic breathing.

Gastroesophageal reflux is established by the absence of wheeze: Absence of wheeze does not diagnose reflux or exclude bronchiolitis in a very young infant.

Bronchiolitis requires audible wheeze at the time of assessment: Young infants may present with apnea and few other respiratory findings.

An upper respiratory infection cannot account for central pauses: A viral illness may be associated with apnea in a young infant; this finding must be taken seriously.
