Avoiding false-negative testing

A patient completed H. pylori antibiotics one week ago and is still taking a PPI. He wants a stool antigen test tomorrow to prove eradication. Which preparation best reduces the risk of a falsely negative result?

  1. Use serum antibodies to confirm cure tomorrow
  2. Take extra bismuth before the sample
  3. Wait only until the first symptom-free day
  4. Wait at least four weeks after antibiotics and hold the PPI for two weeks when clinically appropriate
  5. Test immediately while taking every medication
Answer and explanation

Wait at least four weeks after antibiotics and hold the PPI for two weeks when clinically appropriate

Antibiotics and acid suppression can suppress detection and distort test-of-cure results.

Option explanations

Wait at least four weeks after antibiotics and hold the PPI for two weeks when clinically appropriate: Antibiotics and acid suppression can suppress detection and distort test-of-cure results.

Test immediately while taking every medication: This increases false-negative risk.

Use serum antibodies to confirm cure tomorrow: Serology cannot reliably distinguish past from persistent infection.

Take extra bismuth before the sample: Bismuth can also interfere with detection.

Wait only until the first symptom-free day: Symptoms do not determine adequate test timing.
