Cardiogenic pulmonary edema

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A 63-year-old with acute cardiogenic pulmonary edema has severe dyspnea, diffuse crackles and hypoxemia. She is alert and cooperative, with blood pressure 172/96 mm Hg and no vomiting. Which noninvasive support strategy can be appropriate alongside treatment of the underlying cause?

  1. Use bilevel NIV without monitoring because the mask is noninvasive
  2. CPAP or bilevel NIV with close reassessment
  3. Avoid all positive pressure because cardiac disease is present
  4. Use CPAP only after the patient becomes unresponsive
  5. Treat with oxygen alone and ignore persistent work of breathing
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

CPAP or bilevel NIV with close reassessment

Both approaches can support selected patients with cardiogenic pulmonary edema; choice depends on physiology, local expertise and response.

Understanding the options

CPAP or bilevel NIV with close reassessment
Both approaches can support selected patients with cardiogenic pulmonary edema; choice depends on physiology, local expertise and response.
Avoid all positive pressure because cardiac disease is present
Selected cardiogenic edema patients benefit from noninvasive positive pressure.
Use CPAP only after the patient becomes unresponsive
Loss of airway protection may instead require an invasive approach.
Treat with oxygen alone and ignore persistent work of breathing
Ongoing distress warrants assessment of additional respiratory support.
Use bilevel NIV without monitoring because the mask is noninvasive
Noninvasive support still requires assessment for failure and adverse effects.
