ORIGINAL USMLE-STYLE PRACTICE

An inherited protein-folding defect

A young adult with liver disease and early emphysema has hepatocytes containing retained misfolded alpha-1 antitrypsin within the endoplasmic reticulum. Which general mechanism best explains accumulation of this material inside liver cells despite its deficiency elsewhere?

  1. Primary deposition of extracellular calcium
  2. Overproduction of triglyceride as the only abnormality
  3. Failure to degrade glycogen in lysosomes
  4. Defective folding and transport of an endogenous protein
  5. Excess uptake of an exogenous indigestible pigment
Answer and explanation

Defective folding and transport of an endogenous protein

Misfolded alpha-1 antitrypsin can be retained in hepatocytes instead of being efficiently secreted; reduced circulating protein also affects the lung.

Option explanations

Defective folding and transport of an endogenous protein: Misfolded alpha-1 antitrypsin can be retained in hepatocytes instead of being efficiently secreted; reduced circulating protein also affects the lung.

Excess uptake of an exogenous indigestible pigment: The protein is synthesized endogenously, not inhaled.

Primary deposition of extracellular calcium: The finding is protein retention rather than calcification.

Overproduction of triglyceride as the only abnormality: Triglyceride accumulation does not explain alpha-1 antitrypsin inclusions.

Failure to degrade glycogen in lysosomes: A glycogen degradation defect would accumulate carbohydrate rather than this secretory protein.
