Assessing fracture geometry

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A 41-year-old man has a visible scaphoid waist fracture on radiographs. Before choosing treatment, the surgeon wants a clearer assessment of fragment displacement and the fracture configuration. Which additional study is particularly useful for this purpose?

  1. Bone scintigraphy
  2. Ultrasound of the flexor tendons
  3. MR angiography of the forearm arteries
  4. Repeat examination without additional imaging
  5. CT of the wrist
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

CT of the wrist

CT provides detailed bony assessment of fracture geometry and displacement.

Understanding the options

CT of the wrist
CT provides detailed bony assessment of fracture geometry and displacement.
Bone scintigraphy
It can detect increased bone turnover but does not define fragment geometry as precisely as CT.
Ultrasound of the flexor tendons
This is not the best study of scaphoid fracture displacement.
MR angiography of the forearm arteries
Large-vessel imaging does not answer the question of fracture geometry.
Repeat examination without additional imaging
Examination alone cannot quantify the fracture displacement.
