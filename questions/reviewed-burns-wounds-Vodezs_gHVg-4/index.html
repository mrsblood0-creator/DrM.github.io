Full-thickness burns: 4

A circumferential full-thickness limb burn becomes tight as swelling develops, and distal perfusion worsens. Which complication requires urgent specialist assessment rather than routine dressing changes while waiting for the swelling to resolve?

  1. Re-epithelialization from preserved viable epidermal sources
  2. Urgent assessment of constricting circumferential eschar
  3. Exclude purely superficial burns from resuscitation TBSA
  4. Include partial- and full-thickness areas in TBSA
  5. Cool the burn safely and assess severity
Answer and explanation

Urgent assessment of constricting circumferential eschar

Rigid circumferential burns can compromise perfusion or ventilation and may require specialist release.

Option explanations

Urgent assessment of constricting circumferential eschar: Rigid circumferential burns can compromise perfusion or ventilation and may require specialist release.

Exclude purely superficial burns from resuscitation TBSA: Burn-area estimates used for major burn assessment count partial- and full-thickness injury, not simple superficial erythema. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Include partial- and full-thickness areas in TBSA: Dermal and deeper burns contribute to the burn area used for severity and resuscitation assessment. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Cool the burn safely and assess severity: Appropriate cool running water first aid and protection are used; ice and additional cold injury should be avoided. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Re-epithelialization from preserved viable epidermal sources: Superficial injuries retain capacity for epithelial regeneration; deeper loss reduces this reserve. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
