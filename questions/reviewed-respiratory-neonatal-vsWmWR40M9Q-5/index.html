Oxygen therapy: 5

A patient rescued from an enclosed-space fire has headache and confusion despite an apparently normal standard pulse-oximeter reading. Carbon monoxide exposure remains suspected. Which test best measures the abnormal hemoglobin species responsible for this misleading reading?

  1. Pulse oximetry
  2. Peak expiratory flow
  3. Chest radiography
  4. Blood co-oximetry for carboxyhemoglobin
  5. Arterial blood gas analysis
Answer and explanation

Blood co-oximetry for carboxyhemoglobin

Standard pulse oximetry can appear reassuring in carbon monoxide poisoning; co-oximetry measures abnormal hemoglobin species.

Option explanations

Blood co-oximetry for carboxyhemoglobin: Standard pulse oximetry can appear reassuring in carbon monoxide poisoning; co-oximetry measures abnormal hemoglobin species.

Arterial blood gas analysis: Blood gases assess carbon dioxide and acid-base status as well as oxygen tension; saturation alone cannot assess ventilation. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Pulse oximetry: Pulse oximetry estimates peripheral oxygen saturation but does not directly measure carbon dioxide or reliably exclude dyshemoglobinemia. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Peak expiratory flow: Peak flow helps assess expiratory airflow limitation and change over time, not carbon monoxide binding. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Chest radiography: Radiographs show structural thoracic abnormalities but do not directly quantify blood gases or abnormal hemoglobin species. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
