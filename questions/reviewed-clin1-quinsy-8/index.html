Disposition after aspiration

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

An 18-year-old improves after peritonsillar abscess drainage. He has no stridor, tolerates oral fluids and medications, has stable vital signs, and has reliable follow-up. Which finding would instead most strongly favor hospital admission?

  1. Persistent inability to swallow fluids
  2. Mild residual odynophagia while drinking adequately
  3. Persistent tonsillar erythema with improving vital signs
  4. Tender cervical nodes without progressive swelling
  5. A throat culture still pending after empiric therapy
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Persistent inability to swallow fluids

Persistent inability to drink creates a hydration and medication-delivery problem requiring continued supervised care.

Understanding the options

Persistent inability to swallow fluids
Persistent inability to drink creates a hydration and medication-delivery problem requiring continued supervised care.
Mild residual odynophagia while drinking adequately
Some residual discomfort is expected and does not itself require admission if hydration and medication intake are adequate.
Persistent tonsillar erythema with improving vital signs
Local inflammation may resolve gradually; improving function and physiology support outpatient care.
Tender cervical nodes without progressive swelling
Reactive nodes can persist and are not by themselves a reason for admission.
A throat culture still pending after empiric therapy
A pending culture alone does not require admission when the patient is otherwise suitable for outpatient care.
