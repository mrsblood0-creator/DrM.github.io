Tachycardia with shock

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A 6-year-old has a regular narrow-complex tachycardia at 230/min. He is confused, hypotensive and has cool extremities with weak pulses. The resuscitation team identifies SVT with cardiopulmonary compromise. Which electrical therapy is indicated?

  1. Synchronized cardioversion
  2. Unsynchronized defibrillation
  3. Transcutaneous pacing
  4. Implanted-device antitachycardia pacing
  5. Elective electrophysiologic ablation
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Synchronized cardioversion

Synchronizing the shock to ventricular depolarization treats unstable SVT while reducing the risk of shocking during repolarization.

Understanding the options

Synchronized cardioversion
Synchronizing the shock to ventricular depolarization treats unstable SVT while reducing the risk of shocking during repolarization.
Unsynchronized defibrillation
With a pulse and an organized SVT, synchronization reduces the risk of delivering energy during ventricular repolarization.
Transcutaneous pacing
Pacing is designed to support an inadequately slow rhythm, not terminate this rapid reentrant rhythm.
Implanted-device antitachycardia pacing
No implanted device is present; this is not the emergency electrical strategy for this child.
Elective electrophysiologic ablation
Ablation may address recurrent SVT later but cannot replace immediate stabilization of shock.
