Abrupt regular tachycardia in infancy

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A 3-month-old abruptly becomes irritable and feeds poorly. The monitor shows a fixed rate of 250/min with narrow QRS complexes and no clearly visible P waves. The rate does not vary with sleep or crying. Which rhythm best explains these findings?

  1. Sinus tachycardia
  2. Atrial fibrillation
  3. Complete atrioventricular block
  4. Ventricular fibrillation
  5. Supraventricular tachycardia
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Supraventricular tachycardia

Abrupt onset, very regular rapid rhythm and absent discernible sinus P waves favor SVT.

Understanding the options

Supraventricular tachycardia
Abrupt onset, very regular rapid rhythm and absent discernible sinus P waves favor SVT.
Sinus tachycardia
Sinus tachycardia generally varies and has identifiable sinus P waves.
Atrial fibrillation
Atrial fibrillation usually produces an irregular ventricular response.
Complete atrioventricular block
Complete block commonly produces a slow escape rhythm and atrioventricular dissociation.
Ventricular fibrillation
Ventricular fibrillation has chaotic activity without organized QRS complexes.
