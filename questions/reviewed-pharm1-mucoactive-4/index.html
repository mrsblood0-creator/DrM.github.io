ORIGINAL USMLE-STYLE PRACTICE

A second use of acetylcysteine

A 25-year-old woman presents after a potentially hepatotoxic acetaminophen ingestion. Acetylcysteine is started following toxicology assessment. A student knows the drug as a mucolytic and asks how it protects hepatocytes. Which process is most relevant to its antidotal effect?

  1. Inhibition of ribosomal protein synthesis in injured hepatocytes
  2. Restoration of glutathione-dependent detoxification of reactive metabolites
  3. Inhibition of acetaminophen absorption by binding it in the gut
  4. Blockade of hepatic vitamin K epoxide reductase
  5. Competitive antagonism of hepatic opioid receptors
Answer and explanation

Restoration of glutathione-dependent detoxification of reactive metabolites

Acetylcysteine supports glutathione stores and detoxification of the reactive acetaminophen metabolite NAPQI.

Option explanations

Restoration of glutathione-dependent detoxification of reactive metabolites: Restoring glutathione helps limit reactive-metabolite injury.

Inhibition of acetaminophen absorption by binding it in the gut: Gastrointestinal adsorption describes activated charcoal, not the principal antidotal action here.

Blockade of hepatic vitamin K epoxide reductase: Vitamin K epoxide reductase inhibition would impair clotting-factor activation.

Competitive antagonism of hepatic opioid receptors: Acetaminophen toxicity is not mediated by opioid receptors.

Inhibition of ribosomal protein synthesis in injured hepatocytes: Inhibiting hepatocyte protein synthesis would not explain protection.
