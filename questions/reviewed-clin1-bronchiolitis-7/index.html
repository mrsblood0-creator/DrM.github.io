Apparent improvement that signals exhaustion

An infant hospitalized for bronchiolitis initially has severe retractions and tachypnea. Later, respiratory effort diminishes, but he becomes listless and has recurrent apnea while oxygen requirements rise. What does this change most likely represent?

  1. Impending respiratory failure due to exhaustion
  2. Resolution of bronchiolar obstruction
  3. A lower oxygen requirement after mucus clearance
  4. Compensated metabolic acidosis
  5. Transient breath-holding during agitation
Answer and explanation

Impending respiratory failure due to exhaustion

Reduced effort with worsening consciousness, apnea and increasing oxygen needs indicates deterioration, not recovery.

Option explanations

Impending respiratory failure due to exhaustion: Reduced effort with worsening consciousness, apnea and increasing oxygen needs indicates deterioration, not recovery.

Resolution of bronchiolar obstruction: Improvement should be accompanied by better alertness and gas exchange, not apnea and increasing oxygen need.

A lower oxygen requirement after mucus clearance: The oxygen requirement is rising, contradicting successful clearance with recovery.

Compensated metabolic acidosis: Compensatory ventilation usually increases respiratory effort; recurrent apnea and listlessness instead indicate decompensation.

Transient breath-holding during agitation: The child is listless with worsening respiratory support needs, rather than having brief crying-related episodes.
