Selecting among H2 blockers

A 63-year-old patient takes several medicines whose plasma concentrations depend on hepatic metabolism. An H2 antagonist is being selected for acid-related symptoms. The clinician chooses famotidine rather than cimetidine to limit additional interactions. Which pharmacologic distinction best supports that choice?

  1. Famotidine has no possible adverse effects
  2. Cimetidine is not absorbed orally
  3. Famotidine irreversibly blocks the proton pump
  4. Cimetidine is a broad-spectrum antibiotic
  5. Cimetidine has more prominent inhibition of hepatic drug-metabolizing enzymes
Answer and explanation

Cimetidine has more prominent inhibition of hepatic drug-metabolizing enzymes

Cimetidine has a substantial metabolic interaction profile; choosing famotidine may reduce that specific risk, while other interactions still need review.

Option explanations

Cimetidine has more prominent inhibition of hepatic drug-metabolizing enzymes: Cimetidine has a substantial metabolic interaction profile; choosing famotidine may reduce that specific risk, while other interactions still need review.

Famotidine has no possible adverse effects: Famotidine still has adverse effects and interactions.

Cimetidine is not absorbed orally: Cimetidine is orally absorbed.

Famotidine irreversibly blocks the proton pump: Famotidine blocks H2 receptors, not the pump irreversibly.

Cimetidine is a broad-spectrum antibiotic: Cimetidine is an acid suppressant, not an antibiotic.
