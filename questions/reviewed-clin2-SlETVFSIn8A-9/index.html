Reversible vagal episode

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

During a painful venipuncture, a 27-year-old becomes nauseated and pale, then briefly loses consciousness. Monitoring shows transient sinus slowing that resolves after he is placed supine. Examination and ECG subsequently normalize. Which mechanism most likely contributed?

  1. Fixed complete AV block
  2. Catecholamine-driven sinus acceleration
  3. Transient increase in vagal activity
  4. Sustained atrial reentry
  5. Ventricular pre-excitation
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Transient increase in vagal activity

A vasovagal episode can combine sinus slowing and peripheral vasodilation.

Understanding the options

Transient increase in vagal activity
A vasovagal episode can combine sinus slowing and peripheral vasodilation.
Sustained atrial reentry
Atrial reentry would produce a tachyarrhythmia.
Ventricular pre-excitation
Pre-excitation does not explain this typical vagal trigger and transient slowing.
Fixed complete AV block
The rapid return to normal conduction argues against fixed complete block.
Catecholamine-driven sinus acceleration
Sympathetic acceleration is opposite to the observed response.
