Acute compartment syndrome: 4

A patient has established acute compartment syndrome with worsening neurologic findings. External constriction is addressed while emergency specialist treatment is arranged. Which definitive treatment principle addresses the pathological pressure inside the affected fascial compartments?

  1. Compartment-pressure assessment when needed
  2. Ischemic muscle contracture
  3. Urgent surgical decompression
  4. Acute compartment syndrome
  5. Preserved pulses do not exclude compartment syndrome
Answer and explanation

Urgent surgical decompression

Established acute compartment syndrome requires urgent specialist treatment, usually fasciotomy.

Option explanations

Urgent surgical decompression: Established acute compartment syndrome requires urgent specialist treatment, usually fasciotomy.

Acute compartment syndrome: Escalating pain, pain on passive stretch and a tense compartment after injury are concerning for pressure-related ischemia. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Preserved pulses do not exclude compartment syndrome: Major arterial flow can remain present while microvascular tissue perfusion is compromised. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Compartment-pressure assessment when needed: Pressure measurement can support diagnosis when the examination is unreliable; it must not delay obvious emergency treatment. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Ischemic muscle contracture: Untreated ischemia can cause muscle necrosis, fibrosis and permanent contracture. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
