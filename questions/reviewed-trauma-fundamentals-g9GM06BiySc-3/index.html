Orthopedic trauma foundations: 3

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

An injured limb is about to be manipulated and splinted. The clinician records distal motor, sensory and perfusion findings and plans to repeat them afterward. Which foundational assessment practice is being applied?

  1. Document a neurovascular examination
  2. Clarify mechanism and energy of injury
  3. Inspect the skin for an open injury
  4. Repeat examinations when symptoms evolve
  5. Complete the broader trauma assessment
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Document a neurovascular examination

Motor, sensory and perfusion findings should be recorded before and after interventions.

Understanding the options

Document a neurovascular examination

Clarify mechanism and energy of injury
Mechanism helps predict associated injury, instability and the need for broader assessment. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
Inspect the skin for an open injury
A small communicating wound can make a fracture open even without visible exposed bone. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
Repeat examinations when symptoms evolve
Swelling, pain and neurologic findings can change; a normal initial examination does not end surveillance. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
Complete the broader trauma assessment
A dramatic limb injury must not distract from other life-threatening injuries. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
