Choosing an oral adjunct

A deeply unconscious adult has snoring respirations from loss of upper-airway tone. He does not gag during suctioning. Manual airway opening helps but must be maintained. Which adjunct is appropriate to help keep the tongue from obstructing the pharynx?

  1. A tight circumferential neck bandage
  2. An oral fluid challenge
  3. Oropharyngeal airway
  4. An oral airway despite an intact gag reflex in all patients
  5. A surgical airway as the first response to any snoring
Answer and explanation

Oropharyngeal airway

An oral airway is suitable in a sufficiently unresponsive patient without a gag reflex.

Option explanations

Oropharyngeal airway: An oral airway is suitable in a sufficiently unresponsive patient without a gag reflex.

An oral airway despite an intact gag reflex in all patients: A preserved gag reflex makes oral-airway insertion poorly tolerated and can provoke vomiting.

A surgical airway as the first response to any snoring: Basic maneuvers and adjuncts are appropriate first when they restore patency.

A tight circumferential neck bandage: This does not open the pharynx and can be harmful.

An oral fluid challenge: Giving fluid to a deeply unconscious patient risks aspiration.
