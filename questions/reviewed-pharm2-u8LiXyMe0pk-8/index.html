A single-bolus fibrinolytic

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

An emergency team selects tenecteplase for an eligible patient under its protocol. Unlike an alteplase regimen requiring an infusion, this agent can be administered as a single intravenous bolus. Which feature supports that practical difference?

  1. Longer persistence of active drug in plasma
  2. Complete absence of bleeding risk
  3. Oral absorption exceeding that of alteplase
  4. Irreversible inhibition of platelet cyclooxygenase
  5. Requirement for activation by intestinal bacteria
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Longer persistence of active drug in plasma

Tenecteplase has pharmacokinetic properties permitting single-bolus administration.

Understanding the options

Longer persistence of active drug in plasma
Tenecteplase has pharmacokinetic properties permitting single-bolus administration.
Complete absence of bleeding risk
Both agents can cause major bleeding.
Oral absorption exceeding that of alteplase
Tenecteplase is administered intravenously, not orally.
Irreversible inhibition of platelet cyclooxygenase
Its action is fibrinolytic rather than aspirin-like.
Requirement for activation by intestinal bacteria
No intestinal microbial activation is required.
