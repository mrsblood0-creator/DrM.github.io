Compressions and breaths

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

Two trained rescuers treat a pulseless adult after a witnessed collapse. A defibrillator is being prepared, and a bag-mask device is available for ventilation. No advanced airway has been inserted. Which compression-to-ventilation pattern is appropriate during conventional CPR?

  1. Continuous rapid breaths without compressions
  2. Five compressions followed by five breaths
  3. One breath after every compression
  4. Thirty compressions followed by two breaths
  5. Fifteen compressions followed by two breaths
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Thirty compressions followed by two breaths

Before an advanced airway, coordinated 30:2 CPR is the standard adult approach.

Understanding the options

Thirty compressions followed by two breaths
Before an advanced airway, coordinated 30:2 CPR is the standard adult approach.
Fifteen compressions followed by two breaths
This is not the standard adult ratio in this setting.
Continuous rapid breaths without compressions
Ventilation alone does not provide adequate circulation during cardiac arrest.
Five compressions followed by five breaths
Frequent long pauses compromise compression delivery.
One breath after every compression
This produces excessive interruptions and ventilation.
