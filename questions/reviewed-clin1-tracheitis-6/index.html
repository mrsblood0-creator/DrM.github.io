Tracheitis versus epiglottitis

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

During controlled endoscopy in a febrile child with stridor, the epiglottis appears relatively normal, while the subglottic trachea contains inflamed mucosa and abundant pus. Which diagnosis is most consistent with this anatomic distribution?

  1. Bacterial tracheitis
  2. Primary epiglottitis
  3. Peritonsillar abscess
  4. Retropharyngeal abscess
  5. Isolated allergic laryngeal edema
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Bacterial tracheitis

The principal disease is below the glottis in the trachea, with purulent inflammation.

Understanding the options

Bacterial tracheitis
The principal disease is below the glottis in the trachea, with purulent inflammation.
Primary epiglottitis
Epiglottitis principally affects supraglottic tissues including the epiglottis.
Peritonsillar abscess
A peritonsillar abscess lies beside the tonsil.
Retropharyngeal abscess
A retropharyngeal abscess lies behind the pharynx rather than inside the tracheal lumen.
Isolated allergic laryngeal edema
Allergic edema does not usually produce copious purulent secretions.
