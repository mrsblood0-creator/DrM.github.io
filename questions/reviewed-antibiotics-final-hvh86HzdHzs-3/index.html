Cell-wall inhibitor principles: 3

A bacterial isolate resists a beta-lactam because it produces a protein that chemically destroys the antibiotic before effective target binding. The drug target itself remains unchanged. Which broad mechanism explains the resistance described?

  1. Modification of the drug target
  2. Increased drug efflux
  3. Reduced outer-membrane permeability
  4. Biofilm-associated persistence
  5. Enzymatic drug inactivation
Answer and explanation

Enzymatic drug inactivation

Bacteria can produce enzymes that destroy or chemically modify an antibiotic.

Option explanations

Enzymatic drug inactivation: Bacteria can produce enzymes that destroy or chemically modify an antibiotic.

Modification of the drug target: A changed target can bind the antibiotic less effectively while maintaining its microbial function. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Increased drug efflux: Transport pumps can lower intracellular antibiotic exposure. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Reduced outer-membrane permeability: Loss or alteration of porins can reduce entry of antibiotics into Gram-negative bacteria. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Biofilm-associated persistence: Organisms in biofilms can be difficult to eradicate; device management may be needed despite planktonic susceptibility. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
