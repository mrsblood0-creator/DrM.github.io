Protecting the ulcer from peptic injury

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

Investigators expose an ulcer surface to gastric contents before and after treatment with sucralfate. The treated surface is less vulnerable despite continued acid secretion. Which finding best supports a local protective mechanism?

  1. Complete inhibition of all parietal cells
  2. Permanent elimination of vagal tone
  3. Selective destruction of gastrin-producing cells
  4. Suppression of every pancreatic digestive enzyme
  5. Reduced diffusion of acid and pepsin to the injured surface
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Reduced diffusion of acid and pepsin to the injured surface

An adherent barrier limits contact between ulcer tissue and injurious luminal contents, including pepsin.

Understanding the options

Reduced diffusion of acid and pepsin to the injured surface
An adherent barrier limits contact between ulcer tissue and injurious luminal contents, including pepsin.
Complete inhibition of all parietal cells
Sucralfate does not abolish parietal secretion.
Permanent elimination of vagal tone
Vagal ablation is not its mechanism.
Selective destruction of gastrin-producing cells
G cells are not destroyed.
Suppression of every pancreatic digestive enzyme
Pancreatic enzyme suppression is not required.
