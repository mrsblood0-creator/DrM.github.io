Screening-test interpretation

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A 35-year-old woman taking bupropion has a positive urine amphetamine immunoassay during an employment assessment. She denies stimulant use and has no signs of intoxication. The result could affect her employment. What is the most appropriate laboratory next step?

  1. Repeat only the same immunoassay immediately
  2. Measure a serum ethanol level instead
  3. Assume that bupropion always causes a positive result
  4. Obtain confirmatory testing with a specific analytical method
  5. Diagnose stimulant use disorder from the screening result
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Obtain confirmatory testing with a specific analytical method

Bupropion can cross-react with screening immunoassays; a screening result alone is not proof of amphetamine exposure.

Understanding the options

Obtain confirmatory testing with a specific analytical method
Bupropion can cross-react with screening immunoassays; a screening result alone is not proof of amphetamine exposure.
Diagnose stimulant use disorder from the screening result
A screening assay cannot establish either exposure conclusively or a behavioral diagnosis.
Repeat only the same immunoassay immediately
The same cross-reactivity can persist.
Measure a serum ethanol level instead
Ethanol testing does not identify the substance causing this result.
Assume that bupropion always causes a positive result
Cross-reactivity is possible, not inevitable; confirmation is still required.
