Interpreting synovial leukocytes

An immunosuppressed patient has a painful swollen native joint. Synovial leukocytes are below 25,000 cells/µL, but the clinician remains concerned for infection. Which interpretation best reflects the limitations of the cell count?

  1. Only joint color matters diagnostically
  2. A low count makes culture technically impossible
  3. A lower count reduces probability but cannot exclude infection
  4. Any count below 50,000 definitively excludes infection
  5. Immunosuppression guarantees a very high leukocyte count
Answer and explanation

A lower count reduces probability but cannot exclude infection

Cell-count thresholds are not absolute, particularly in immunosuppressed patients.

Option explanations

A lower count reduces probability but cannot exclude infection: Cell-count thresholds are not absolute, particularly in immunosuppressed patients.

Any count below 50,000 definitively excludes infection: No single threshold reliably excludes septic arthritis.

Immunosuppression guarantees a very high leukocyte count: Immunosuppression can blunt inflammatory responses.

Only joint color matters diagnostically: Appearance alone cannot establish the diagnosis.

A low count makes culture technically impossible: Culture can still be performed.
