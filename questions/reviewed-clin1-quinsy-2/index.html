Anatomic location of quinsy

An adolescent undergoes drainage after examination reveals a unilateral soft-palate bulge, marked odynophagia, and contralateral uvular deviation. Pus is aspirated from the swollen peritonsillar region. In which anatomic location did this collection most likely form?

  1. Between the tonsillar capsule and superior pharyngeal constrictor
  2. Between the posterior pharyngeal wall and prevertebral tissues
  3. Within the submandibular and sublingual spaces
  4. Within lymph nodes along the posterior cervical chain
  5. Within the parapharyngeal space lateral to the constrictor muscle
Answer and explanation

Between the tonsillar capsule and superior pharyngeal constrictor

The peritonsillar space lies external to the palatine tonsillar capsule and internal to the pharyngeal musculature.

Option explanations

Between the tonsillar capsule and superior pharyngeal constrictor: The peritonsillar space lies external to the palatine tonsillar capsule and internal to the pharyngeal musculature.

Between the posterior pharyngeal wall and prevertebral tissues: This is the retropharyngeal region, which produces posterior pharyngeal findings rather than a peritonsillar collection.

Within the submandibular and sublingual spaces: Infection here causes floor-of-mouth swelling, as in Ludwig angina, rather than a peritonsillar bulge.

Within lymph nodes along the posterior cervical chain: Cervical lymphadenitis does not locate pus immediately outside the tonsillar capsule.

Within the parapharyngeal space lateral to the constrictor muscle: Parapharyngeal spread is a possible complication, but the described aspirated collection is peritonsillar.
