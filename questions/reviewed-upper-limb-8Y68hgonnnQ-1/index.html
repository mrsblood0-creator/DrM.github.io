Anterior shoulder dislocation: 1

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A young athlete experiences shoulder pain after forced abduction and external rotation. Radiographs show the humeral head lying anterior to the glenoid rather than behind or directly below it. Which type of dislocation has occurred?

  1. Posterior shoulder dislocation
  2. Inferior shoulder dislocation
  3. Acromioclavicular separation
  4. Posterior sternoclavicular dislocation
  5. Anterior shoulder dislocation
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Anterior shoulder dislocation

Anterior glenohumeral displacement often follows abduction and external rotation and places the head anterior to the glenoid.

Understanding the options

Anterior shoulder dislocation
Anterior glenohumeral displacement often follows abduction and external rotation and places the head anterior to the glenoid.
Posterior shoulder dislocation
Posterior dislocation may follow seizures or electrical injury and can present with fixed internal rotation. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
Inferior shoulder dislocation
Luxatio erecta places the humeral head below the glenoid, often with the arm fixed overhead. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
Acromioclavicular separation
AC injury disrupts the clavicle-acromion relationship while the humeral head may remain normally located. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
Posterior sternoclavicular dislocation
Posterior medial clavicular displacement threatens mediastinal structures and warrants urgent specialist care. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
