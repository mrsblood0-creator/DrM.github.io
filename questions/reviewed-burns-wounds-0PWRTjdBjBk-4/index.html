Hypertrophic and keloid scars: 4

A patient asks whether simply cutting out a keloid guarantees permanent resolution. Which management concern explains why specialist treatment often combines approaches rather than relying on excision alone for this scar type?

  1. Keloid
  2. Hypertrophic scar
  3. Potential spontaneous flattening of hypertrophic scars
  4. Scar contracture limiting movement
  5. High recurrence risk after excision alone
Answer and explanation

High recurrence risk after excision alone

Keloid management often requires a combined strategy because simple excision can recur.

Option explanations

High recurrence risk after excision alone: Keloid management often requires a combined strategy because simple excision can recur.

Keloid: A keloid extends beyond the original wound boundaries and can recur after treatment. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Hypertrophic scar: A hypertrophic scar remains within the original wound boundaries and may regress over time. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Potential spontaneous flattening of hypertrophic scars: Hypertrophic scars may improve with maturation, unlike the typical persistent extension of keloids. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Scar contracture limiting movement: Scar shortening across a joint can restrict function and warrants assessment and rehabilitation. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
