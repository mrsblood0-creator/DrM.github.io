Sugar content in a formulation

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A patient with diabetes is prescribed lactulose solution. The pharmacist reviews the manufacturer’s listed residual galactose and lactose content. What is the best reason to review the particular formulation rather than assume it is metabolically inert?

  1. Diabetes makes colonic bacteria unable to metabolize lactulose
  2. Any diabetes diagnosis is an absolute contraindication to every lactulose product
  3. The preparation may contain absorbable accompanying sugars
  4. Lactulose is converted entirely into intravenous glucose
  5. All sugar-free products necessarily contain identical excipients
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

The preparation may contain absorbable accompanying sugars

Poor lactulose absorption does not imply that every component of the solution is unabsorbable.

Understanding the options

The preparation may contain absorbable accompanying sugars
Poor lactulose absorption does not imply that every component of the solution is unabsorbable.
Lactulose is converted entirely into intravenous glucose
There is no intravenous conversion pathway.
All sugar-free products necessarily contain identical excipients
Formulations differ and should be checked individually.
Diabetes makes colonic bacteria unable to metabolize lactulose
Diabetes does not uniformly abolish bacterial fermentation.
Any diabetes diagnosis is an absolute contraindication to every lactulose product
The issue is formulation-specific caution and monitoring rather than a universal prohibition.
