Stress fractures: 5

An athlete’s stress fracture has healed clinically, and the athlete wants to resume the same abrupt training increase that preceded it. Which return-to-activity principle best addresses recurrence risk while rebuilding physical capacity?

  1. Fatigue stress injury
  2. Insufficiency stress injury
  3. MRI for persistent suspicion despite negative early radiographs
  4. Protect the injured site and obtain appropriate assessment
  5. Gradual return after clinical healing and risk-factor review
Answer and explanation

Gradual return after clinical healing and risk-factor review

Training load, nutrition and biomechanical factors should be addressed before a graded return.

Option explanations

Gradual return after clinical healing and risk-factor review: Training load, nutrition and biomechanical factors should be addressed before a graded return.

Fatigue stress injury: A rapid rise in repetitive load can exceed repair capacity in otherwise normal bone. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Insufficiency stress injury: Physiological load can exceed the strength of osteoporotic or otherwise weakened bone. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

MRI for persistent suspicion despite negative early radiographs: Early stress injuries may be radiographically occult; MRI can detect marrow and fracture changes. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Protect the injured site and obtain appropriate assessment: Continuing painful loading may progress a stress injury, especially at high-risk sites. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
