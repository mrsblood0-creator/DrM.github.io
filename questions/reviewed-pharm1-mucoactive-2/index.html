Wheezing during mucolytic nebulization

A patient with asthma and thick secretions develops new chest tightness and wheezing during nebulized acetylcysteine. Airflow continues to worsen despite an initial inhaled bronchodilator. Oxygenation is being monitored. Which immediate medication action is most appropriate?

  1. Continue unchanged because wheezing proves mucus clearance
  2. Add a cough suppressant while continuing the nebulization
  3. Replace the bronchodilator with another acetylcysteine dose
  4. Stop acetylcysteine and treat the bronchospasm
  5. Increase acetylcysteine concentration to complete mucolysis
Answer and explanation

Stop acetylcysteine and treat the bronchospasm

Inhaled acetylcysteine can cause unpredictable bronchospasm; progressive bronchospasm warrants discontinuation.

Option explanations

Stop acetylcysteine and treat the bronchospasm: The temporal reaction and progression require stopping the provoking drug and managing the airway.

Increase acetylcysteine concentration to complete mucolysis: Increasing exposure may worsen the reaction.

Continue unchanged because wheezing proves mucus clearance: Wheezing with deteriorating airflow is not evidence of successful clearance.

Add a cough suppressant while continuing the nebulization: Suppressing cough does not treat bronchospasm and may hinder clearance.

Replace the bronchodilator with another acetylcysteine dose: Acetylcysteine is not a rescue bronchodilator.
