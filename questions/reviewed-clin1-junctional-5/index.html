A protective escape focus

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

An older patient develops sinus pauses, followed by a junctional escape rhythm at 45/min. Blood pressure is maintained. A trainee proposes suppressing the junctional focus solely to restore a normal-looking tracing. Why is that approach potentially harmful?

  1. The escape focus may be maintaining ventricular perfusion when the sinus node fails
  2. A junctional focus always causes ventricular fibrillation
  3. Suppressing any rhythm automatically restores sinus-node function
  4. The escape rhythm proves the patient has no pulse
  5. A narrow QRS guarantees the sinus node is functioning normally
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

The escape focus may be maintaining ventricular perfusion when the sinus node fails

An escape rhythm is a backup pacemaker; suppressing it without correcting the primary problem can worsen bradycardia or cause asystole.

Understanding the options

The escape focus may be maintaining ventricular perfusion when the sinus node fails
An escape rhythm is a backup pacemaker; suppressing it without correcting the primary problem can worsen bradycardia or cause asystole.
A junctional focus always causes ventricular fibrillation
Junctional escape is not intrinsically equivalent to ventricular fibrillation.
Suppressing any rhythm automatically restores sinus-node function
Suppression does not repair sinus-node dysfunction.
The escape rhythm proves the patient has no pulse
An organized escape rhythm may produce a pulse and adequate pressure.
A narrow QRS guarantees the sinus node is functioning normally
QRS width reflects ventricular activation, not normal sinus-node function.
