SAAG interpretation: 3

A patient’s serum-ascites albumin gradient is 1.7 g/dL. The clinician interprets it with the history and other fluid measurements. Which broad hemodynamic mechanism is supported by a gradient above the usual 1.1 g/dL threshold?

  1. Non-portal-hypertensive mechanism
  2. 2.0 g/dL
  3. 0.6 g/dL
  4. Cardiac ascites
  5. Portal-hypertensive mechanism
Answer and explanation

Portal-hypertensive mechanism

A serum-minus-ascitic albumin gradient of at least 1.1 g/dL supports portal hypertension in the appropriate context.

Option explanations

Portal-hypertensive mechanism: A serum-minus-ascitic albumin gradient of at least 1.1 g/dL supports portal hypertension in the appropriate context.

Non-portal-hypertensive mechanism: A gradient below 1.1 g/dL suggests causes such as peritoneal malignancy or inflammation rather than typical portal hypertension. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

2.0 g/dL: Subtract ascitic albumin from serum albumin: 3.0 minus 1.0 equals 2.0 g/dL. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

0.6 g/dL: Subtract ascitic albumin from serum albumin: 2.8 minus 2.2 equals 0.6 g/dL. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Cardiac ascites: A high gradient with relatively high ascitic protein and systemic venous congestion can suggest a cardiac cause. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
