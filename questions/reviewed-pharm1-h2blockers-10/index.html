Reversible receptor blockade versus pump inhibition

In a gastric-cell experiment, histamine-driven acid secretion returns after famotidine is removed from the preparation. This differs from prolonged inhibition after an activated PPI. Which distinction best accounts for the different recovery patterns?

  1. Famotidine forms a lifelong mucosal coating
  2. Famotidine acts through reversible receptor antagonism
  3. Famotidine permanently destroys parietal cells
  4. Famotidine irreversibly alkylates the proton pump
  5. Famotidine depletes all gastric stem cells
Answer and explanation

Famotidine acts through reversible receptor antagonism

H2 blockade is reversible; PPIs have a longer-lasting pump-directed inhibitory mechanism.

Option explanations

Famotidine acts through reversible receptor antagonism: H2 blockade is reversible; PPIs have a longer-lasting pump-directed inhibitory mechanism.

Famotidine permanently destroys parietal cells: Cell destruction is not required for H2 blockade.

Famotidine irreversibly alkylates the proton pump: Irreversible pump inhibition characterizes PPIs.

Famotidine depletes all gastric stem cells: Stem-cell depletion is not involved.

Famotidine forms a lifelong mucosal coating: Famotidine does not form a mucosal coating.
