Systemic antibiotic treatment

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

After the airway is secured in a child with epiglottitis, blood cultures are obtained without delaying therapy. Which initial antibiotic approach is most appropriate while the organism and susceptibilities remain unknown?

  1. Parenteral antibiotics covering likely invasive respiratory bacteria
  2. Oral penicillin alone after discharge
  3. Wait for a positive blood culture before starting antibiotics
  4. Topical antibiotic application to the supraglottis
  5. Corticosteroid treatment without systemic antibiotics
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Parenteral antibiotics covering likely invasive respiratory bacteria

Airway support does not treat the invasive infection; empiric IV antibiotics are required and later tailored.

Understanding the options

Parenteral antibiotics covering likely invasive respiratory bacteria
Airway support does not treat the invasive infection; empiric IV antibiotics are required and later tailored.
Oral penicillin alone after discharge
This child has required airway stabilization and needs inpatient parenteral management, not immediate oral outpatient treatment.
Wait for a positive blood culture before starting antibiotics
Cultures should not delay empiric treatment of a potentially invasive bacterial infection.
Topical antibiotic application to the supraglottis
Local application is not adequate treatment for this invasive infection.
Corticosteroid treatment without systemic antibiotics
Steroids do not replace antibacterial therapy in bacterial epiglottitis.
