Neonatal cyanosis: 2

A comfortable newborn has bluish hands and feet during early transition, but the tongue is pink and central oxygenation is reassuring on assessment. Which descriptive finding best distinguishes this peripheral pattern from blue oral mucosa?

  1. Compare right-hand and foot oxygen saturations
  2. Further clinical assessment and echocardiography when indicated
  3. Peripheral acrocyanosis
  4. Central cyanosis
  5. Duct-dependent congenital heart disease
Answer and explanation

Peripheral acrocyanosis

Bluish hands and feet with a pink tongue can reflect peripheral circulation rather than central hypoxemia; the overall clinical state still matters.

Option explanations

Peripheral acrocyanosis: Bluish hands and feet with a pink tongue can reflect peripheral circulation rather than central hypoxemia; the overall clinical state still matters.

Central cyanosis: Blue discoloration of the tongue or oral mucosa suggests systemic arterial desaturation or an abnormal hemoglobin state and needs urgent evaluation. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Duct-dependent congenital heart disease: Deterioration as the ductus closes can signal critical congenital heart disease requiring urgent specialist assessment and ductal support when indicated. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Compare right-hand and foot oxygen saturations: Right-hand measurement is preductal and a foot measurement is postductal; a difference can help assess ductal shunting but is not diagnostic by itself. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Further clinical assessment and echocardiography when indicated: A passed newborn oxygen screen does not exclude every important heart defect; new symptoms require assessment despite a previous pass. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
