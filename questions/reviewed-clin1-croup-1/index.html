Barking cough at night

A 2-year-old with two days of rhinorrhea awakens with a harsh barking cough and hoarse voice. Inspiratory stridor occurs when crying, but he is playful when calm and has no drooling. Which diagnosis is most likely?

  1. Viral croup
  2. Epiglottitis
  3. Retropharyngeal abscess
  4. Bronchiolitis
  5. Peritonsillar abscess
Answer and explanation

Viral croup

The age, viral prodrome, barking cough and hoarseness are typical of croup.

Option explanations

Viral croup: The age, viral prodrome, barking cough and hoarseness are typical of croup.

Epiglottitis: Epiglottitis more often causes marked dysphagia, drooling and toxicity without a prominent bark.

Retropharyngeal abscess: A deep-neck abscess usually has fever, painful swallowing and neck restriction.

Bronchiolitis: Bronchiolitis predominantly causes lower-airway crackles and wheeze in infants.

Peritonsillar abscess: Quinsy produces focal peritonsillar findings rather than this classic bark.
