Cimetidine and anticoagulation

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A 66-year-old man with stable warfarin therapy starts cimetidine for dyspepsia. At follow-up, his INR is elevated despite unchanged warfarin dosing, diet and adherence. There is no evidence of a new liver disorder. Which interaction best explains the increased anticoagulant effect?

  1. Induction of warfarin-metabolizing enzymes
  2. Reduced hepatic metabolism of warfarin
  3. Increased synthesis of clotting factors
  4. Reduced intestinal warfarin absorption
  5. Direct irreversible platelet COX inhibition
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Reduced hepatic metabolism of warfarin

Cimetidine can inhibit hepatic drug metabolism and increase warfarin exposure; anticoagulation monitoring is needed.

Understanding the options

Reduced hepatic metabolism of warfarin
Cimetidine can inhibit hepatic drug metabolism and increase warfarin exposure; anticoagulation monitoring is needed.
Increased synthesis of clotting factors
More clotting factors would oppose anticoagulation.
Reduced intestinal warfarin absorption
Reduced absorption would tend to lower the effect.
Direct irreversible platelet COX inhibition
This is aspirin's mechanism, not the main cimetidine interaction.
Induction of warfarin-metabolizing enzymes
Induction would generally lower warfarin exposure.
