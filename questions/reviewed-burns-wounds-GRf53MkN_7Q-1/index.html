Superficial burns: 1

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

After brief sun exposure, a patient has red tender skin without blisters or loss of sensation. The injury is confined to the outer skin layer. Which burn-depth category best describes this presentation?

  1. Superficial partial-thickness burn
  2. Deep partial-thickness burn
  3. Full-thickness burn
  4. Burn extending into deeper structures
  5. Superficial epidermal burn
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Superficial epidermal burn

A superficial burn affects epidermis, is often red and painful, and does not form the typical dermal blister pattern.

Understanding the options

Superficial epidermal burn
A superficial burn affects epidermis, is often red and painful, and does not form the typical dermal blister pattern.
Superficial partial-thickness burn
A moist, pink, blistered and very painful wound with preserved blanching suggests superficial dermal involvement. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
Deep partial-thickness burn
Deeper dermal injury may appear pale or mottled with reduced blanching and sensation and can require surgery. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
Full-thickness burn
Destruction of the full dermis produces a dry leathery wound that may be insensate centrally. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
Burn extending into deeper structures
Fourth-degree terminology describes extension beyond skin into tissue such as muscle, tendon or bone. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
