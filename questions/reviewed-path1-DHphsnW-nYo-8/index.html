Removing an adaptive stimulus

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

Serial bronchial samples from a smoker initially show mature squamous epithelium replacing ciliated cells. After sustained smoking cessation, follow-up samples show restoration of ciliated epithelium without treatment directed at a neoplasm. Which principle does this observation demonstrate?

  1. Mature squamous cells must directly divide into neurons
  2. Hypertrophy is necessarily irreversible
  3. Necrosis reverses through regeneration of the same dead cells
  4. Metaplasia can be reversible when the stimulus is removed
  5. Metaplasia always represents invasive cancer
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Metaplasia can be reversible when the stimulus is removed

Metaplastic epithelial populations can be replaced by the normal phenotype when persistent stress resolves.

Understanding the options

Metaplasia can be reversible when the stimulus is removed
Metaplastic epithelial populations can be replaced by the normal phenotype when persistent stress resolves.
Metaplasia always represents invasive cancer
Metaplasia is not itself synonymous with invasion.
Mature squamous cells must directly divide into neurons
Restoration involves altered differentiation of progenitor populations, not conversion to an unrelated neural lineage.
Hypertrophy is necessarily irreversible
Many hypertrophic adaptations also regress.
Necrosis reverses through regeneration of the same dead cells
Dead cells do not recover; regeneration replaces them with new cells.
