Matching prophylaxis to the evidence

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A 71-year-old with prior ulcer complications must continue an NSAID. A clinician discusses adding misoprostol. Which outcome best describes its labeled gastrointestinal preventive indication rather than an assumed general benefit for every digestive complaint?

  1. Eradication of H. pylori with monotherapy
  2. Rapid reversal of mechanical bowel obstruction
  3. Prevention of all NSAID-related renal injury
  4. Reliable elimination of all NSAID-associated abdominal pain
  5. Reduction in NSAID-induced gastric ulcer risk
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Reduction in NSAID-induced gastric ulcer risk

The labeled indication is reduction of NSAID-induced gastric ulcer risk in high-risk patients; symptoms and ulcers are not interchangeable outcomes.

Understanding the options

Reduction in NSAID-induced gastric ulcer risk
The labeled indication is reduction of NSAID-induced gastric ulcer risk in high-risk patients; symptoms and ulcers are not interchangeable outcomes.
Eradication of H. pylori with monotherapy
It is not an antimicrobial eradication regimen.
Rapid reversal of mechanical bowel obstruction
It is not a treatment for mechanical obstruction.
Prevention of all NSAID-related renal injury
Gastroprotection does not eliminate NSAID nephrotoxicity.
Reliable elimination of all NSAID-associated abdominal pain
It has not reliably eliminated all NSAID-related abdominal discomfort.
