Survival signaling in an abnormal clone

A lymphoid cell line overexpresses BCL-2. Compared with control cells, it survives growth-factor withdrawal despite signals that ordinarily trigger mitochondrial apoptosis. Which event is most directly inhibited by the increased activity of this antiapoptotic protein?

  1. Mitochondrial outer-membrane permeabilization
  2. ATP-dependent sodium pumping
  3. Extracellular collagen breakdown
  4. Lysosomal acidification
  5. Superoxide generation by NADPH oxidase
Answer and explanation

Mitochondrial outer-membrane permeabilization

Anti-apoptotic BCL-2 proteins oppose BAX/BAK-mediated mitochondrial membrane permeabilization and cytochrome c release.

Option explanations

Mitochondrial outer-membrane permeabilization: Anti-apoptotic BCL-2 proteins oppose BAX/BAK-mediated mitochondrial membrane permeabilization and cytochrome c release.

ATP-dependent sodium pumping: BCL-2 does not preserve cells primarily by directly operating sodium pumps.

Extracellular collagen breakdown: Matrix degradation is not the central mitochondrial apoptosis checkpoint.

Lysosomal acidification: Lysosomal acidity is not the direct target of this survival mechanism.

Superoxide generation by NADPH oxidase: The phagocyte oxidase system is distinct from the BCL-2 checkpoint.
