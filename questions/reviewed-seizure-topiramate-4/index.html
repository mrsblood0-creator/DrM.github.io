A change in classroom performance

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A 20-year-old college student has fewer seizures after topiramate titration but struggles to retrieve familiar words and completes assignments more slowly. Neurologic examination is otherwise unchanged. There are no discrete episodes of impaired awareness. Which explanation is most likely?

  1. Medication-associated cognitive slowing
  2. Recurrent focal impaired-awareness seizures
  3. Acute dominant-hemisphere infarction
  4. Primary progressive aphasia
  5. Myasthenia gravis
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Medication-associated cognitive slowing

Topiramate can impair word retrieval and processing speed.

Understanding the options

Medication-associated cognitive slowing
Topiramate can impair word retrieval and processing speed.
Recurrent focal impaired-awareness seizures
Continuous difficulty without discrete spells is less consistent with this explanation.
Acute dominant-hemisphere infarction
An abrupt focal deficit would be expected rather than difficulty emerging during titration.
Primary progressive aphasia
The age and close medication relationship make a neurodegenerative aphasia unlikely.
Myasthenia gravis
This causes fatigable weakness, not impaired word retrieval.
