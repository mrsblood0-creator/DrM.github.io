Transient AV nodal blockade

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A stable adolescent has regular narrow-complex SVT that persists after a vagal maneuver. A rapid intravenous bolus produces a brief pause followed by sinus rhythm. Which mechanism most likely terminated the tachycardia?

  1. Transient inhibition of atrioventricular nodal conduction
  2. Persistent inhibition of ventricular sodium channels
  3. Prolongation of ventricular repolarization
  4. Competitive blockade of cardiac beta receptors
  5. Suppression of automaticity by vagal stimulation at the carotid sinus
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Transient inhibition of atrioventricular nodal conduction

Adenosine briefly blocks AV nodal conduction and can interrupt an AV-node-dependent circuit.

Understanding the options

Transient inhibition of atrioventricular nodal conduction
Adenosine briefly blocks AV nodal conduction and can interrupt an AV-node-dependent circuit.
Persistent inhibition of ventricular sodium channels
Adenosine does not terminate AV-node-dependent SVT through sustained ventricular sodium-channel blockade.
Prolongation of ventricular repolarization
Potassium-channel-blocking antiarrhythmics prolong repolarization; adenosine acts predominantly through brief AV nodal inhibition.
Competitive blockade of cardiac beta receptors
Beta blockers slow nodal conduction, but the ultrashort pause after a rapid bolus is characteristic of adenosine.
Suppression of automaticity by vagal stimulation at the carotid sinus
A vagal maneuver can slow the AV node, but this response followed an intravenous drug rather than mechanical vagal stimulation.
