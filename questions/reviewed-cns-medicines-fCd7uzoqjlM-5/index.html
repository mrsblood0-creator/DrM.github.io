Benzodiazepines: 5

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

An older patient taking a long-acting benzodiazepine has daytime somnolence, impaired coordination and repeated falls. The clinician reviews exposure and safer management options. Which recognized adverse-effect category best explains the functional deterioration?

  1. Additive sedation and impaired alertness
  2. Complex sleep behavior
  3. Residual next-day impairment
  4. Additive CNS and respiratory depression
  5. Sedation-related falls
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Sedation-related falls

Impaired balance, coordination and alertness increase falls risk, especially in older adults.

Understanding the options

Sedation-related falls
Impaired balance, coordination and alertness increase falls risk, especially in older adults.
Additive sedation and impaired alertness
Combining melatonin with other sedating substances can increase drowsiness and impairment; review all medicines and alcohol exposure. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
Complex sleep behavior
Sleepwalking, sleep-driving or other activities without full awareness can occur with Z-drugs and require discontinuation and clinical review. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
Residual next-day impairment
Sedative effects may persist after awakening and impair driving even when the patient feels awake. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
Additive CNS and respiratory depression
Combining sedatives with opioids or alcohol increases risk of profound sedation and respiratory depression. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
