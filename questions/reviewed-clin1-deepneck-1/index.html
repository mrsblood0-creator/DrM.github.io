Torticollis after an upper respiratory infection

A 3-year-old develops fever, painful swallowing, and refusal to extend the neck several days after a respiratory infection. He holds his head tilted and has posterior pharyngeal fullness. Which diagnosis best integrates the age and examination?

  1. Retropharyngeal abscess
  2. Uncomplicated viral pharyngitis
  3. Peritonsillar abscess
  4. Viral croup
  5. Acute otitis media
Answer and explanation

Retropharyngeal abscess

Suppuration in retropharyngeal lymph nodes can follow infection in young children and produce torticollis and posterior swelling.

Option explanations

Retropharyngeal abscess: Suppuration in retropharyngeal lymph nodes can follow infection in young children and produce torticollis and posterior swelling.

Uncomplicated viral pharyngitis: Marked restriction of neck movement and a posterior mass suggest a complication.

Peritonsillar abscess: Quinsy more typically causes lateral peritonsillar bulging and trismus.

Viral croup: Croup usually produces a barking cough rather than painful neck restriction.

Acute otitis media: Otitis alone does not explain the posterior pharyngeal findings.
