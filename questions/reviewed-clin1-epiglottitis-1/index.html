Drooling and a forward-leaning posture

An incompletely immunized 4-year-old develops abrupt high fever, severe throat pain, drooling and difficulty swallowing. He sits leaning forward and has a muffled voice without a barking cough. Which diagnosis requires particular concern?

  1. Acute epiglottitis
  2. Typical viral croup
  3. Uncomplicated streptococcal pharyngitis
  4. Viral bronchiolitis
  5. Seasonal allergic rhinitis
Answer and explanation

Acute epiglottitis

The combination of toxic appearance, dysphagia, drooling and preferred upright posture suggests supraglottic inflammation.

Option explanations

Acute epiglottitis: The combination of toxic appearance, dysphagia, drooling and preferred upright posture suggests supraglottic inflammation.

Typical viral croup: Croup more often has a prominent barking cough and less severe swallowing difficulty.

Uncomplicated streptococcal pharyngitis: Uncomplicated pharyngitis should not produce this degree of airway distress.

Viral bronchiolitis: Bronchiolitis affects the small airways and does not explain drooling and muffled voice.

Seasonal allergic rhinitis: Allergic rhinitis does not account for high fever and severe dysphagia.
