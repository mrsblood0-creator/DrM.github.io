Nutrition when oral intake fails

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A 61-year-old man with severe acute pancreatitis cannot meet his nutritional needs orally. His gastrointestinal tract remains functional, and enteral tube placement is feasible. Which route is generally preferred over central venous nutritional support in this setting?

  1. Withhold nutrition until all imaging abnormalities resolve
  2. Enteral tube feeding
  3. Routine total parenteral nutrition first
  4. Only intravenous saline until discharge
  5. Only intravenous glucose for several weeks
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Enteral tube feeding

Enteral nutrition is preferred when oral intake is inadequate and the gut can be used.

Understanding the options

Enteral tube feeding
Enteral nutrition is preferred when oral intake is inadequate and the gut can be used.
Routine total parenteral nutrition first
Parenteral nutrition is generally reserved for inadequate or infeasible enteral feeding.
Only intravenous saline until discharge
Saline does not meet nutritional requirements.
Only intravenous glucose for several weeks
Glucose alone is not complete nutrition.
Withhold nutrition until all imaging abnormalities resolve
Imaging resolution is not a prerequisite for nutritional support.
