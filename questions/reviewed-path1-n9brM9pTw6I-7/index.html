Tissue injury after flow returns

An ischemic intestinal segment regains blood flow after removal of an arterial embolus. Some epithelial cells die despite restored oxygen delivery. Analysis shows abrupt oxidant generation and inflammatory-cell recruitment. Which mechanism best accounts for this additional injury?

  1. Reperfusion-associated reactive oxygen species production
  2. Immediate repair of every mitochondrial membrane
  3. Complete prevention of neutrophil activation
  4. Permanent suppression of all oxygen metabolism
  5. Conversion of necrotic cells into normal cells
Answer and explanation

Reperfusion-associated reactive oxygen species production

Returning oxygen can fuel oxidant formation in injured cells and recruited leukocytes; calcium overload and inflammation also contribute to reperfusion injury.

Option explanations

Reperfusion-associated reactive oxygen species production: Returning oxygen can fuel oxidant formation in injured cells and recruited leukocytes; calcium overload and inflammation also contribute to reperfusion injury.

Immediate repair of every mitochondrial membrane: Restoring flow does not instantly reverse accumulated organelle injury.

Complete prevention of neutrophil activation: Reperfusion can promote rather than prevent inflammatory activation.

Permanent suppression of all oxygen metabolism: Flow restoration reintroduces oxygen; permanent suppression does not explain the observed burst.

Conversion of necrotic cells into normal cells: Cells already irreversibly injured cannot be restored by perfusion.
