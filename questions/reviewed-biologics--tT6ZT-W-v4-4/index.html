Corticosteroid adjustment

A 46-year-old woman with severe eosinophilic asthma improves after starting mepolizumab. She has taken systemic corticosteroids for months and wants to stop them immediately. Which plan best addresses medication withdrawal?

  1. Double the biologic dose to prevent steroid withdrawal
  2. Reduce corticosteroids gradually under clinician supervision when appropriate
  3. Stop all corticosteroids on the day of the next injection
  4. Replace the corticosteroid with extra mepolizumab as needed
  5. Assume normal adrenal function because asthma symptoms improved
Answer and explanation

Reduce corticosteroids gradually under clinician supervision when appropriate

Biologic improvement does not remove the risks of abrupt corticosteroid withdrawal.

Option explanations

Reduce corticosteroids gradually under clinician supervision when appropriate: Biologic improvement does not remove the risks of abrupt corticosteroid withdrawal.

Stop all corticosteroids on the day of the next injection: Abrupt discontinuation may cause adrenal insufficiency or reveal previously suppressed disease.

Replace the corticosteroid with extra mepolizumab as needed: Mepolizumab is not an as-needed steroid replacement.

Assume normal adrenal function because asthma symptoms improved: Symptom improvement does not demonstrate recovery of the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis.

Double the biologic dose to prevent steroid withdrawal: Dose escalation is not a substitute for an appropriate taper.
