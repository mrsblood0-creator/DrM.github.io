An occult wrist fracture

A 23-year-old man falls onto his outstretched hand while skating. He has focal anatomic snuffbox tenderness and pain with gripping. Initial wrist radiographs show no fracture. Which approach best addresses the suspected injury?

  1. Begin forceful wrist manipulation before reassessment
  2. Exclude fracture unless the wrist is visibly deformed
  3. Immobilize the wrist and arrange further imaging or timely reassessment
  4. Allow unrestricted activity because the radiograph is normal
  5. Diagnose a complete scapholunate tear solely from snuffbox tenderness
Answer and explanation

Immobilize the wrist and arrange further imaging or timely reassessment

An initially occult scaphoid fracture remains possible; a normal first radiograph does not safely exclude it.

Option explanations

Immobilize the wrist and arrange further imaging or timely reassessment: An initially occult scaphoid fracture remains possible; a normal first radiograph does not safely exclude it.

Allow unrestricted activity because the radiograph is normal: The clinical suspicion remains despite an initially normal film.

Diagnose a complete scapholunate tear solely from snuffbox tenderness: That finding does not establish a specific ligament tear.

Begin forceful wrist manipulation before reassessment: This may worsen an unrecognized fracture.

Exclude fracture unless the wrist is visibly deformed: Scaphoid fractures can occur without obvious deformity.
