Confirming the deposit type

A 76-year-old man has restrictive cardiomyopathy, Congo red-positive cardiac deposits, and a small monoclonal protein in serum. Immunohistochemical typing is inconclusive. Which test most directly establishes the protein composing the deposits before subtype-specific treatment?

  1. Mass spectrometry of microdissected amyloid deposits
  2. Repeating serum total protein concentration
  3. Measuring serum troponin alone
  4. Repeating Congo red staining alone
  5. Measuring urinary albumin concentration
Answer and explanation

Mass spectrometry of microdissected amyloid deposits

Proteomic analysis identifies the deposited protein. A monoclonal protein alone does not prove AL because monoclonal gammopathy can coexist with other amyloid types.

Option explanations

Mass spectrometry of microdissected amyloid deposits: Proteomic analysis identifies the deposited protein. A monoclonal protein alone does not prove AL because monoclonal gammopathy can coexist with other amyloid types.

Repeating serum total protein concentration: Total protein does not identify the protein within tissue deposits.

Measuring serum troponin alone: Troponin reflects cardiac injury, not fibril identity.

Repeating Congo red staining alone: Congo red confirms amyloid but does not distinguish the precursor.

Measuring urinary albumin concentration: Albuminuria identifies renal involvement, not amyloid type.
