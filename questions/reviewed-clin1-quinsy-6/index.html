Referred ear pain

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A 20-year-old with unilateral peritonsillar swelling reports severe ear pain on the same side. Otoscopy is normal, and there is no mastoid tenderness or hearing loss. Which mechanism best explains the otalgia in this setting?

  1. Referred sensory pain through the glossopharyngeal nerve
  2. Referred sensory pain through the ophthalmic trigeminal division
  3. Primary inflammation of the tympanic membrane
  4. Obstruction of the external auditory canal
  5. Spread of infection to the mastoid air cells
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Referred sensory pain through the glossopharyngeal nerve

The glossopharyngeal nerve supplies the tonsillar region and contributes sensory innervation to the middle ear, permitting referred pain.

Understanding the options

Referred sensory pain through the glossopharyngeal nerve
The glossopharyngeal nerve supplies the tonsillar region and contributes sensory innervation to the middle ear, permitting referred pain.
Referred sensory pain through the ophthalmic trigeminal division
This division does not supply the tonsillar region and middle-ear sensory pathway implicated here.
Primary inflammation of the tympanic membrane
Otoscopy is normal, making a primary middle-ear process less likely.
Obstruction of the external auditory canal
Canal obstruction would be detected on ear examination and does not explain the association with quinsy.
Spread of infection to the mastoid air cells
Mastoid disease generally produces relevant ear or mastoid findings, which are absent.
