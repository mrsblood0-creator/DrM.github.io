Penetrating trauma priorities

A 31-year-old has an isolated penetrating torso injury with severe external bleeding. He is alert, moves all limbs normally and has no blunt-trauma mechanism or neurologic symptoms. What is the best approach to routine long-board immobilization before transport?

  1. Use a collar as a substitute for hemorrhage control
  2. Do not delay hemorrhage control and transport for routine immobilization
  3. Delay bleeding control until full long-board packaging is complete
  4. Apply routine full immobilization to every penetrating injury regardless of findings
  5. Assume penetrating injuries never require spine assessment
Answer and explanation

Do not delay hemorrhage control and transport for routine immobilization

Routine motion restriction is generally not indicated solely for isolated penetrating trauma without neurologic findings; lifesaving care takes priority.

Option explanations

Do not delay hemorrhage control and transport for routine immobilization: Routine motion restriction is generally not indicated solely for isolated penetrating trauma without neurologic findings; lifesaving care takes priority.

Delay bleeding control until full long-board packaging is complete: This can delay treatment of the immediate threat.

Apply routine full immobilization to every penetrating injury regardless of findings: Mechanism and clinical findings matter; a blanket approach is not supported.

Assume penetrating injuries never require spine assessment: Spinal injury can occur, so assessment remains necessary.

Use a collar as a substitute for hemorrhage control: A collar does not treat life-threatening blood loss.
