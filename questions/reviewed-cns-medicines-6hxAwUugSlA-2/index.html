Barbiturates: 2

A patient taking phenobarbital has reduced exposure to another medicine metabolized by hepatic enzymes. Adherence to the second drug is unchanged. Which pharmacokinetic property of phenobarbital best explains the lower concentration of the other medicine?

  1. Hepatic enzyme induction
  2. Prolonged GABA-A chloride-channel opening
  3. Increased frequency of GABA-A channel opening
  4. Precipitation of acute porphyria
  5. Redistribution from brain to other tissues
Answer and explanation

Hepatic enzyme induction

Phenobarbital induces drug-metabolizing enzymes and can reduce exposure to susceptible coadministered medicines.

Option explanations

Hepatic enzyme induction: Phenobarbital induces drug-metabolizing enzymes and can reduce exposure to susceptible coadministered medicines.

Prolonged GABA-A chloride-channel opening: Barbiturates classically increase the duration of GABA-A channel opening and can produce profound depression at high exposure. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Increased frequency of GABA-A channel opening: Benzodiazepines increase opening frequency in the presence of GABA. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Precipitation of acute porphyria: Barbiturates can provoke attacks in susceptible acute hepatic porphyrias. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Redistribution from brain to other tissues: A brief effect after an IV lipophilic anesthetic bolus can end through redistribution before elimination is complete. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
