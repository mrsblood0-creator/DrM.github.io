Accumulation after renal decline

A 76-year-old man on stable levetiracetam therapy develops somnolence after dehydration-associated acute kidney injury. He has no focal neurologic deficit, hypoglycemia, or new sedative exposure. Which pharmacokinetic change most plausibly increases his exposure to the antiseizure medication?

  1. Increased hepatic first-pass extraction
  2. Increased elimination through bile
  3. Reduced renal elimination of unchanged drug
  4. Reduced hepatic CYP3A4 metabolism
  5. Increased renal clearance
Answer and explanation

Reduced renal elimination of unchanged drug

Renal impairment reduces levetiracetam clearance.

Option explanations

Reduced renal elimination of unchanged drug: Renal impairment reduces levetiracetam clearance.

Reduced hepatic CYP3A4 metabolism: CYP3A4 is not its principal elimination pathway.

Increased renal clearance: This would lower, rather than increase, exposure.

Increased hepatic first-pass extraction: Greater first-pass removal would reduce oral systemic availability.

Increased elimination through bile: Greater elimination would not explain accumulation.
