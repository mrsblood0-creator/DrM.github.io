Statins: 1

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A patient begins a statin to lower LDL cholesterol. The medication reduces hepatic cholesterol production by inhibiting a key enzyme rather than directly trapping dietary fat in the bowel. Which mechanism best explains its primary action?

  1. Increased statin exposure through a drug interaction
  2. Reduction of atherosclerotic cardiovascular risk
  3. HMG-CoA reductase inhibition
  4. Increased hepatic LDL receptor expression
  5. Statin-associated muscle injury
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

HMG-CoA reductase inhibition

Statins inhibit the rate-limiting enzyme in hepatic cholesterol synthesis.

Understanding the options

HMG-CoA reductase inhibition
Statins inhibit the rate-limiting enzyme in hepatic cholesterol synthesis.
Increased hepatic LDL receptor expression
Reduced hepatic cholesterol promotes LDL receptor expression and circulating LDL clearance. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
Statin-associated muscle injury
New muscle pain with marked CK elevation requires assessment for myopathy or rhabdomyolysis. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
Increased statin exposure through a drug interaction
CYP3A4 inhibitors can increase exposure to susceptible statins such as simvastatin. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
Reduction of atherosclerotic cardiovascular risk
Appropriate statin therapy reduces cardiovascular events, not merely the laboratory LDL number. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
