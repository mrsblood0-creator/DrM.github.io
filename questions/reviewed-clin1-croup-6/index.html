Normal saturation with significant work

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A 2-year-old with croup has an oxygen saturation of 98% but persistent stridor, marked retractions and increasing agitation. A trainee calls the illness mild because saturation is normal. Which interpretation is more accurate?

  1. Normal saturation does not exclude significant upper-airway obstruction
  2. Severity is determined only by oxygen saturation
  3. Retractions indicate lower-airway disease exclusively
  4. Agitation proves symptoms are behavioral
  5. Stridor at rest is always benign
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Normal saturation does not exclude significant upper-airway obstruction

Hypoxemia may be a late sign; effort, air entry and mental state are essential to severity assessment.

Understanding the options

Normal saturation does not exclude significant upper-airway obstruction
Hypoxemia may be a late sign; effort, air entry and mental state are essential to severity assessment.
Severity is determined only by oxygen saturation
A single oxygen reading cannot quantify obstruction severity.
Retractions indicate lower-airway disease exclusively
Retractions can occur against a narrowed upper airway.
Agitation proves symptoms are behavioral
Agitation may reflect distress or developing hypoxia.
Stridor at rest is always benign
Persistent rest stridor requires assessment in its clinical context.
