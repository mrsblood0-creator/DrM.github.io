Diarrhea after antibiotic exposure

A patient completes rifaximin and later develops frequent watery stools and abdominal pain. The patient assumes that a poorly absorbed antibiotic cannot cause antibiotic-associated colitis. Which diagnosis remains important to evaluate?

  1. Primary adrenal insufficiency caused by RNA-polymerase inhibition
  2. Mechanical gastric outlet obstruction
  3. Nephrotic syndrome from reduced intestinal absorption
  4. Clostridioides difficile infection
  5. Acute pancreatitis solely because stools are watery
Answer and explanation

Clostridioides difficile infection

Local antibiotic exposure can still alter the intestinal microbial environment.

Option explanations

Clostridioides difficile infection: Local antibiotic exposure can still alter the intestinal microbial environment.

Acute pancreatitis solely because stools are watery: The presentation does not specifically establish pancreatic inflammation.

Primary adrenal insufficiency caused by RNA-polymerase inhibition: This is not an established mechanism of the drug.

Mechanical gastric outlet obstruction: This usually produces a different clinical pattern, particularly vomiting and retained gastric contents.

Nephrotic syndrome from reduced intestinal absorption: Poor absorption does not explain proteinuria or this diarrheal syndrome.
