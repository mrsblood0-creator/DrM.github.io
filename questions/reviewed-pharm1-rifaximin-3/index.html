Preventing recurrent encephalopathy

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A 59-year-old with cirrhosis has had several episodes of overt hepatic encephalopathy despite adherence to lactulose. An oral, minimally absorbed antibiotic is added for recurrence reduction. Which action most directly contributes to its benefit?

  1. Competitive antagonism of cerebral benzodiazepine receptors
  2. Chelation of circulating ammonia in plasma
  3. Inhibition of renal glutamine metabolism
  4. Reduction of intestinal bacterial contributions to nitrogenous toxin production
  5. Direct stimulation of hepatic urea-cycle enzymes
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Reduction of intestinal bacterial contributions to nitrogenous toxin production

Gut bacterial activity contributes to the nitrogen burden relevant to encephalopathy.

Understanding the options

Reduction of intestinal bacterial contributions to nitrogenous toxin production
Gut bacterial activity contributes to the nitrogen burden relevant to encephalopathy.
Direct stimulation of hepatic urea-cycle enzymes
Rifaximin does not restore hepatocyte urea-cycle capacity directly.
Competitive antagonism of cerebral benzodiazepine receptors
This describes a different pharmacological strategy, not rifaximin.
Chelation of circulating ammonia in plasma
The drug is an intestinal antibiotic, not a systemic ammonia chelator.
Inhibition of renal glutamine metabolism
A renal enzyme effect is not its principal therapeutic mechanism.
