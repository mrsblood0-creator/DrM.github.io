Codeine: 3

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A patient receives little analgesia from codeine despite adherence. Testing identifies very low CYP2D6 activity, and no other explanation is evident. Which pharmacokinetic mechanism best accounts for the reduced response to treatment?

  1. Reduced morphine formation in a poor metabolizer
  2. CYP2D6 conversion to morphine
  3. Excess morphine formation in an ultrarapid metabolizer
  4. Pediatric respiratory risk and prescribing restrictions
  5. Reduced activation from CYP2D6 inhibition
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Reduced morphine formation in a poor metabolizer

Low CYP2D6 activity can limit codeine analgesia.

Understanding the options

Reduced morphine formation in a poor metabolizer
Low CYP2D6 activity can limit codeine analgesia.
CYP2D6 conversion to morphine
Codeine’s analgesic effect partly depends on conversion to morphine by CYP2D6. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
Excess morphine formation in an ultrarapid metabolizer
Rapid CYP2D6 conversion can cause unexpectedly high morphine exposure and respiratory toxicity. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
Pediatric respiratory risk and prescribing restrictions
Codeine has important age- and procedure-specific contraindications; it is not a routine safe pediatric analgesic. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
Reduced activation from CYP2D6 inhibition
A strong CYP2D6 inhibitor can reduce conversion of codeine to morphine and alter response. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
