Imaging a stable deep-neck infection

A 4-year-old has fever, painful neck extension, and dysphagia. He is breathing comfortably without stridor and maintains oxygenation. A deep-neck collection is suspected, but its size and spread are unclear. Which imaging study is most useful?

  1. Contrast-enhanced neck CT with airway support available
  2. Lateral neck radiography as the definitive study
  3. Ultrasound confined to the superficial cervical nodes
  4. Noncontrast neck CT
  5. Routine chest radiography alone
Answer and explanation

Contrast-enhanced neck CT with airway support available

Contrast CT can localize a collection and assess deep-space extension; airway planning remains essential.

Option explanations

Contrast-enhanced neck CT with airway support available: Contrast CT can localize a collection and assess deep-space extension; airway planning remains essential.

Lateral neck radiography as the definitive study: A plain film may suggest prevertebral swelling but is less useful for defining collection size and deep extension.

Ultrasound confined to the superficial cervical nodes: Ultrasound has limited access to some deep retropharyngeal structures and may not map the suspected collection.

Noncontrast neck CT: Contrast better characterizes enhancement and helps distinguish a collection from surrounding tissues.

Routine chest radiography alone: A chest film cannot adequately define the suspected cervical collection.
