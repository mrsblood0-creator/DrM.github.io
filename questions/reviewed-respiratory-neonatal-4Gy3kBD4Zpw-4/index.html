Bronchial asthma: 4

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A patient whose asthma is usually controlled develops wheezing after a viral upper-respiratory illness. There is no focal consolidation or other evidence establishing bacterial pneumonia. Which mechanism best explains this familiar pattern of symptom worsening?

  1. Trigger-associated worsening of airway narrowing
  2. Variable expiratory airflow limitation
  3. Repeat objective testing when symptomatic or use an appropriate variability test
  4. Type 2 airway inflammation
  5. Bronchial hyperresponsiveness
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Trigger-associated worsening of airway narrowing

Viral infections, allergens and irritants can provoke symptoms in susceptible airways without each episode implying bacterial pneumonia.

Understanding the options

Trigger-associated worsening of airway narrowing
Viral infections, allergens and irritants can provoke symptoms in susceptible airways without each episode implying bacterial pneumonia.
Variable expiratory airflow limitation
Episodic symptoms plus demonstrably variable expiratory airflow support asthma; variability is a defining diagnostic feature. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
Repeat objective testing when symptomatic or use an appropriate variability test
Normal spirometry between episodes does not exclude asthma; diagnostic confirmation may require repeated or alternative testing. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
Type 2 airway inflammation
Eosinophilic inflammation is a common asthma phenotype, involving pathways such as IL-4, IL-5 and IL-13; not all asthma is eosinophilic. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
Bronchial hyperresponsiveness
Excessive airway narrowing to a stimulus is characteristic of hyperresponsive airways, although challenge results require clinical interpretation. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
