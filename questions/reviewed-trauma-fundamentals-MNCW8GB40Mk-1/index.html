Orthopedic trauma history and examination: 1

A patient reports falling onto an arm, but the clinician asks about height, speed, direction and whether the hand was fixed. Which history component helps predict associated injuries and distinguish a low-energy event from major trauma?

  1. Document a neurovascular examination
  2. Repeat examinations when symptoms evolve
  3. Complete the broader trauma assessment
  4. Clarify mechanism and energy of injury
  5. Inspect the skin for an open injury
Answer and explanation

Clarify mechanism and energy of injury

Mechanism helps predict associated injury, instability and the need for broader assessment.

Option explanations

Clarify mechanism and energy of injury: Mechanism helps predict associated injury, instability and the need for broader assessment.

Inspect the skin for an open injury: A small communicating wound can make a fracture open even without visible exposed bone. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Document a neurovascular examination: Motor, sensory and perfusion findings should be recorded before and after interventions. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Repeat examinations when symptoms evolve: Swelling, pain and neurologic findings can change; a normal initial examination does not end surveillance. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
