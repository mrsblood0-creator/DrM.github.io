Etomidate and routine steroids

A 60-year-old receives etomidate for emergency intubation. He has no separate indication for corticosteroids. A colleague proposes routine hydrocortisone solely to prevent adverse effects of transient etomidate-associated adrenal suppression. Which response best follows the RSI guideline?

  1. Give corticosteroids to every patient who receives etomidate
  2. Reverse etomidate with naloxone
  3. Use flumazenil to restore cortisol synthesis
  4. Give succinylcholine to correct adrenal suppression
  5. Do not routinely give corticosteroids solely for this reason
Answer and explanation

Do not routinely give corticosteroids solely for this reason

SCCM suggests against routine corticosteroids solely to counter etomidate-related adrenal suppression.

Option explanations

Do not routinely give corticosteroids solely for this reason: SCCM suggests against routine corticosteroids solely to counter etomidate-related adrenal suppression.

Give corticosteroids to every patient who receives etomidate: Etomidate exposure alone does not establish this indication.

Reverse etomidate with naloxone: Naloxone does not reverse etomidate.

Use flumazenil to restore cortisol synthesis: Flumazenil targets benzodiazepine effects.

Give succinylcholine to correct adrenal suppression: Neuromuscular blockade does not restore adrenal steroid synthesis.
