Penicillin pharmacology: 2

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A staphylococcal isolate inactivates penicillin before the drug can bind its target. Testing demonstrates an enzyme that cleaves the characteristic antibiotic ring. Which resistance mechanism accounts for the loss of activity in this isolate?

  1. Binding to D-Ala-D-Ala
  2. Altered penicillin-binding proteins
  3. Absence of a peptidoglycan cell wall
  4. Hydrolysis of the beta-lactam ring
  5. Inhibition of peptidoglycan cross-linking
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Hydrolysis of the beta-lactam ring

Beta-lactamases inactivate susceptible beta-lactam drugs by ring hydrolysis.

Understanding the options

Hydrolysis of the beta-lactam ring
Beta-lactamases inactivate susceptible beta-lactam drugs by ring hydrolysis.
Inhibition of peptidoglycan cross-linking
Beta-lactams bind PBPs and inhibit transpeptidation during cell-wall synthesis. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
Binding to D-Ala-D-Ala
Vancomycin binds the terminal dipeptide of peptidoglycan precursors. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
Altered penicillin-binding proteins
Changes in PBPs can reduce beta-lactam affinity; PBP2a is central to methicillin resistance. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
Absence of a peptidoglycan cell wall
Mycoplasma lacks the target required for beta-lactam activity. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
