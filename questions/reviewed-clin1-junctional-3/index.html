P waves hidden within the QRS

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A regular narrow-complex rhythm at 52/min has no visible P waves. A repeat tracing later shows small retrograde P waves just after the QRS. How can the initially absent P waves be explained without assuming the atria were electrically inactive?

  1. Atrial depolarization occurred simultaneously with ventricular activation and was hidden in the QRS
  2. Every atrial cell had become permanently inexcitable
  3. The ECG machine cannot record any atrial activity
  4. The patient necessarily had ventricular fibrillation
  5. The atria were anatomically absent
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Atrial depolarization occurred simultaneously with ventricular activation and was hidden in the QRS

Timing of retrograde atrial conduction can place the P wave before, within or after the QRS.

Understanding the options

Atrial depolarization occurred simultaneously with ventricular activation and was hidden in the QRS
Timing of retrograde atrial conduction can place the P wave before, within or after the QRS.
Every atrial cell had become permanently inexcitable
The subsequent organized P waves argue against permanent atrial inexcitability.
The ECG machine cannot record any atrial activity
ECGs can record atrial activity when its signal is visible.
The patient necessarily had ventricular fibrillation
Ventricular fibrillation is chaotic and incompatible with this organized rhythm.
The atria were anatomically absent
A changing surface tracing does not imply anatomic absence.
