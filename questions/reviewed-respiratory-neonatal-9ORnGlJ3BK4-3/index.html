Atelectasis on chest radiographs: 3

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A radiologist suspects lobar collapse and compares the expected fissure location with the current film. The involved lobe appears smaller, and its boundary has moved. Which finding most directly supports local lobar volume loss?

  1. Air-space consolidation without appreciable volume loss
  2. Displacement of a fissure toward the collapsed lobe
  3. Pulmonary volume loss
  4. Shift toward the side of volume loss
  5. Resorption atelectasis from airway obstruction
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Displacement of a fissure toward the collapsed lobe

Fissural movement is a useful local sign of lobar volume loss.

Understanding the options

Displacement of a fissure toward the collapsed lobe
Fissural movement is a useful local sign of lobar volume loss.
Pulmonary volume loss
Atelectasis combines reduced aeration with loss of lung volume, helping distinguish it from uncomplicated air-space filling. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
Shift toward the side of volume loss
Substantial collapse can pull the mediastinum toward the affected side; a pressure-producing pleural process may push it away. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
Resorption atelectasis from airway obstruction
A blocked bronchus prevents replenishment of alveolar gas, which is absorbed, allowing distal collapse. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
Air-space consolidation without appreciable volume loss
Alveolar filling can produce opacity and air bronchograms while preserving volume; opacity alone does not establish collapse. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
