New low sodium

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A 76-year-old woman becomes confused and unsteady 2 weeks after starting sertraline. She is clinically euvolemic. Sodium is 122 mmol/L, serum osmolality is low, urine remains concentrated, and urine sodium is elevated. Renal function, morning cortisol and thyroid studies are normal. Which mechanism best explains these findings?

  1. Aldosterone excess
  2. Isolated excess water intake with fully suppressed ADH
  3. Inappropriate antidiuretic hormone activity
  4. Loss of renal response to antidiuretic hormone
  5. Hyperglycemia-induced extracellular water shift
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Inappropriate antidiuretic hormone activity

The pattern suggests impaired water excretion consistent with drug-associated SIADH; symptomatic hyponatremia needs prompt assessment.

Understanding the options

Inappropriate antidiuretic hormone activity
The pattern suggests impaired water excretion consistent with drug-associated SIADH; symptomatic hyponatremia needs prompt assessment.
Loss of renal response to antidiuretic hormone
This produces dilute urine and tends toward hypernatremia if water intake is inadequate.
Hyperglycemia-induced extracellular water shift
Glucose-related hyponatremia is not the hypotonic pattern described.
Aldosterone excess
This does not explain euvolemic hypotonic hyponatremia with concentrated urine.
Isolated excess water intake with fully suppressed ADH
With intact suppression, urine would usually be maximally dilute.
