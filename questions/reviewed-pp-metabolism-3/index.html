Enzyme induction of an active drug

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A patient is stable on an active medication cleared mainly by CYP3A4. A potent inducer of that enzyme is added, and no active metabolite contributes to efficacy. After induction develops, what change in the original drug’s exposure is most likely?

  1. Lower exposure from increased metabolic clearance
  2. Higher exposure from blocked metabolism
  3. No interaction is possible with any CYP substrate
  4. Complete conversion into a permanent receptor antagonist
  5. Elimination becomes independent of enzyme activity
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Lower exposure from increased metabolic clearance

Enzyme induction can accelerate metabolism of an active substrate drug.

Understanding the options

Lower exposure from increased metabolic clearance
Enzyme induction can accelerate metabolism of an active substrate drug.
Higher exposure from blocked metabolism
Induction increases rather than blocks metabolic capacity.
No interaction is possible with any CYP substrate
Substrates can be affected by induction.
Complete conversion into a permanent receptor antagonist
Induction does not inherently change receptor activity in that way.
Elimination becomes independent of enzyme activity
The stem specifies enzyme-dependent clearance.
