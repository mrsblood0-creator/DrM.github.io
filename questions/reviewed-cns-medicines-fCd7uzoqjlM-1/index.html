Benzodiazepines: 1

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A patient receives diazepam for an appropriate acute indication. At the receptor level, the drug enhances the effect of endogenous GABA rather than directly substituting for it. Which classic channel-gating change explains benzodiazepine action?

  1. Hepatic enzyme induction
  2. Precipitation of acute porphyria
  3. Redistribution from brain to other tissues
  4. Increased frequency of GABA-A channel opening
  5. Prolonged GABA-A chloride-channel opening
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Increased frequency of GABA-A channel opening

Benzodiazepines increase opening frequency in the presence of GABA.

Understanding the options

Increased frequency of GABA-A channel opening
Benzodiazepines increase opening frequency in the presence of GABA.
Prolonged GABA-A chloride-channel opening
Barbiturates classically increase the duration of GABA-A channel opening and can produce profound depression at high exposure. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
Hepatic enzyme induction
Phenobarbital induces drug-metabolizing enzymes and can reduce exposure to susceptible coadministered medicines. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
Precipitation of acute porphyria
Barbiturates can provoke attacks in susceptible acute hepatic porphyrias. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
Redistribution from brain to other tissues
A brief effect after an IV lipophilic anesthetic bolus can end through redistribution before elimination is complete. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
