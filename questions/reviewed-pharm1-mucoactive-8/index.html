Persistent cough despite symptom treatment

A 57-year-old man has taken guaifenesin for nine days. Although sputum is easier to expectorate, his cough persists and he has developed fever and pleuritic chest discomfort. Which interpretation best supports the next step?

  1. Increasing the dose will substitute for assessment of persistent symptoms
  2. All productive coughs can be managed indefinitely with an expectorant
  3. Symptomatic mucus relief does not treat the underlying cause and reassessment is needed
  4. Easier expectoration excludes pneumonia
  5. The fever confirms guaifenesin is clearing the infection
Answer and explanation

Symptomatic mucus relief does not treat the underlying cause and reassessment is needed

Guaifenesin may relieve congestion without treating the cause; persistent or worsening symptoms need evaluation.

Option explanations

Symptomatic mucus relief does not treat the underlying cause and reassessment is needed: The new systemic and chest symptoms warrant assessment of the underlying illness.

Easier expectoration excludes pneumonia: Improved expectoration does not rule out pneumonia or another complication.

The fever confirms guaifenesin is clearing the infection: Fever is not proof of a beneficial expectorant effect.

Increasing the dose will substitute for assessment of persistent symptoms: Dose escalation cannot replace evaluation of a worsening illness.

All productive coughs can be managed indefinitely with an expectorant: A persistent cough requires attention to its cause, not indefinite symptomatic treatment.
