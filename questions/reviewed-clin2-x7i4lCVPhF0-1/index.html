Rescue oxygenation

After induction of anesthesia, tracheal intubation is unsuccessful and face-mask ventilation is difficult despite optimization. A trained anesthesiologist has an appropriate supraglottic airway available. Oxygen saturation is falling. What is the most appropriate purpose of inserting this device?

  1. Establish a surgical opening below the larynx
  2. Replace the need to assess ventilation after insertion
  3. Treat bronchospasm by directly relaxing airway smooth muscle
  4. Restore ventilation and oxygenation while planning the next airway step
  5. Guarantee permanent protection from all aspiration
Answer and explanation

Restore ventilation and oxygenation while planning the next airway step

A supraglottic device can serve as rescue ventilation; oxygenation takes priority over repeating unsuccessful laryngoscopy.

Option explanations

Restore ventilation and oxygenation while planning the next airway step: A supraglottic device can serve as rescue ventilation; oxygenation takes priority over repeating unsuccessful laryngoscopy.

Guarantee permanent protection from all aspiration: The device does not provide an absolute tracheal barrier.

Establish a surgical opening below the larynx: A supraglottic device is not an invasive front-of-neck airway.

Replace the need to assess ventilation after insertion: Placement must be followed by confirmation of effective ventilation.

Treat bronchospasm by directly relaxing airway smooth muscle: The device itself is not a bronchodilator.
