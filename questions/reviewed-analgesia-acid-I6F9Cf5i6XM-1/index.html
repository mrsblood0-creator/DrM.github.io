Nefopam: 1

A clinician chooses nefopam for an appropriate pain indication and explains that it is not an opioid or a conventional anti-inflammatory NSAID. Which description best reflects its central analgesic action without overstating certainty about every molecular mechanism?

  1. Antimuscarinic-type adverse effects
  2. Tachycardia and sweating
  3. Central nonopioid analgesia
  4. Seizure risk
  5. MAO inhibitor interaction
Answer and explanation

Central nonopioid analgesia

Nefopam has centrally mediated analgesic effects; monoamine handling is implicated, but its mechanism is not fully defined.

Option explanations

Central nonopioid analgesia: Nefopam has centrally mediated analgesic effects; monoamine handling is implicated, but its mechanism is not fully defined.

Seizure risk: Nefopam is contraindicated in patients with a history of convulsive disorders in relevant product labeling. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

MAO inhibitor interaction: Nefopam should not be combined with MAO inhibitors under its prescribing restrictions. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Antimuscarinic-type adverse effects: Dry mouth and urinary retention can occur; angle-closure glaucoma warrants caution. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Tachycardia and sweating: Nefopam can produce autonomic adverse effects including tachycardia and sweating. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
