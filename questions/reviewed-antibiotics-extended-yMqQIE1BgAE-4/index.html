Penicillin pharmacology: 4

Minutes after receiving penicillin, a patient develops generalized hives, wheezing and hypotension. The infusion is stopped and emergency treatment begins. Which adverse-reaction category best describes this rapid multisystem presentation following exposure to the antibiotic?

  1. Rate-related vancomycin infusion reaction
  2. Immediate hypersensitivity
  3. Ceftriaxone-calcium precipitation
  4. Concentration-related neurotoxicity
  5. Reduced valproate exposure
Answer and explanation

Immediate hypersensitivity

Urticaria, wheeze and hypotension soon after exposure suggest anaphylaxis and require emergency treatment.

Option explanations

Immediate hypersensitivity: Urticaria, wheeze and hypotension soon after exposure suggest anaphylaxis and require emergency treatment.

Ceftriaxone-calcium precipitation: Ceftriaxone is contraindicated in neonates requiring IV calcium-containing solutions because of precipitation risk. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Concentration-related neurotoxicity: Accumulation of some beta-lactams in renal impairment can cause encephalopathy, myoclonus or seizures. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Reduced valproate exposure: Carbapenems can markedly lower valproate concentrations and increase breakthrough seizure risk. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Rate-related vancomycin infusion reaction: Flushing and pruritus during rapid vancomycin infusion can reflect non-IgE-mediated mast-cell activation. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
