Oxidant-mediated loss of protein function

Cells exposed to an oxidant retain normal quantities of an enzyme but show sharply reduced enzyme activity. Protein analysis reveals abnormal cross-links and oxidation of amino-acid side chains. Which process best explains the result?

  1. Oxidative modification of protein structure
  2. Increased transcription of the same functional enzyme
  3. Simple dilution of the enzyme by extracellular fluid
  4. Selective replacement of DNA with RNA
  5. Physiologic extracellular collagen maturation
Answer and explanation

Oxidative modification of protein structure

Oxidation can alter active sites, folding, and intermolecular interactions, impairing function even when the amount of protein is unchanged.

Option explanations

Oxidative modification of protein structure: Oxidation can alter active sites, folding, and intermolecular interactions, impairing function even when the amount of protein is unchanged.

Increased transcription of the same functional enzyme: More intact enzyme would not explain inactivation of the existing molecules.

Simple dilution of the enzyme by extracellular fluid: Measured protein amount is unchanged and structural alterations are demonstrated.

Selective replacement of DNA with RNA: This is not a recognized mechanism of protein oxidation.

Physiologic extracellular collagen maturation: The finding involves damaged intracellular enzyme molecules rather than normal matrix maturation.
