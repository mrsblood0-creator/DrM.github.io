Seizure after treatment

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A 42-year-old man taking bupropion for depression has a witnessed generalized tonic-clonic seizure after a recent dose increase. Glucose and sodium are normal, and brain imaging shows no acute lesion. He recovers after emergency treatment. Which medication plan is most appropriate?

  1. Restart the previous dose after 24 hours
  2. Continue bupropion and add prophylactic phenytoin
  3. Combine the entire daily dose into one morning dose
  4. Change to another bupropion formulation immediately
  5. Discontinue bupropion and do not restart it
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Discontinue bupropion and do not restart it

A treatment-associated seizure requires stopping bupropion rather than attempting a lower-dose rechallenge.

Understanding the options

Discontinue bupropion and do not restart it
A treatment-associated seizure requires stopping bupropion rather than attempting a lower-dose rechallenge.
Restart the previous dose after 24 hours
A lower dose does not make a rechallenge appropriate after this event.
Continue bupropion and add prophylactic phenytoin
Adding an antiseizure drug is not a substitute for withdrawing the offending medication.
Combine the entire daily dose into one morning dose
Larger single exposures can increase rather than reduce seizure risk.
Change to another bupropion formulation immediately
Changing formulation does not remove the contraindication created by the seizure.
