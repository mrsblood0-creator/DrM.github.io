Hidden premature P wave

A 58-year-old is referred for apparent intermittent pauses. Careful review shows that each pause begins with a small early deflection deforming the preceding T wave; this deflection is not followed by a QRS. Which explanation best accounts for the pattern?

  1. Sinus arrest with no atrial activity
  2. A ventricular escape rhythm
  3. A sustained atrial flutter circuit
  4. A normal respiratory sinus variation
  5. A blocked premature atrial contraction
Answer and explanation

A blocked premature atrial contraction

A PAC may arrive while the AV node is refractory and fail to conduct; its P wave can be hidden in the T wave.

Option explanations

A blocked premature atrial contraction: A PAC may arrive while the AV node is refractory and fail to conduct; its P wave can be hidden in the T wave.

Sinus arrest with no atrial activity: The premature atrial deflection argues against absence of sinus or atrial activation alone.

A ventricular escape rhythm: Escape rhythms produce delayed ventricular beats.

A sustained atrial flutter circuit: Flutter would show ongoing repetitive atrial activity.

A normal respiratory sinus variation: Respiratory variation changes timing gradually without a hidden premature P wave.
