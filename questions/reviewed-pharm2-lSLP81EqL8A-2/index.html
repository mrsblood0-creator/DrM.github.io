Treating a toxic wide complex

A 33-year-old man has a suspected imipramine overdose. After initial airway assessment and IV access, blood pressure is 78/44 mm Hg and ECG shows a QRS of 160 ms with ventricular ectopy. Which medication best addresses the cardiac sodium-channel toxicity?

  1. Intravenous verapamil
  2. Intravenous adenosine
  3. Intravenous propranolol
  4. Intravenous sodium bicarbonate
  5. Intravenous procainamide
Answer and explanation

Intravenous sodium bicarbonate

Sodium loading and alkalinization are used for clinically important tricyclic sodium-channel blockade, with ECG and acid-base monitoring.

Option explanations

Intravenous sodium bicarbonate: Sodium loading and alkalinization are used for clinically important tricyclic sodium-channel blockade, with ECG and acid-base monitoring.

Intravenous procainamide: Additional sodium-channel blockade can worsen this poisoning.

Intravenous verapamil: It does not reverse tricyclic conduction toxicity and may worsen hemodynamics.

Intravenous adenosine: This is not an AV-node-dependent reentrant supraventricular tachycardia.

Intravenous propranolol: Beta blockade does not correct the toxic conduction defect and may worsen hypotension.
