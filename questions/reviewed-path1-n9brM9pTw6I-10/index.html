Storage proteins as oxidant protection

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

Investigators compare cells containing normal ferritin with cells unable to safely sequester iron. After peroxide exposure, the latter sustain more DNA damage despite similar total iron content. Which property of ferritin best explains this difference?

  1. Sequestration of iron away from the redox-active labile pool
  2. Catalysis of superoxide generation
  3. Direct replacement of damaged DNA bases
  4. Conversion of all peroxide to oxygen
  5. Proteolysis of ubiquitinated proteins
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Sequestration of iron away from the redox-active labile pool

Safely storing iron limits iron-catalyzed radical formation; total iron content alone does not measure the reactive iron pool.

Understanding the options

Sequestration of iron away from the redox-active labile pool
Safely storing iron limits iron-catalyzed radical formation; total iron content alone does not measure the reactive iron pool.
Catalysis of superoxide generation
Ferritin storage limits rather than intentionally drives iron-dependent oxidant damage.
Direct replacement of damaged DNA bases
DNA repair enzymes, not ferritin, replace damaged bases.
Conversion of all peroxide to oxygen
Catalase performs this reaction.
Proteolysis of ubiquitinated proteins
Proteasomes perform ubiquitin-dependent degradation.
