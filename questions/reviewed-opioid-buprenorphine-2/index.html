Risk from a sedative combination

A 45-year-old woman receiving buprenorphine for opioid use disorder takes nonprescribed alprazolam and becomes difficult to awaken with slow breathing. Her respiratory rate is 8/min, and assisted ventilation is initiated. Which interpretation of this event is most accurate?

  1. Alprazolam acts as an opioid antagonist
  2. The combination reliably protects against sedation
  3. Buprenorphine’s only respiratory effect is hyperventilation
  4. Buprenorphine can cause serious respiratory depression, especially with CNS depressants
  5. Partial agonism makes opioid respiratory depression impossible
Answer and explanation

Buprenorphine can cause serious respiratory depression, especially with CNS depressants

Partial agonism does not eliminate respiratory risk, and benzodiazepines increase that risk.

Option explanations

Buprenorphine can cause serious respiratory depression, especially with CNS depressants: Partial agonism does not eliminate respiratory risk, and benzodiazepines increase that risk.

Partial agonism makes opioid respiratory depression impossible: This is a dangerous misconception; serious respiratory depression can occur.

Alprazolam acts as an opioid antagonist: It is a benzodiazepine, not an opioid antagonist.

The combination reliably protects against sedation: The drugs can instead have additive depressant effects.

Buprenorphine’s only respiratory effect is hyperventilation: This contradicts the recognized opioid respiratory toxicity.
