Burkitt lymphoma: 1

A child has a rapidly enlarging abdominal mass composed of mature B cells with a MYC rearrangement and an extremely high proliferation index. Which lymphoma best fits the combined clinical and molecular findings?

  1. MYC activation
  2. t(8;14) involving MYC and the immunoglobulin heavy-chain locus
  3. Tingible-body macrophages among proliferating tumor cells
  4. Tumor lysis syndrome
  5. Burkitt lymphoma
Answer and explanation

Burkitt lymphoma

A rapidly growing mature B-cell lymphoma with MYC rearrangement and a very high proliferative rate fits Burkitt lymphoma.

Option explanations

Burkitt lymphoma: A rapidly growing mature B-cell lymphoma with MYC rearrangement and a very high proliferative rate fits Burkitt lymphoma.

MYC activation: Dysregulated MYC promotes the rapid proliferation characteristic of Burkitt lymphoma. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

t(8;14) involving MYC and the immunoglobulin heavy-chain locus: This is the classic translocation; variant immunoglobulin-locus translocations also occur. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Tingible-body macrophages among proliferating tumor cells: Macrophages containing apoptotic debris create the pale stars in the starry-sky pattern. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Tumor lysis syndrome: Rapid cell breakdown can produce hyperkalemia, hyperphosphatemia, hyperuricemia and kidney injury before or after therapy. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
