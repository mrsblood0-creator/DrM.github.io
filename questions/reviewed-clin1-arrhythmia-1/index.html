Variable tachycardia in a dehydrated child

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A 4-year-old with fever and diarrhea is alert but thirsty. His heart rate varies from 145 to 175/min with crying. ECG shows an upright P wave before every narrow QRS complex. Which approach best addresses the rhythm?

  1. Treat fever and volume depletion while reassessing perfusion
  2. Give adenosine to terminate an AV nodal reentry circuit
  3. Perform immediate synchronized cardioversion
  4. Begin transcutaneous pacing
  5. Give amiodarone to suppress ventricular automaticity
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Treat fever and volume depletion while reassessing perfusion

The variable rate and normal atrial activation support compensatory sinus tachycardia; address its cause.

Understanding the options

Treat fever and volume depletion while reassessing perfusion
The variable rate and normal atrial activation support compensatory sinus tachycardia; address its cause.
Give adenosine to terminate an AV nodal reentry circuit
Adenosine targets selected reentrant tachycardias rather than a physiologic sinus response.
Perform immediate synchronized cardioversion
Electrical conversion does not correct fever or dehydration and is not indicated for this stable sinus rhythm.
Begin transcutaneous pacing
Pacing treats selected bradyarrhythmias, not this tachycardia.
Give amiodarone to suppress ventricular automaticity
There is no wide-complex ventricular rhythm to support this choice.
