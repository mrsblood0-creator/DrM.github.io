Normal chest radiograph anatomy: 3

A healthy adult has a good-quality upright chest radiograph showing the right diaphragmatic dome slightly above the left, with clear lungs and sharp angles. Which interpretation best explains this isolated finding in the stated setting?

  1. Left ventricle
  2. Costophrenic angle
  3. Patient rotation
  4. Normally higher right hemidiaphragm
  5. Right atrium
Answer and explanation

Normally higher right hemidiaphragm

The liver contributes to the normal right hemidiaphragm being somewhat higher than the left.

Option explanations

Normally higher right hemidiaphragm: The liver contributes to the normal right hemidiaphragm being somewhat higher than the left.

Right atrium: The right atrium forms most of the right cardiac border on a standard frontal chest radiograph. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Left ventricle: The left ventricle forms much of the lower left cardiac contour on the frontal radiograph. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Costophrenic angle: The lateral junction of diaphragm and chest wall is normally sharp; blunting may indicate pleural fluid or another abnormality. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Patient rotation: Unequal distances from the medial clavicular heads to the spinous processes suggest rotation and can distort interpretation. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
