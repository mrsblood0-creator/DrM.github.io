Hemodynamic adverse effects

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A 32-year-old man begins amphetamine treatment for ADHD. Two weeks later his resting pulse has increased from 72/min to 101/min and blood pressure from 118/74 to 148/90 mm Hg on repeated measurements. Which mechanism best explains these changes?

  1. Direct blockade of vascular alpha-1 receptors
  2. Selective inhibition of the sinus-node funny current
  3. Increased renal sodium excretion as the primary action
  4. Increased catecholaminergic activity
  5. Reduced peripheral norepinephrine signaling
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Increased catecholaminergic activity

Stimulant effects can increase heart rate and blood pressure; sustained changes require treatment review.

Understanding the options

Increased catecholaminergic activity
Stimulant effects can increase heart rate and blood pressure; sustained changes require treatment review.
Reduced peripheral norepinephrine signaling
Reduced signaling would not explain this sympathetic pattern.
Direct blockade of vascular alpha-1 receptors
Alpha-1 blockade would favor vasodilation and hypotension.
Selective inhibition of the sinus-node funny current
This would slow rather than accelerate the heart rate.
Increased renal sodium excretion as the primary action
Natriuresis is not the explanation for the stimulant hemodynamic effects.
