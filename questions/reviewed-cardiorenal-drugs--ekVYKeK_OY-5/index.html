Common amlodipine effects: 5

A patient asks why amlodipine lowers pressure yet can also produce flushing and ankle edema. Which principal vascular action connects the desired blood-pressure effect with these common adverse effects of the drug?

  1. Palpitations
  2. Arteriolar L-type calcium-channel blockade
  3. Precapillary arteriolar dilation
  4. Vasodilatory flushing
  5. Vasodilatory headache
Answer and explanation

Arteriolar L-type calcium-channel blockade

Amlodipine predominantly relaxes vascular smooth muscle and lowers systemic resistance.

Option explanations

Arteriolar L-type calcium-channel blockade: Amlodipine predominantly relaxes vascular smooth muscle and lowers systemic resistance.

Precapillary arteriolar dilation: Preferential arteriolar dilation increases capillary hydrostatic pressure and can cause dependent edema without primary fluid overload. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Vasodilatory flushing: Cutaneous vasodilation can cause warmth and flushing. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Vasodilatory headache: Headache can occur with vasodilator therapy and must be interpreted in clinical context. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Palpitations: A patient may notice a forceful or rapid heartbeat during vasodilator treatment; persistent symptoms need assessment. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
