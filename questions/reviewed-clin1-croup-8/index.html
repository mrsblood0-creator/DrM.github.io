Recognizing an atypical age

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A 2-month-old has recurrent episodes labeled croup, persistent noisy breathing between illnesses and poor response to standard treatment. He is stable today. Which next step is most appropriate rather than repeatedly assuming ordinary viral croup?

  1. Assess for an underlying structural airway disorder
  2. Continue treating each episode as ordinary croup without interval assessment
  3. Start a long-term asthma controller without upper-airway assessment
  4. Prescribe repeated empiric antibiotics without investigating the airway
  5. Reassure based only on normal oxygen saturation today
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Assess for an underlying structural airway disorder

Very young age, recurrence and interval symptoms warrant evaluation for an underlying airway abnormality.

Understanding the options

Assess for an underlying structural airway disorder
Very young age, recurrence and interval symptoms warrant evaluation for an underlying airway abnormality.
Continue treating each episode as ordinary croup without interval assessment
Very young age, interval symptoms and poor response are atypical and warrant evaluation.
Start a long-term asthma controller without upper-airway assessment
Persistent stridor points to upper-airway pathology rather than automatically to asthma.
Prescribe repeated empiric antibiotics without investigating the airway
Antibiotics do not establish or correct an underlying structural cause.
Reassure based only on normal oxygen saturation today
Current stability does not explain the recurrent atypical history or remove the need for evaluation.
