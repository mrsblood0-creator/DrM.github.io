Symptom relief does not settle the diagnosis

A 64-year-old man has taken antacids most days for three months. They briefly relieve burning, but he now reports progressive difficulty swallowing solids and unintentional weight loss. Which conclusion best guides the next step?

  1. Temporary relief does not exclude serious disease and further evaluation is needed
  2. Improvement with antacids confirms uncomplicated reflux
  3. The response proves the symptoms are caused by excess acid secretion alone
  4. Switching antacid salts removes the need to investigate swallowing difficulty
  5. Escalating antacid frequency is sufficient if relief lasts several minutes
Answer and explanation

Temporary relief does not exclude serious disease and further evaluation is needed

Antacids relieve symptoms without establishing the cause; progressive dysphagia and weight loss require evaluation.

Option explanations

Temporary relief does not exclude serious disease and further evaluation is needed: The new features cannot be explained away by temporary symptom relief.

Improvement with antacids confirms uncomplicated reflux: A response to neutralization is not a diagnostic test for uncomplicated reflux.

The response proves the symptoms are caused by excess acid secretion alone: Acid-related relief does not exclude structural or malignant disease.

Switching antacid salts removes the need to investigate swallowing difficulty: Changing salts does not evaluate a progressive swallowing problem.

Escalating antacid frequency is sufficient if relief lasts several minutes: Increasing symptomatic treatment could delay investigation of important findings.
