Intermittent symptoms with normal ECG

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A 36-year-old describes brief palpitations several times daily, but a clinic ECG records normal sinus rhythm. The clinician suspects intermittent atrial ectopy and wants to document the rhythm during symptoms. Which investigation is most directly useful?

  1. A single serum sodium measurement
  2. Carotid ultrasound
  3. Ambulatory ECG recording
  4. Routine coronary angiography
  5. A resting chest radiograph alone
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Ambulatory ECG recording

Ambulatory ECG can capture intermittent ectopy and link it to symptoms.

Understanding the options

Ambulatory ECG recording
Ambulatory ECG can capture intermittent ectopy and link it to symptoms.
Routine coronary angiography
Angiography is not the first method for documenting intermittent palpitations without ischemic features.
A resting chest radiograph alone
Radiography cannot identify transient atrial activation.
A single serum sodium measurement
Sodium measurement alone does not document the rhythm.
Carotid ultrasound
Carotid imaging does not capture electrical events.
