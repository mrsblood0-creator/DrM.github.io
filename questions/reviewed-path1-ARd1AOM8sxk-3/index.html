Purulent tissue destruction

A febrile patient has a bacterial lung abscess. Microscopy shows abundant neutrophils and a liquefied center in which normal alveolar architecture is no longer recognizable. Which source most directly contributes to the enzymatic digestion of the involved tissue?

  1. Deposited immunoglobulin light-chain fibrils
  2. Accumulated neutral triglyceride droplets
  3. Dystrophic calcium alone
  4. Hydrolytic enzymes from inflammatory cells and injured tissue
  5. Newly synthesized type I collagen
Answer and explanation

Hydrolytic enzymes from inflammatory cells and injured tissue

Enzymatic digestion by leukocytes and damaged cells produces liquefaction and purulent debris.

Option explanations

Hydrolytic enzymes from inflammatory cells and injured tissue: Enzymatic digestion by leukocytes and damaged cells produces liquefaction and purulent debris.

Newly synthesized type I collagen: Collagen deposition organizes repair rather than creating pus.

Deposited immunoglobulin light-chain fibrils: Amyloid deposition is a protein accumulation process.

Accumulated neutral triglyceride droplets: Neutral lipid accumulation produces steatosis, not an abscess cavity.

Dystrophic calcium alone: Calcium deposition does not account for enzymatic liquefaction.
