Macrocytosis after prolonged acid suppression

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A 74-year-old patient who has received prolonged proton pump inhibitor therapy develops macrocytosis and distal sensory symptoms. Testing confirms vitamin B12 deficiency, and dietary intake is adequate. Which mechanism can contribute to this deficiency during sustained acid suppression?

  1. Chelation of B12 by calcium carbonate in the PPI
  2. Reduced release of food-bound vitamin B12 in a less acidic stomach
  3. Direct destruction of intrinsic-factor-producing cells in every patient
  4. Increased urinary B12 secretion
  5. Selective inhibition of folate reductase
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Reduced release of food-bound vitamin B12 in a less acidic stomach

Hypochlorhydria can impair release of B12 from food; other causes of deficiency still require evaluation.

Understanding the options

Reduced release of food-bound vitamin B12 in a less acidic stomach
Hypochlorhydria can impair release of B12 from food; other causes of deficiency still require evaluation.
Direct destruction of intrinsic-factor-producing cells in every patient
PPI use does not inevitably cause autoimmune parietal-cell destruction.
Increased urinary B12 secretion
Renal wasting is not the principal mechanism.
Selective inhibition of folate reductase
Folate reductase inhibition is a different drug effect.
Chelation of B12 by calcium carbonate in the PPI
PPIs are not calcium carbonate antacids.
