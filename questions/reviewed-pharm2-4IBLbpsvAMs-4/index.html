Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A 41-year-old man develops difficulty initiating urination and a weak stream after an atomoxetine dose increase. Urinalysis shows no infection, and there are no new neurologic findings. Which interpretation is most appropriate?

  1. Evidence that atomoxetine blocks dopamine D2 receptors
  2. A recognized urinary adverse effect of atomoxetine
  3. Definite bacterial cystitis despite the assessment
  4. A harmless finding that never requires evaluation
  5. Proof of irreversible spinal cord injury
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

A recognized urinary adverse effect of atomoxetine

Urinary hesitancy or retention can occur and should prompt assessment and review of the drug contribution.

Understanding the options

A recognized urinary adverse effect of atomoxetine
Urinary hesitancy or retention can occur and should prompt assessment and review of the drug contribution.
Definite bacterial cystitis despite the assessment
The supplied findings do not establish infection.
A harmless finding that never requires evaluation
Retention can be clinically significant and warrants review.
Proof of irreversible spinal cord injury
The timing and lack of neurologic findings do not support this conclusion.
Evidence that atomoxetine blocks dopamine D2 receptors
Atomoxetine is a norepinephrine reuptake inhibitor, not a D2 antagonist.
