Pneumothorax on chest radiographs: 2

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A supine trauma patient has an unusually deep and lucent lateral costophrenic region rather than a conspicuous apical pleural line. Pleural air is suspected in this position. Which named radiographic sign describes the finding?

  1. Immediate clinical treatment of suspected tension pneumothorax
  2. Skin-fold artifact
  3. Mediastinal displacement away from the pressurized hemithorax
  4. Deep sulcus sign
  5. Visceral pleural line with absent peripheral lung markings
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Deep sulcus sign

In a supine patient, pleural air can collect anterobasally and make a costophrenic sulcus unusually deep and lucent.

Understanding the options

Deep sulcus sign
In a supine patient, pleural air can collect anterobasally and make a costophrenic sulcus unusually deep and lucent.
Visceral pleural line with absent peripheral lung markings
Pleural air separates the visceral pleura from the chest wall, leaving no lung vessels peripheral to the true pleural line. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
Immediate clinical treatment of suspected tension pneumothorax
An unstable patient with a convincing tension pattern needs urgent decompression by a trained team; imaging must not delay treatment. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
Skin-fold artifact
A skin fold can mimic a pleural line, but visible pulmonary markings beyond the apparent edge favor an artifact. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
Mediastinal displacement away from the pressurized hemithorax
Pressure from a large or tension pneumothorax can push mediastinal structures contralaterally; clinical instability determines urgency. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
