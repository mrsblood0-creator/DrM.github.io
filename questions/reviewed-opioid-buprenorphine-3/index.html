The antagonist in a combination product

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A patient asks why an opioid antagonist is included in buprenorphine/naloxone sublingual treatment. The clinician explains that route of administration matters. In a person dependent on a full opioid agonist, which effect is associated with parenteral misuse of this combination?

  1. Naloxone prevents all possible misuse by every route
  2. Naloxone irreversibly inhibits CYP3A4
  3. Naloxone can precipitate opioid withdrawal
  4. Naloxone increases mu-receptor intrinsic activity
  5. Naloxone converts buprenorphine into a full agonist
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Naloxone can precipitate opioid withdrawal

Parenteral naloxone can antagonize opioid receptors and provoke withdrawal.

Understanding the options

Naloxone can precipitate opioid withdrawal
Parenteral naloxone can antagonize opioid receptors and provoke withdrawal.
Naloxone increases mu-receptor intrinsic activity
Naloxone is an antagonist rather than an agonist enhancer.
Naloxone converts buprenorphine into a full agonist
It does not change buprenorphine’s intrinsic activity.
Naloxone prevents all possible misuse by every route
The combination does not eliminate every form of misuse.
Naloxone irreversibly inhibits CYP3A4
Its relevant action is receptor antagonism, not this metabolic effect.
