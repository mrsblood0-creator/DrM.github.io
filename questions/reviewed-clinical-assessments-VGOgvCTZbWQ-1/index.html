Epilepsy principles: 1

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A patient has two unprovoked seizures separated by several weeks, without an acute reversible trigger for either event. Which established diagnostic route supports epilepsy rather than treating the events as a single transient provoked episode?

  1. Acute symptomatic seizure
  2. Focal seizure onset
  3. Convulsive status epilepticus requiring emergency treatment
  4. High predicted recurrence risk can establish epilepsy after one seizure
  5. Epilepsy after two unprovoked seizures more than 24 hours apart
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Epilepsy after two unprovoked seizures more than 24 hours apart

This is one route to the ILAE clinical definition; other routes include sufficiently high recurrence risk after one seizure or a defined epilepsy syndrome.

Understanding the options

Epilepsy after two unprovoked seizures more than 24 hours apart
This is one route to the ILAE clinical definition; other routes include sufficiently high recurrence risk after one seizure or a defined epilepsy syndrome.
Acute symptomatic seizure
A seizure during a transient acute metabolic insult does not by itself establish epilepsy. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
Focal seizure onset
Initial signs arising from a limited network in one hemisphere support focal onset, even if bilateral convulsions later occur. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
Convulsive status epilepticus requiring emergency treatment
An ongoing bilateral tonic-clonic seizure at five minutes warrants emergency treatment; do not wait for a prolonged historical threshold. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
High predicted recurrence risk can establish epilepsy after one seizure
The ILAE definition allows one unprovoked seizure with a recurrence risk of at least 60% over ten years in an appropriate assessment. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
