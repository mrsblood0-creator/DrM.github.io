Congenital long QT syndrome

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A 19-year-old woman with genetically confirmed congenital long QT syndrome is assessed before surgery. She has normal electrolytes and has never had nausea-related dehydration. Which proposed postoperative antiemetic warrants avoidance specifically because it can further prolong ventricular repolarization?

  1. Scopolamine
  2. A nonpharmacologic nausea intervention
  3. Ondansetron
  4. Aprepitant
  5. Dexamethasone
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Ondansetron

The ondansetron label advises avoidance in congenital long QT syndrome, even without current electrolyte depletion.

Understanding the options

Ondansetron
The ondansetron label advises avoidance in congenital long QT syndrome, even without current electrolyte depletion.
Aprepitant
NK1 antagonism does not carry this particular ondansetron warning.
Dexamethasone
Dexamethasone is not selected for direct QT prolongation here.
Scopolamine
Scopolamine's key concerns are antimuscarinic effects.
A nonpharmacologic nausea intervention
A nonpharmacologic intervention has no direct drug-induced QT effect.
