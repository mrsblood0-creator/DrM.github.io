A large apparent volume

A drug has an apparent volume of distribution of 500 L in a 70-kg adult. A student argues that this is impossible because total body water is much smaller. Which explanation best resolves the apparent contradiction?

  1. The drug must be completely unabsorbed
  2. Extensive tissue binding can make plasma concentrations very low
  3. The patient must contain 500 L of extracellular fluid
  4. The drug cannot leave the bloodstream
  5. The laboratory must always have made an error
Answer and explanation

Extensive tissue binding can make plasma concentrations very low

Vd is a proportionality constant, not a literal anatomical fluid volume.

Option explanations

Extensive tissue binding can make plasma concentrations very low: Vd is a proportionality constant, not a literal anatomical fluid volume.

The patient must contain 500 L of extracellular fluid: The value does not represent a physical compartment.

The drug cannot leave the bloodstream: Plasma confinement generally gives a small Vd.

The laboratory must always have made an error: A large apparent Vd can be pharmacologically plausible.

The drug must be completely unabsorbed: The measured body amount and plasma concentration concern systemic distribution.
