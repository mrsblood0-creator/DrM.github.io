A negative screening biopsy

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A 70-year-old woman has unexplained ventricular thickening, low electrocardiographic voltage, and progressive heart failure. An abdominal fat aspirate contains no amyloid, but clinical suspicion remains high. Which interpretation is most appropriate?

  1. A negative fat aspirate does not exclude amyloidosis
  2. All systemic amyloidosis has been excluded
  3. Only hereditary amyloidosis has been excluded
  4. The result proves hypertensive heart disease
  5. Amyloid is always visible in every tissue
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

A negative fat aspirate does not exclude amyloidosis

Deposits can be patchy and screening-biopsy sensitivity varies by amyloid type. Further subtype-appropriate evaluation or involved-organ biopsy may be needed.

Understanding the options

A negative fat aspirate does not exclude amyloidosis
Deposits can be patchy and screening-biopsy sensitivity varies by amyloid type. Further subtype-appropriate evaluation or involved-organ biopsy may be needed.
All systemic amyloidosis has been excluded
A screening sample can miss deposits.
Only hereditary amyloidosis has been excluded
Fat sampling does not selectively exclude inherited forms.
The result proves hypertensive heart disease
A negative biopsy does not establish a competing diagnosis.
Amyloid is always visible in every tissue
Distribution and burden differ between tissues and patients.
