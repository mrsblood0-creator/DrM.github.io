Symptoms that change the assessment

A 45-year-old previously had occasional asymptomatic PACs. He now reports exertional fainting and prolonged rapid palpitations. A relative died suddenly at a young age. Which response is most appropriate despite the prior reassuring evaluation?

  1. Treat with anticoagulation without documenting the rhythm
  2. Reassess for sustained arrhythmia and underlying cardiac disease
  3. Attribute all new symptoms to harmless PACs
  4. Stop all evaluation because a past echocardiogram was normal
  5. Reassure solely because PACs originate above the ventricles
Answer and explanation

Reassess for sustained arrhythmia and underlying cardiac disease

New syncope, sustained symptoms and family history require renewed evaluation.

Option explanations

Reassess for sustained arrhythmia and underlying cardiac disease: New syncope, sustained symptoms and family history require renewed evaluation.

Attribute all new symptoms to harmless PACs: A previous low-risk diagnosis does not explain every later symptom.

Stop all evaluation because a past echocardiogram was normal: Prior normal findings do not override new warning features.

Reassure solely because PACs originate above the ventricles: Atrial origin alone does not make the entire presentation benign.

Treat with anticoagulation without documenting the rhythm: Treatment should follow a documented diagnosis and indication.
