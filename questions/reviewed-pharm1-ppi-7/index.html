Kidney injury after a new PPI

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A 55-year-old woman develops an unexplained increase in creatinine several weeks after starting omeprazole. Urinalysis abnormalities prompt evaluation, and acute tubulointerstitial nephritis is diagnosed. There is no evidence of urinary obstruction. Which interpretation best reflects the recognized drug association?

  1. PPIs cause only predictable dose-dependent glomerular vasoconstriction
  2. Normal prior renal function excludes a drug reaction
  3. The diagnosis requires simultaneous aluminum toxicity
  4. The kidney injury proves H. pylori eradication
  5. A PPI can trigger immune-mediated interstitial kidney injury
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

A PPI can trigger immune-mediated interstitial kidney injury

Acute tubulointerstitial nephritis is a recognized PPI-associated adverse reaction and requires prompt drug review.

Understanding the options

A PPI can trigger immune-mediated interstitial kidney injury
Acute tubulointerstitial nephritis is a recognized PPI-associated adverse reaction and requires prompt drug review.
PPIs cause only predictable dose-dependent glomerular vasoconstriction
This is not simply predictable hemodynamic vasoconstriction.
Normal prior renal function excludes a drug reaction
Hypersensitivity injury can occur despite previously normal kidneys.
The diagnosis requires simultaneous aluminum toxicity
Aluminum toxicity is not required.
The kidney injury proves H. pylori eradication
Renal injury says nothing about bacterial eradication.
