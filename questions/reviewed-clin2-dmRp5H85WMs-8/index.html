A reversible contributor

A 63-year-old with diarrhea develops new palpitations. Monitoring identifies PACs, and potassium is 2.9 mmol/L. He is otherwise stable with no sustained tachycardia. Which action most directly addresses a reversible contributor to the ectopy?

  1. Start anticoagulation before reassessment
  2. Perform immediate AV-node ablation
  3. Assume the electrolyte result is irrelevant
  4. Correct the electrolyte deficit and evaluate its cause
  5. Administer a potassium-wasting diuretic
Answer and explanation

Correct the electrolyte deficit and evaluate its cause

Electrolyte disturbances can contribute to ectopy and should be corrected safely.

Option explanations

Correct the electrolyte deficit and evaluate its cause: Electrolyte disturbances can contribute to ectopy and should be corrected safely.

Administer a potassium-wasting diuretic: Further potassium loss could aggravate the problem.

Start anticoagulation before reassessment: PACs alone do not require anticoagulation.

Perform immediate AV-node ablation: Ablation is not an initial response to a reversible biochemical problem.

Assume the electrolyte result is irrelevant: The abnormal result is clinically relevant.
