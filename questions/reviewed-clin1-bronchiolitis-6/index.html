Feeding support in respiratory illness

A 6-month-old with bronchiolitis cannot take enough milk because feeding increases respiratory effort. He is alert, has no impending respiratory failure and has no contraindication to enteral feeding. Which route is a reasonable first option for hydration support?

  1. Nasogastric or orogastric fluids
  2. Repeated full oral feeds despite increased respiratory effort
  3. Central venous parenteral nutrition
  4. Routine intravenous fluid boluses without signs of shock
  5. Maintenance fluids restricted to half requirements without reassessment
Answer and explanation

Nasogastric or orogastric fluids

Tube feeding can maintain hydration when oral intake is inadequate and enteral support is tolerated.

Option explanations

Nasogastric or orogastric fluids: Tube feeding can maintain hydration when oral intake is inadequate and enteral support is tolerated.

Repeated full oral feeds despite increased respiratory effort: Feeding stress and inadequate intake make enteral tube support preferable to forcing oral feeds.

Central venous parenteral nutrition: This is invasive and unnecessary when the gastrointestinal tract can be used.

Routine intravenous fluid boluses without signs of shock: Boluses treat shock or selected deficits; the described need is hydration support in a stable infant.

Maintenance fluids restricted to half requirements without reassessment: Fluid needs require clinical assessment; an arbitrary restriction can worsen dehydration.
