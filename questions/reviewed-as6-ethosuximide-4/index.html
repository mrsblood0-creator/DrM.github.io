An important adverse-effect history

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A 12-year-old girl previously developed severe immune thrombocytopenia that was attributed to ethosuximide. Her new clinician reviews options for recurrent absence seizures. Which prescribing decision best reflects the significance of this documented adverse reaction?

  1. Avoid re-exposure to ethosuximide
  2. Retry ethosuximide at a higher dose
  3. Ignore the history if platelets are now normal
  4. Give ethosuximide only with aspirin
  5. Use platelet counts to prove an immediate rechallenge is harmless
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Avoid re-exposure to ethosuximide

Drug-induced immune thrombocytopenia can recur rapidly on re-exposure; a documented reaction is a reason to avoid the drug.

Understanding the options

Avoid re-exposure to ethosuximide
Drug-induced immune thrombocytopenia can recur rapidly on re-exposure; a documented reaction is a reason to avoid the drug.
Retry ethosuximide at a higher dose
Higher dosing does not prevent an immune recurrence.
Ignore the history if platelets are now normal
Recovery does not remove the risk of re-exposure.
Give ethosuximide only with aspirin
Aspirin can worsen bleeding risk and does not prevent the immune reaction.
Use platelet counts to prove an immediate rechallenge is harmless
Monitoring cannot make a known dangerous rechallenge harmless.
