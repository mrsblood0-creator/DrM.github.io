ORIGINAL USMLE-STYLE PRACTICE

Effect outlasts plasma exposure

In a pharmacology study, volunteers receive a single dose of omeprazole. Plasma drug concentrations fall within hours, but gastric acid secretion remains suppressed well after circulating concentrations have declined. Which property best explains the mismatch between plasma half-life and antisecretory duration?

  1. Permanent destruction of gastric parietal cells
  2. Continuous neutralization by a luminal calcium salt
  3. Long-lived blockade of all vagal neurons
  4. Indefinite storage of unchanged drug in blood
  5. Irreversible pump inhibition requiring recovery of functional pumps
Answer and explanation

Irreversible pump inhibition requiring recovery of functional pumps

Acid secretion recovers as functional pumps become available, rather than tracking only plasma drug concentration.

Option explanations

Irreversible pump inhibition requiring recovery of functional pumps: Acid secretion recovers as functional pumps become available, rather than tracking only plasma drug concentration.

Permanent destruction of gastric parietal cells: Parietal cells are not permanently destroyed.

Continuous neutralization by a luminal calcium salt: Antacids act by luminal neutralization.

Long-lived blockade of all vagal neurons: PPIs do not ablate vagal neurons.

Indefinite storage of unchanged drug in blood: The drug does not remain indefinitely in circulation.
