Choosing antibiotics: 1

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A chart says penicillin allergy, but the patient recalls only nausea years earlier without rash, breathing difficulty or mucosal injury. Before excluding an entire antibiotic class, which part of the prescribing assessment is most useful?

  1. Clarify the allergy reaction and timing
  2. Drug penetration at the infection site
  3. Renal function and individualized dosing
  4. Narrow therapy using reliable microbiology
  5. Source control
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Clarify the allergy reaction and timing

A detailed allergy history distinguishes intolerance from immediate or severe delayed hypersensitivity and informs safe selection.

Understanding the options

Clarify the allergy reaction and timing

Drug penetration at the infection site
An agent must reach effective concentrations where the infection is located; an in-vitro susceptible result alone is insufficient. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
Renal function and individualized dosing
Renal impairment can alter antimicrobial exposure and require dose or interval adjustment. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
Narrow therapy using reliable microbiology
Once the pathogen and susceptibilities are known, an effective narrower regimen may reduce unnecessary exposure. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
Source control
An abscess, infected device or obstructed infected system may require drainage, removal or relief in addition to antibiotics. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
