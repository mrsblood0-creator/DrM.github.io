Pethidine and meperidine: 2

A patient receiving repeated meperidine develops tremor and seizures. Renal function is impaired, and an excitatory metabolite has accumulated. Which mechanism best explains this toxicity rather than uncomplicated opioid-induced sedation alone?

  1. Precipitated opioid withdrawal
  2. QT prolongation and torsades risk
  3. Normeperidine neurotoxicity
  4. Support ventilation and obtain emergency care
  5. Recurrent toxicity after antagonist effect wanes
Answer and explanation

Normeperidine neurotoxicity

Meperidine’s metabolite can accumulate and cause CNS excitation or seizures, especially with renal impairment.

Option explanations

Normeperidine neurotoxicity: Meperidine’s metabolite can accumulate and cause CNS excitation or seizures, especially with renal impairment.

Support ventilation and obtain emergency care: Airway and breathing support accompany overdose reversal; naloxone does not replace resuscitation. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Recurrent toxicity after antagonist effect wanes: Many opioids last longer than naloxone, so repeat dosing and monitoring may be needed. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Precipitated opioid withdrawal: An antagonist or high-affinity partial agonist can abruptly reduce receptor stimulation in a dependent patient. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

QT prolongation and torsades risk: Methadone can prolong repolarization; interacting medicines and electrolyte abnormalities increase concern. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
