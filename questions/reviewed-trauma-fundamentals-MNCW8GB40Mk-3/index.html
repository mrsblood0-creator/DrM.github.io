Orthopedic trauma history and examination: 3

Before reducing a deformed limb, the clinician records pulses, perfusion, sensation and motor function, then repeats the findings after splinting. Which documentation principle helps identify both pre-existing and new deficits around treatment?

  1. Complete the broader trauma assessment
  2. Document a neurovascular examination
  3. Clarify mechanism and energy of injury
  4. Inspect the skin for an open injury
  5. Repeat examinations when symptoms evolve
Answer and explanation

Document a neurovascular examination

Motor, sensory and perfusion findings should be recorded before and after interventions.

Option explanations

Document a neurovascular examination: Motor, sensory and perfusion findings should be recorded before and after interventions.

Clarify mechanism and energy of injury: Mechanism helps predict associated injury, instability and the need for broader assessment. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Inspect the skin for an open injury: A small communicating wound can make a fracture open even without visible exposed bone. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Repeat examinations when symptoms evolve: Swelling, pain and neurologic findings can change; a normal initial examination does not end surveillance. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Complete the broader trauma assessment: A dramatic limb injury must not distract from other life-threatening injuries. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
