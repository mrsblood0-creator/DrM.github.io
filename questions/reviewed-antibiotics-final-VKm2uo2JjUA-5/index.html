Chloramphenicol: 5

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A clinician considers chloramphenicol for a susceptible infection but identifies a safer effective alternative. The patient has no special reason to require chloramphenicol. Which prescribing principle best reflects the drug’s important hematologic safety limitations?

  1. Reserve use when safer effective alternatives are unsuitable
  2. Immature neonatal chloramphenicol elimination
  3. Reversible dose-related marrow suppression
  4. Unpredictable aplastic anemia
  5. Monitor blood counts and clinical toxicity
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Reserve use when safer effective alternatives are unsuitable

Chloramphenicol toxicity limits its use despite broad antimicrobial activity.

Understanding the options

Reserve use when safer effective alternatives are unsuitable
Chloramphenicol toxicity limits its use despite broad antimicrobial activity.
Immature neonatal chloramphenicol elimination
Limited neonatal metabolism and elimination can cause chloramphenicol accumulation and gray baby syndrome. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
Reversible dose-related marrow suppression
Chloramphenicol can suppress marrow in an exposure-related fashion, distinct from rare idiosyncratic aplastic anemia. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
Unpredictable aplastic anemia
Rare severe marrow failure with chloramphenicol is not reliably prevented by keeping ordinary drug levels therapeutic. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
Monitor blood counts and clinical toxicity
Blood-count monitoring helps detect some toxicity but does not guarantee prevention of idiosyncratic aplastic anemia. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
