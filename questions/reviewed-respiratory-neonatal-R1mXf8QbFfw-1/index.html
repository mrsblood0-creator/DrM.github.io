Neonatal cyanosis: 1

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A newborn has blue discoloration involving the tongue and oral mucosa as well as the skin. The finding persists during assessment. Which category of cyanosis requires investigation for systemic desaturation rather than attributing it only to cold extremities?

  1. Duct-dependent congenital heart disease
  2. Compare right-hand and foot oxygen saturations
  3. Further clinical assessment and echocardiography when indicated
  4. Central cyanosis
  5. Peripheral acrocyanosis
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Central cyanosis

Blue discoloration of the tongue or oral mucosa suggests systemic arterial desaturation or an abnormal hemoglobin state and needs urgent evaluation.

Understanding the options

Central cyanosis
Blue discoloration of the tongue or oral mucosa suggests systemic arterial desaturation or an abnormal hemoglobin state and needs urgent evaluation.
Peripheral acrocyanosis
Bluish hands and feet with a pink tongue can reflect peripheral circulation rather than central hypoxemia; the overall clinical state still matters. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
Duct-dependent congenital heart disease
Deterioration as the ductus closes can signal critical congenital heart disease requiring urgent specialist assessment and ductal support when indicated. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
Compare right-hand and foot oxygen saturations
Right-hand measurement is preductal and a foot measurement is postductal; a difference can help assess ductal shunting but is not diagnostic by itself. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
Further clinical assessment and echocardiography when indicated
A passed newborn oxygen screen does not exclude every important heart defect; new symptoms require assessment despite a previous pass. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
