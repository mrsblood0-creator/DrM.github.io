Renal impairment and drug exposure

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A 76-year-old patient with diabetic gastroparesis has substantially reduced creatinine clearance. After receiving a usual adult metoclopramide regimen, she develops marked restlessness and drowsiness. Which prescribing adjustment best addresses the pharmacokinetic risk?

  1. Add a dopamine antagonist routinely
  2. Maintain the dose because renal function is irrelevant
  3. Use indefinite treatment without reassessment
  4. Reduce dosage to account for impaired clearance
  5. Increase dosage to overcome urinary loss
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Reduce dosage to account for impaired clearance

Metoclopramide exposure increases with renal impairment; the label recommends dose reduction according to indication and renal function.

Understanding the options

Reduce dosage to account for impaired clearance
Metoclopramide exposure increases with renal impairment; the label recommends dose reduction according to indication and renal function.
Increase dosage to overcome urinary loss
Impaired clearance promotes accumulation rather than excess urinary loss.
Add a dopamine antagonist routinely
Another dopamine antagonist increases neurologic risk.
Maintain the dose because renal function is irrelevant
Renal function materially affects dosing.
Use indefinite treatment without reassessment
Ongoing need and neurologic toxicity require reassessment.
