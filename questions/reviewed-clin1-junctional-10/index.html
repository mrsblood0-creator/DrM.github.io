Narrow QRS physiology

A medical student asks why a junctional rhythm often has narrow QRS complexes despite beginning outside the sinus node. There is no pre-existing bundle-branch block. Which conduction pathway best explains the ventricular activation pattern?

  1. Entry into the His–Purkinje system with coordinated ventricular depolarization
  2. Slow cell-to-cell spread beginning in ventricular muscle
  3. Activation through a right ventricular pacing lead
  4. Complete electrical isolation of the ventricles from the conduction system
  5. Random activation of isolated ventricular regions
Answer and explanation

Entry into the His–Purkinje system with coordinated ventricular depolarization

A junctional impulse can use the normal rapid ventricular conduction system, preserving a narrow QRS.

Option explanations

Entry into the His–Purkinje system with coordinated ventricular depolarization: A junctional impulse can use the normal rapid ventricular conduction system, preserving a narrow QRS.

Slow cell-to-cell spread beginning in ventricular muscle: Myocardial cell-to-cell activation usually broadens ventricular depolarization.

Activation through a right ventricular pacing lead: No pacing device is described.

Complete electrical isolation of the ventricles from the conduction system: Isolation from the conduction system would not explain rapid coordinated activation.

Random activation of isolated ventricular regions: Disorganized regional activation would not produce a regular narrow QRS pattern.
