Selecting an initial oral antibiotic

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A fully immunized 4-year-old is diagnosed with uncomplicated bacterial community-acquired pneumonia. He is stable for outpatient treatment and has no beta-lactam allergy or risk factors for resistant organisms. Which oral agent is generally first line for typical pneumococcal disease?

  1. Amoxicillin
  2. Azithromycin
  3. Ciprofloxacin
  4. Clindamycin
  5. Amoxicillin–clavulanate
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Amoxicillin

Amoxicillin provides appropriate narrow-spectrum coverage for common typical bacterial pneumonia in this setting.

Understanding the options

Amoxicillin
Amoxicillin provides appropriate narrow-spectrum coverage for common typical bacterial pneumonia in this setting.
Azithromycin
A macrolide targets atypical pathogens and is not the routine first choice for typical pneumococcal disease in this uncomplicated setting.
Ciprofloxacin
Its spectrum and pediatric stewardship considerations make it inappropriate as routine first-line treatment here.
Clindamycin
This may be useful for selected resistant gram-positive infections or allergies but is broader than necessary for the stated case.
Amoxicillin–clavulanate
Broader beta-lactamase coverage is not routinely required for an uncomplicated typical pneumococcal infection without added risk factors.
