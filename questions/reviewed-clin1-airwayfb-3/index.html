Unilateral hyperinflation

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A child with suspected peanut aspiration has a hyperlucent left lung that remains expanded on expiration. The mediastinum shifts slightly rightward. Which mechanism best explains this radiographic pattern in the affected lung?

  1. Ball-valve obstruction permitting inspiration more than expiration
  2. Complete obstruction with absorption of distal gas
  3. Diffuse alveolar filling by pus
  4. Compression from a large pleural effusion
  5. Reduced surfactant production throughout both lungs
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Ball-valve obstruction permitting inspiration more than expiration

Partial bronchial obstruction may admit air during inspiration but impede its exit, causing unilateral air trapping.

Understanding the options

Ball-valve obstruction permitting inspiration more than expiration
Partial bronchial obstruction may admit air during inspiration but impede its exit, causing unilateral air trapping.
Complete obstruction with absorption of distal gas
Complete occlusion more often causes distal resorption atelectasis.
Diffuse alveolar filling by pus
Alveolar pus increases opacity rather than hyperlucency.
Compression from a large pleural effusion
A pleural effusion produces fluid opacity and compresses lung tissue.
Reduced surfactant production throughout both lungs
A bilateral surfactant disorder does not explain a focal postchoking pattern.
