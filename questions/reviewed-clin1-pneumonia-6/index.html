Persistent fever and pleural fluid

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A 7-year-old being treated for bacterial pneumonia remains febrile and develops increasing unilateral chest pain. Breath sounds are reduced over the lower hemithorax, and percussion is dull. Which complication should be assessed first?

  1. Parapneumonic effusion or empyema
  2. Pneumothorax
  3. Right-to-left intracardiac shunting
  4. Postinfectious bronchial hyperreactivity
  5. Acute pulmonary embolism
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Parapneumonic effusion or empyema

Persistent fever with dullness and reduced breath sounds suggests infected or inflammatory pleural fluid.

Understanding the options

Parapneumonic effusion or empyema
Persistent fever with dullness and reduced breath sounds suggests infected or inflammatory pleural fluid.
Pneumothorax
Pleural air usually causes hyperresonance rather than dullness to percussion.
Right-to-left intracardiac shunting
A shunt may cause hypoxemia but does not explain new focal chest pain and dullness during pneumonia.
Postinfectious bronchial hyperreactivity
This generally causes wheeze rather than a focal pleural examination with persistent fever.
Acute pulmonary embolism
Pleuritic pain can occur, but the infectious course and dullness with reduced basal breath sounds point to pleural fluid.
