Unreliable examination

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A 29-year-old intoxicated patient is brought in after a high-speed collision. He denies neck pain but cannot sustain attention or give a consistent history. There is no immediately obvious limb weakness. Why is the absence of reported pain insufficient to clear the spine clinically?

  1. Pain is never relevant to spine assessment
  2. A normal pulse excludes occult spinal trauma
  3. Intoxication makes the examination unreliable
  4. All intoxicated patients necessarily have a cervical fracture
  5. Normal limb movement guarantees ligamentous stability
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Intoxication makes the examination unreliable

A reliable examination is required for clinical clearance; the negative symptom report cannot be used alone.

Understanding the options

Intoxication makes the examination unreliable
A reliable examination is required for clinical clearance; the negative symptom report cannot be used alone.
All intoxicated patients necessarily have a cervical fracture
Intoxication does not prove injury; it limits reliable exclusion.
Normal limb movement guarantees ligamentous stability
Gross movement does not establish absence of injury.
Pain is never relevant to spine assessment
Midline pain or tenderness is relevant when assessment is reliable.
A normal pulse excludes occult spinal trauma
Pulse does not determine spinal integrity.
