After successful rescue

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

During induction for an elective operation, a supraglottic airway restores oxygenation after failed tracheal intubation. Oxygen saturation is now 98%, ventilation is effective, and the surgeon has not begun the procedure. What should the airway team do next?

  1. Stop monitoring because oxygenation has improved
  2. Reassess the plan, including whether to awaken the patient or proceed with an appropriate alternative strategy
  3. Repeat unlimited intubation attempts immediately
  4. Assume aspiration risk is now absent
  5. Remove the effective device before preparing any alternative
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Reassess the plan, including whether to awaken the patient or proceed with an appropriate alternative strategy

Successful rescue creates time to make a deliberate plan; it does not automatically require proceeding with surgery.

Understanding the options

Reassess the plan, including whether to awaken the patient or proceed with an appropriate alternative strategy
Successful rescue creates time to make a deliberate plan; it does not automatically require proceeding with surgery.
Repeat unlimited intubation attempts immediately
Repeated attempts can cause trauma and convert a manageable airway into a worse situation.
Assume aspiration risk is now absent
A rescue device does not erase aspiration risk.
Remove the effective device before preparing any alternative
This risks losing a functioning ventilation route.
Stop monitoring because oxygenation has improved
Continued monitoring is essential.
