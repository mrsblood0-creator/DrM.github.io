Bone pain during prolonged aluminum use

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A 49-year-old man with a low-phosphate diet has used high doses of aluminum hydroxide for months. He develops proximal muscle weakness and diffuse bone pain. Testing shows hypophosphatemia with preserved renal function. Which mechanism best links the antacid to these findings?

  1. Binding dietary phosphate in the intestinal lumen
  2. Blocking vitamin B12 binding to intrinsic factor
  3. Increasing renal phosphate reabsorption
  4. Stimulating intestinal phosphate transport
  5. Increasing synthesis of calcitriol
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Binding dietary phosphate in the intestinal lumen

Aluminum binds phosphate in the gut, reducing absorption and potentially depleting body phosphate.

Understanding the options

Binding dietary phosphate in the intestinal lumen
Luminal phosphate binding explains reduced phosphate availability.
Blocking vitamin B12 binding to intrinsic factor
B12 malabsorption does not directly explain the low phosphate.
Increasing renal phosphate reabsorption
Increased renal reabsorption would conserve phosphate.
Stimulating intestinal phosphate transport
Increased intestinal transport would raise phosphate absorption.
Increasing synthesis of calcitriol
Calcitriol generally supports intestinal phosphate absorption.
