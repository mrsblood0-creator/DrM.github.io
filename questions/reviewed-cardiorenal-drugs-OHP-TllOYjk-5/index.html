Osmotic diuretics: 5

A patient with severe renal dysfunction cannot effectively excrete administered mannitol. The team is concerned about retained osmotic solute and worsening circulatory overload. Which physiological complication is particularly relevant to this loss of elimination?

  1. Hyponatremia
  2. Metabolic acidosis from bicarbonate loss
  3. Expansion of intravascular volume
  4. Hypokalemia
  5. Hyperkalemia
Answer and explanation

Expansion of intravascular volume

Mannitol can initially draw water into the circulation and worsen pulmonary edema in susceptible patients.

Option explanations

Expansion of intravascular volume: Mannitol can initially draw water into the circulation and worsen pulmonary edema in susceptible patients.

Hypokalemia: Loop and thiazide diuretics can increase distal potassium loss. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Hyperkalemia: Potassium-sparing diuretics can raise potassium, especially with renal impairment or other potassium-raising medicines. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Hyponatremia: Thiazides can impair urinary dilution and cause clinically important hyponatremia. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Metabolic acidosis from bicarbonate loss: Acetazolamide promotes bicarbonaturia and can cause hyperchloremic metabolic acidosis. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
