FSGS: 1

A patient with proteinuria has a biopsy showing scarring in portions of some glomerular tufts, while other glomeruli appear unaffected. Which histopathologic diagnosis best describes this combination of distribution and extent of injury?

  1. IgA nephropathy
  2. Infection-related glomerulonephritis
  3. Focal segmental glomerulosclerosis
  4. Minimal change disease
  5. Membranous nephropathy
Answer and explanation

Focal segmental glomerulosclerosis

FSGS is a pattern of segmental scarring affecting only some glomeruli; primary, adaptive and genetic causes must be distinguished.

Option explanations

Focal segmental glomerulosclerosis: FSGS is a pattern of segmental scarring affecting only some glomeruli; primary, adaptive and genetic causes must be distinguished.

Minimal change disease: Childhood nephrotic syndrome with little change on light microscopy and diffuse foot-process effacement suggests minimal change disease. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Membranous nephropathy: Subepithelial immune deposits and capillary-wall thickening characterize membranous nephropathy. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

IgA nephropathy: Mesangial IgA deposition commonly presents with hematuria rather than the specified classic nephrotic patterns. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Infection-related glomerulonephritis: An inflammatory nephritic presentation after infection differs from a purely nephrotic podocyte pattern. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
