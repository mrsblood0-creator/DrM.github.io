Focal respiratory findings with fever

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A previously healthy 5-year-old has fever, cough and right-sided abdominal pain. Respiratory rate is 42/min. Examination shows reduced air entry and focal crackles at the right base, with no abdominal guarding. Which diagnosis best unifies the findings?

  1. Right lower-lobe pneumonia
  2. Acute appendicitis
  3. Viral gastroenteritis
  4. Acute cystitis
  5. Intussusception
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Right lower-lobe pneumonia

Lower-lobe inflammation can cause referred abdominal pain; tachypnea and focal lung findings identify the likely source.

Understanding the options

Right lower-lobe pneumonia
Lower-lobe inflammation can cause referred abdominal pain; tachypnea and focal lung findings identify the likely source.
Acute appendicitis
Appendicitis does not explain focal crackles and reduced basal air entry.
Viral gastroenteritis
There is no diarrhea or vomiting pattern, and focal respiratory findings require explanation.
Acute cystitis
Urinary infection would not explain the localized chest abnormalities.
Intussusception
Intussusception typically causes episodic abdominal symptoms rather than this febrile focal respiratory pattern.
