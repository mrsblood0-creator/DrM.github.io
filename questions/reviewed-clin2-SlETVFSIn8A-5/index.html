A 52-year-old presents with fatigue, constipation, cold intolerance and recent weight gain. His pulse is 48/min with a regular sinus rhythm. He has dry skin and delayed relaxation of ankle reflexes. Which test best evaluates a reversible cause of the slow rate?

  1. Plasma renin activity alone
  2. Thyroid-stimulating hormone and free thyroxine
  3. Serum metanephrines
  4. Morning aldosterone concentration alone
  5. Serum tryptase
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Thyroid-stimulating hormone and free thyroxine

The clinical constellation suggests hypothyroidism, a reversible cause of sinus slowing.

Understanding the options

Thyroid-stimulating hormone and free thyroxine
The clinical constellation suggests hypothyroidism, a reversible cause of sinus slowing.
Serum metanephrines
Catecholamine excess usually causes tachycardia and episodic hypertension.
Morning aldosterone concentration alone
Aldosterone testing does not assess this symptom pattern.
Serum tryptase
Tryptase evaluates mast-cell activation rather than chronic slowing with cold intolerance.
Plasma renin activity alone
Renin alone does not explain the hypothyroid features.
