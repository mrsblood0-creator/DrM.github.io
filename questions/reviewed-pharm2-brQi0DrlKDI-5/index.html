Reconsidering a pediatric prescription

A parent requests the same prescription codeine cough syrup previously used by an adult household member for a school-age child with a viral respiratory illness. Which safety concern most strongly argues against prescribing this opioid cough product?

  1. Unpredictable respiratory depression in children
  2. Inevitable permanent tooth discoloration
  3. Rapid suppression of thyroid hormone synthesis
  4. Universal immune-mediated renal failure
  5. Direct destruction of bronchial cartilage
Answer and explanation

Unpredictable respiratory depression in children

Serious pediatric opioid toxicity led to restrictions on prescription opioid cough products.

Option explanations

Unpredictable respiratory depression in children: Serious pediatric opioid toxicity led to restrictions on prescription opioid cough products.

Inevitable permanent tooth discoloration: Tooth discoloration is not the characteristic codeine hazard.

Rapid suppression of thyroid hormone synthesis: Codeine is not an antithyroid drug.

Universal immune-mediated renal failure: Renal failure is not an inevitable response to codeine.

Direct destruction of bronchial cartilage: Cartilage destruction does not explain pediatric restrictions.
