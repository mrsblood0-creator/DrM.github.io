ORIGINAL USMLE-STYLE PRACTICE

Urgent reperfusion

A 52-year-old man has persistent ischemic chest pain and new diagnostic ST elevations in contiguous leads. Symptoms began 90 minutes ago, and a PCI-capable team can provide timely intervention. Which strategy directly addresses the occluded coronary artery?

  1. Schedule a routine stress test during ongoing STEMI
  2. Prompt primary percutaneous coronary intervention
  3. Wait until Q waves form before acting
  4. Treat only with an oral antacid
  5. Delay reperfusion until troponin reaches its peak
Answer and explanation

Prompt primary percutaneous coronary intervention

Timely reperfusion is central to STEMI management; primary PCI opens the culprit artery.

Option explanations

Prompt primary percutaneous coronary intervention: Timely reperfusion is central to STEMI management; primary PCI opens the culprit artery.

Wait until Q waves form before acting: Waiting delays myocardial salvage.

Treat only with an oral antacid: This does not address coronary occlusion.

Delay reperfusion until troponin reaches its peak: Biomarker peak is not a prerequisite for STEMI reperfusion.

Schedule a routine stress test during ongoing STEMI: Stress testing is not the acute treatment of an occluded artery.
