ORIGINAL USMLE-STYLE PRACTICE

Leakage distinguishes a destructive process

A tissue specimen after a toxic insult shows clusters of swollen cells with ruptured membranes. Neutrophils accumulate nearby even though no microorganisms are identified. Which event most directly connects this necrotic injury to the inflammatory-cell recruitment?

  1. Regulated increase in cell size
  2. Release of intracellular damage-associated molecules
  3. Sequestration of contents within apoptotic bodies
  4. Exposure of phosphatidylserine without membrane rupture
  5. Inhibition of all extracellular signaling
Answer and explanation

Release of intracellular damage-associated molecules

Necrotic membrane disruption exposes intracellular molecules that activate innate inflammatory responses.

Option explanations

Release of intracellular damage-associated molecules: Necrotic membrane disruption exposes intracellular molecules that activate innate inflammatory responses.

Sequestration of contents within apoptotic bodies: Containment in apoptotic bodies generally limits inflammatory stimulation.

Exposure of phosphatidylserine without membrane rupture: Phosphatidylserine exposure promotes orderly phagocytosis but does not explain rupture-related leakage.

Inhibition of all extracellular signaling: The infiltrate indicates activation, not complete inhibition, of extracellular signaling.

Regulated increase in cell size: Hypertrophy does not require membrane rupture or necrotic inflammation.
