Rifaximin molecular target

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A patient with recurrent hepatic encephalopathy receives an oral rifamycin that acts predominantly in the intestine. In a bacterial culture, the drug prevents formation of new RNA strands without directly binding ribosomes. Which target best explains this effect?

  1. DNA-dependent RNA polymerase
  2. 30S ribosomal subunit
  3. 50S ribosomal subunit
  4. DNA gyrase
  5. Penicillin-binding proteins
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

DNA-dependent RNA polymerase

Blocking bacterial transcription reduces RNA synthesis and downstream protein production.

Understanding the options

DNA-dependent RNA polymerase
Blocking bacterial transcription reduces RNA synthesis and downstream protein production.
30S ribosomal subunit
Aminoglycosides and tetracyclines act at this ribosomal subunit.
50S ribosomal subunit
Macrolides and several other antibiotics inhibit translation here.
DNA gyrase
Fluoroquinolones inhibit bacterial topoisomerases rather than transcription directly.
Penicillin-binding proteins
Beta-lactams inhibit peptidoglycan cross-linking through these proteins.
