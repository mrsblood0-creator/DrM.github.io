Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A tennis player develops ulnar-sided wrist pain after a twisting injury. Forearm rotation and ulnar loading reproduce symptoms, and specialist assessment identifies a tear in the fibrocartilaginous structure stabilizing the distal radioulnar joint. Which structure is injured?

  1. Perilunate dislocation
  2. Lunate dislocation
  3. Triangular fibrocartilage complex injury
  4. Scaphoid fracture
  5. Scapholunate ligament injury
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Triangular fibrocartilage complex injury

The TFCC supports the ulnar wrist and distal radioulnar joint; injury can produce ulnar-sided pain and pain with rotation.

Understanding the options

Triangular fibrocartilage complex injury
The TFCC supports the ulnar wrist and distal radioulnar joint; injury can produce ulnar-sided pain and pain with rotation.
Scaphoid fracture
Focal snuffbox or scaphoid-tubercle tenderness after a fall raises concern for scaphoid fracture even when initial films are negative. This is not the pattern specified in this case.
Scapholunate ligament injury
Scapholunate dissociation widens the interval between the scaphoid and lunate and can cause dorsal wrist pain or instability. This is not the pattern specified in this case.
Perilunate dislocation
The lunate remains aligned with the radius while the capitate and remaining carpus are displaced, commonly dorsally. This is not the pattern specified in this case.
Lunate dislocation
The lunate itself displaces, typically volarly, losing its normal relationship with the distal radius and capitate. This is not the pattern specified in this case.
