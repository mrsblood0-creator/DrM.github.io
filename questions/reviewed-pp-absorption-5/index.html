Reduced perfusion at an injection site

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A patient in circulatory shock receives a drug into skeletal muscle. Its systemic effect is delayed compared with the same dose in a well-perfused volunteer. Which change most directly accounts for the slower absorption from this administration site?

  1. Increased renal clearance before absorption
  2. An increase in systemic receptor number
  3. Instantaneous equilibration with all tissues
  4. Reduced local blood flow
  5. Complete elimination of all first-pass metabolism
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Reduced local blood flow

Poor perfusion slows transfer of an intramuscular dose into systemic circulation.

Understanding the options

Reduced local blood flow
Poor perfusion slows transfer of an intramuscular dose into systemic circulation.
Complete elimination of all first-pass metabolism
Avoidance of first-pass metabolism does not explain delayed uptake from muscle.
Increased renal clearance before absorption
Renal clearance acts after the drug reaches the circulation.
An increase in systemic receptor number
Receptor number changes response, not local drug absorption.
Instantaneous equilibration with all tissues
Instantaneous distribution would not explain the delay.
