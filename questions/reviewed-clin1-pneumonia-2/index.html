Disposition of a child with hypoxemia

A 3-year-old with pneumonia has marked chest retractions, poor oral intake and persistent oxygen saturation of 86% on room air using an appropriately placed probe. She is becoming fatigued. Which next step is most appropriate?

  1. Hospital treatment with oxygen and assessment for respiratory support
  2. Outpatient oral antibiotics with review in 48 hours
  3. Observation without oxygen until blood gas confirmation
  4. A bronchodilator trial followed by discharge if wheeze improves
  5. Intravenous antibiotics while deferring respiratory support
Answer and explanation

Hospital treatment with oxygen and assessment for respiratory support

Persistent severe hypoxemia, respiratory distress and impaired intake require monitored inpatient care and prompt support.

Option explanations

Hospital treatment with oxygen and assessment for respiratory support: Persistent severe hypoxemia, respiratory distress and impaired intake require monitored inpatient care and prompt support.

Outpatient oral antibiotics with review in 48 hours: Severe hypoxemia and fatigue make outpatient treatment unsafe despite the availability of oral antibiotics.

Observation without oxygen until blood gas confirmation: A reliable saturation of 86% with respiratory distress warrants oxygen immediately; a blood gas should not delay it.

A bronchodilator trial followed by discharge if wheeze improves: Pneumonia with fatigue and persistent severe hypoxemia requires monitored care, even if coexisting wheeze responds.

Intravenous antibiotics while deferring respiratory support: Antimicrobials do not immediately correct hypoxemia or impending ventilatory failure.
