Foam cells in an arterial plaque

A coronary atherosclerotic plaque contains many macrophages with foamy cytoplasm. Adjacent extracellular needle-shaped clefts mark material removed during processing. Which substance most directly accounts for both the macrophage lipid accumulation and the extracellular clefts?

  1. Bilirubin
  2. Cholesterol and cholesterol esters
  3. Glycogen
  4. Melanin
  5. Immunoglobulin light chains
Answer and explanation

Cholesterol and cholesterol esters

Macrophages can accumulate cholesterol esters; dissolved cholesterol crystals leave clefts during tissue processing.

Option explanations

Cholesterol and cholesterol esters: Macrophages can accumulate cholesterol esters; dissolved cholesterol crystals leave clefts during tissue processing.

Glycogen: Glycogen does not produce cholesterol clefts.

Melanin: Melanin produces pigment rather than a lipid-rich atheroma.

Immunoglobulin light chains: Light chains may form extracellular amyloid, not the characteristic lipid clefts.

Bilirubin: Bilirubin does not account for foam cells in atherosclerosis.
