Radiographic lag after clinical recovery

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A 9-year-old treated for uncomplicated pneumonia is afebrile, active and eating normally. Cough is resolving and oxygen saturation is normal. A family member asks whether another chest radiograph is needed before school return. Which response is most appropriate?

  1. No routine repeat imaging if clinical recovery remains satisfactory
  2. Repeat the radiograph before returning to school
  3. Extend antibiotics until radiographic consolidation clears
  4. Obtain CT to exclude residual structural damage
  5. Arrange bronchoscopy to document complete resolution
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

No routine repeat imaging if clinical recovery remains satisfactory

Clinical improvement usually guides recovery; routine repeat radiographs are not needed after uncomplicated disease.

Understanding the options

No routine repeat imaging if clinical recovery remains satisfactory
Clinical improvement usually guides recovery; routine repeat radiographs are not needed after uncomplicated disease.
Repeat the radiograph before returning to school
School return is based on clinical recovery and relevant public health advice, not mandatory radiographic clearing.
Extend antibiotics until radiographic consolidation clears
Imaging may lag behind improvement; persistent opacity alone does not require prolonged treatment.
Obtain CT to exclude residual structural damage
CT is not routinely warranted after uncomplicated clinical recovery.
Arrange bronchoscopy to document complete resolution
Invasive airway assessment is not indicated in this recovered child without recurrent focal disease or other concern.
