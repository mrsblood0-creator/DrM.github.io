An NSAID-associated ulcer

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A 67-year-old man taking daily naproxen for arthritis develops epigastric pain and an endoscopically confirmed gastric ulcer. H. pylori testing is negative. Which medication mechanism contributes to loss of normal mucosal protection?

  1. Selective neutralization of all gastric acid
  2. Reduced prostaglandin synthesis from cyclooxygenase inhibition
  3. Direct eradication of gastric bacteria
  4. Increased mucosal bicarbonate protection
  5. Permanent blockade of acid secretion
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Reduced prostaglandin synthesis from cyclooxygenase inhibition

NSAIDs impair prostaglandin-supported mucosal defenses.

Understanding the options

Reduced prostaglandin synthesis from cyclooxygenase inhibition
NSAIDs impair prostaglandin-supported mucosal defenses.
Direct eradication of gastric bacteria
Eradication is not the mechanism of NSAID injury.
Increased mucosal bicarbonate protection
That would protect rather than weaken the mucosa.
Permanent blockade of acid secretion
Naproxen is not an acid-suppressing drug.
Selective neutralization of all gastric acid
NSAIDs do not act as antacids.
