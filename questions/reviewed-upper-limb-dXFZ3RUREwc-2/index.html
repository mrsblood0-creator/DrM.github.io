Inferior shoulder dislocation: 2

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

Following an inferior shoulder dislocation, sensation over the lateral shoulder is reduced and deltoid activation is weak. Distal hand movement remains present. Which nerve best accounts for this specific motor and sensory pattern?

  1. Radial nerve
  2. Ulnar nerve
  3. Median nerve
  4. Musculocutaneous nerve
  5. Axillary nerve
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Axillary nerve

The axillary nerve supplies deltoid and sensation over the lateral shoulder; it is vulnerable with shoulder dislocation or proximal humeral injury.

Understanding the options

Axillary nerve
The axillary nerve supplies deltoid and sensation over the lateral shoulder; it is vulnerable with shoulder dislocation or proximal humeral injury.
Radial nerve
The radial nerve supplies wrist and finger extensors; humeral shaft injury can cause wrist drop and dorsal first-web-space sensory loss. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
Ulnar nerve
The ulnar nerve passes behind the medial epicondyle and supplies interossei and sensation to the little finger. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
Median nerve
Median nerve injury can impair thumb opposition and sensation in the radial palmar digits. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
Musculocutaneous nerve
This nerve supplies major elbow flexors and sensation over the lateral forearm. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
