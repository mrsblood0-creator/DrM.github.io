Black pigment in draining nodes

A long-term city resident undergoes examination of otherwise preserved lung tissue. Black particles are present in alveolar macrophages and hilar lymph nodes without destructive fibrosis or an iron-stain reaction. What is the most likely source of the pigment?

  1. Breakdown of local erythrocytes
  2. Accumulated bile pigment
  3. Retained immunoglobulin
  4. Oxidized mitochondrial lipid alone
  5. Inhaled carbonaceous material
Answer and explanation

Inhaled carbonaceous material

Inhaled carbon can be taken up by pulmonary macrophages and transported to draining lymph nodes, producing anthracotic pigment.

Option explanations

Inhaled carbonaceous material: Inhaled carbon can be taken up by pulmonary macrophages and transported to draining lymph nodes, producing anthracotic pigment.

Breakdown of local erythrocytes: Hemorrhage produces iron-containing hemosiderin rather than carbon particles.

Accumulated bile pigment: Bile pigment does not preferentially follow this inhalational pathway.

Retained immunoglobulin: Immunoglobulin forms protein inclusions in producing cells.

Oxidized mitochondrial lipid alone: Lipofuscin is yellow-brown residual pigment rather than exogenous black particles.
