Barbiturates: 4

An intravenous lipophilic barbiturate produces brief anesthesia after a single bolus. Consciousness returns before most of the dose has been eliminated from the body. Which pharmacokinetic process best explains the early termination of the effect?

  1. Increased frequency of GABA-A channel opening
  2. Hepatic enzyme induction
  3. Precipitation of acute porphyria
  4. Redistribution from brain to other tissues
  5. Prolonged GABA-A chloride-channel opening
Answer and explanation

Redistribution from brain to other tissues

A brief effect after an IV lipophilic anesthetic bolus can end through redistribution before elimination is complete.

Option explanations

Redistribution from brain to other tissues: A brief effect after an IV lipophilic anesthetic bolus can end through redistribution before elimination is complete.

Prolonged GABA-A chloride-channel opening: Barbiturates classically increase the duration of GABA-A channel opening and can produce profound depression at high exposure. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Increased frequency of GABA-A channel opening: Benzodiazepines increase opening frequency in the presence of GABA. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Hepatic enzyme induction: Phenobarbital induces drug-metabolizing enzymes and can reduce exposure to susceptible coadministered medicines. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Precipitation of acute porphyria: Barbiturates can provoke attacks in susceptible acute hepatic porphyrias. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
