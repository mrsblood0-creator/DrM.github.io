Buspirone: 2

A patient takes the first buspirone dose during an acute episode of anxiety and expects relief within minutes. The clinician explains why regular treatment is needed before assessing benefit. Which pharmacologic feature is being emphasized?

  1. Delayed anxiolytic onset
  2. Additive sedation and impaired alertness
  3. Complex sleep behavior
  4. Residual next-day impairment
  5. Additive CNS and respiratory depression
Answer and explanation

Delayed anxiolytic onset

Buspirone usually requires repeated dosing over weeks and is not an immediate rescue medicine for a panic episode.

Option explanations

Delayed anxiolytic onset: Buspirone usually requires repeated dosing over weeks and is not an immediate rescue medicine for a panic episode.

Additive sedation and impaired alertness: Combining melatonin with other sedating substances can increase drowsiness and impairment; review all medicines and alcohol exposure. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Complex sleep behavior: Sleepwalking, sleep-driving or other activities without full awareness can occur with Z-drugs and require discontinuation and clinical review. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Residual next-day impairment: Sedative effects may persist after awakening and impair driving even when the patient feels awake. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Additive CNS and respiratory depression: Combining sedatives with opioids or alcohol increases risk of profound sedation and respiratory depression. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
