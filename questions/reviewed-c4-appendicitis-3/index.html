An inconclusive score

A 27-year-old woman has right lower quadrant tenderness, anorexia and nausea but no clear peritonitis. Her clinical risk score places her in an intermediate-risk group. Pregnancy testing is negative. What is the best interpretation of this score?

  1. It eliminates the need to consider gynecologic causes
  2. It makes reassessment unnecessary if symptoms worsen
  3. It supports further evaluation rather than proving or excluding appendicitis
  4. It proves appendicitis with complete certainty
  5. It excludes appendicitis permanently
Answer and explanation

It supports further evaluation rather than proving or excluding appendicitis

Clinical scores stratify risk; intermediate probability commonly warrants observation and imaging.

Option explanations

It supports further evaluation rather than proving or excluding appendicitis: Clinical scores stratify risk; intermediate probability commonly warrants observation and imaging.

It proves appendicitis with complete certainty: A score alone does not establish the diagnosis.

It excludes appendicitis permanently: Intermediate risk cannot exclude disease.

It eliminates the need to consider gynecologic causes: Alternative diagnoses remain important.

It makes reassessment unnecessary if symptoms worsen: Worsening symptoms require reassessment.
