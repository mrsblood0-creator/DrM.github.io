Recognizing ventricular ectopy

A 43-year-old feels an occasional skipped beat. ECG shows an early broad QRS complex lasting 150 ms, without a preceding P wave, followed by a discordant T wave and a pause. Which origin best accounts for this isolated premature complex?

  1. Ectopic ventricular depolarization
  2. Early sinus-node discharge
  3. Atrial depolarization with normal conduction
  4. Respiratory modulation of sinus rate
  5. Failure of a sinus impulse to leave the node
Answer and explanation

Ectopic ventricular depolarization

The early wide complex without a preceding atrial impulse is typical of a PVC.

Option explanations

Ectopic ventricular depolarization: The early wide complex without a preceding atrial impulse is typical of a PVC.

Early sinus-node discharge: A sinus impulse normally has a preceding sinus P wave.

Atrial depolarization with normal conduction: Normally conducted atrial ectopy usually produces a narrow QRS.

Respiratory modulation of sinus rate: Respiratory variation changes intervals without generating a separate broad ectopic beat.

Failure of a sinus impulse to leave the node: Sinus exit failure produces a pause, not an early broad complex.
