Delayed cavity after stroke

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

An adult has a cerebral infarction after an arterial occlusion. Weeks later, the infarcted region has become a cystic cavity with loss of its original tissue architecture rather than persistent solid ghost outlines. Which process best explains this evolution?

  1. Protein denaturation that preserves cell outlines
  2. Calcium soap formation in adipose tissue
  3. Immune-complex deposition confined to vessel walls
  4. Granulomatous encapsulation of caseous debris
  5. Hydrolytic digestion of dead tissue
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Hydrolytic digestion of dead tissue

Brain infarcts undergo liquefactive necrosis, in which enzymatic digestion removes the dead tissue.

Understanding the options

Hydrolytic digestion of dead tissue
Brain infarcts undergo liquefactive necrosis, in which enzymatic digestion removes the dead tissue.
Protein denaturation that preserves cell outlines
Architecture-preserving denaturation characterizes coagulative necrosis.
Calcium soap formation in adipose tissue
Calcium soaps form in fat necrosis rather than an ordinary cerebral infarct.
Immune-complex deposition confined to vessel walls
Vessel-wall injury does not describe the parenchymal cavity.
Granulomatous encapsulation of caseous debris
Caseating granulomas are not the expected response to a simple ischemic stroke.
