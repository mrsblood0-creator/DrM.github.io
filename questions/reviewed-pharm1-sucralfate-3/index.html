An antibiotic loses bioavailability

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A 38-year-old patient takes oral ciprofloxacin together with every dose of sucralfate. Despite verified adherence and an organism susceptible to ciprofloxacin, antibiotic exposure is lower than expected. Renal function has not changed. Which interaction best accounts for the reduced systemic concentration?

  1. Inhibition of renal secretion increases elimination
  2. Displacement from albumin permanently inactivates the drug
  3. Increased bacterial synthesis of folate
  4. Binding within the gastrointestinal lumen reduces absorption
  5. Induction of hepatic CYP3A4 accelerates clearance
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Binding within the gastrointestinal lumen reduces absorption

Sucralfate can bind other drugs in the gut and reduce bioavailability; drug-specific separation instructions are important.

Understanding the options

Binding within the gastrointestinal lumen reduces absorption
Sucralfate can bind other drugs in the gut and reduce bioavailability; drug-specific separation instructions are important.
Induction of hepatic CYP3A4 accelerates clearance
The interaction is primarily local rather than enzyme induction.
Inhibition of renal secretion increases elimination
Inhibiting secretion would reduce, not increase, renal clearance.
Displacement from albumin permanently inactivates the drug
Albumin displacement does not permanently inactivate ciprofloxacin.
Increased bacterial synthesis of folate
Bacterial folate synthesis does not explain low plasma exposure.
