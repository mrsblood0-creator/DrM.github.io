Diuretic foundations: 5

A patient combines two potassium-sparing medicines and uses a potassium-containing salt substitute. The clinician orders an electrolyte check. Which abnormality is the principal concern created by these combined influences on potassium balance?

  1. Contraction-associated metabolic alkalosis
  2. Hyperkalemia
  3. Hypokalemia
  4. Hyponatremia
  5. Metabolic acidosis from bicarbonate loss
Answer and explanation

Hyperkalemia

Potassium-sparing diuretics can raise potassium, especially with renal impairment or other potassium-raising medicines.

Option explanations

Hyperkalemia: Potassium-sparing diuretics can raise potassium, especially with renal impairment or other potassium-raising medicines.

Hypokalemia: Loop and thiazide diuretics can increase distal potassium loss. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Hyponatremia: Thiazides can impair urinary dilution and cause clinically important hyponatremia. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Metabolic acidosis from bicarbonate loss: Acetazolamide promotes bicarbonaturia and can cause hyperchloremic metabolic acidosis. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Contraction-associated metabolic alkalosis: Volume and chloride depletion with loop or thiazide treatment can promote metabolic alkalosis. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
