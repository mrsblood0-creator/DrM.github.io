Purpose of preoxygenation

A 64-year-old with pneumonia requires urgent intubation. Before induction, the team administers a high inspired oxygen concentration while maintaining a well-fitting interface. The resident asks why this step matters even when pulse oximetry has improved. Which physiologic goal is most important?

  1. Increase the oxygen reserve available during apnea
  2. Eliminate the need for a rescue airway plan
  3. Prevent all aspiration of gastric contents
  4. Provide unconsciousness before paralysis
  5. Ensure that carbon dioxide cannot rise
Answer and explanation

Increase the oxygen reserve available during apnea

Preoxygenation replaces alveolar nitrogen with oxygen and extends the reserve during apnea.

Option explanations

Increase the oxygen reserve available during apnea: Preoxygenation replaces alveolar nitrogen with oxygen and extends the reserve during apnea.

Eliminate the need for a rescue airway plan: A rescue plan remains essential.

Prevent all aspiration of gastric contents: Oxygen delivery does not seal the esophagus or prevent regurgitation.

Provide unconsciousness before paralysis: Oxygen is not an induction agent.

Ensure that carbon dioxide cannot rise: Carbon dioxide can still accumulate during apnea.
