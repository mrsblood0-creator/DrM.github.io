A reassuring saturation

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A 49-year-old man receiving supplemental oxygen becomes increasingly drowsy after procedural sedation. His saturation remains 98%, but respiratory rate is 5/min and chest excursions are shallow. Which interpretation should guide the immediate assessment?

  1. A low respiratory rate is harmless when oxygen is administered
  2. The saturation excludes upper-airway obstruction
  3. The reading proves that protective reflexes are intact
  4. Normal oxygen saturation does not exclude inadequate ventilation
  5. Normal saturation establishes adequate carbon dioxide elimination
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Normal oxygen saturation does not exclude inadequate ventilation

Supplemental oxygen can preserve saturation despite hypoventilation. Breathing effort, ventilation and airway protection need immediate assessment.

Understanding the options

Normal oxygen saturation does not exclude inadequate ventilation
Supplemental oxygen can preserve saturation despite hypoventilation. Breathing effort, ventilation and airway protection need immediate assessment.
Normal saturation establishes adequate carbon dioxide elimination
Pulse oximetry measures oxygenation, not carbon dioxide clearance.
A low respiratory rate is harmless when oxygen is administered
Oxygen does not correct inadequate ventilation or loss of airway protection.
The saturation excludes upper-airway obstruction
Partial or developing obstruction may precede desaturation, especially with oxygen.
The reading proves that protective reflexes are intact
Protective reflexes cannot be inferred from pulse oximetry.
