Bone healing sequence: 5

A fracture is anatomically reduced and held with absolute stability. Healing progresses through osteonal reconstruction with little external callus, unlike typical secondary healing. Which healing mode best explains the limited callus despite successful union?

  1. Remodeling
  2. Direct bone healing
  3. Fracture hematoma and inflammation
  4. Soft callus formation
  5. Hard callus formation
Answer and explanation

Direct bone healing

Absolute stability and close apposition can permit osteonal healing with little visible external callus.

Option explanations

Direct bone healing: Absolute stability and close apposition can permit osteonal healing with little visible external callus.

Fracture hematoma and inflammation: Vascular injury produces a hematoma and an early inflammatory repair response. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Soft callus formation: Fibrocartilaginous tissue initially bridges the fracture before substantial mineralization. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Hard callus formation: Woven bone replaces the soft callus and increases structural continuity. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Remodeling: Woven bone is reorganized toward stronger lamellar architecture according to loading. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
