Nocturnal pauses

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A 61-year-old is referred after a sleep monitor recorded sinus slowing and brief pauses overnight. He has loud snoring, witnessed apneas and daytime somnolence but no daytime syncope. Which evaluation should be prioritized before assuming that he needs a permanent pacemaker?

  1. Assessment for obstructive sleep apnea
  2. Electrophysiologic ablation of the sinus node
  3. Coronary angiography solely for the pauses
  4. Routine permanent pacing based only on the nocturnal rate
  5. Exercise restriction without further assessment
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Assessment for obstructive sleep apnea

Sleep-disordered breathing can produce nocturnal bradyarrhythmia and should be evaluated.

Understanding the options

Assessment for obstructive sleep apnea
Sleep-disordered breathing can produce nocturnal bradyarrhythmia and should be evaluated.
Electrophysiologic ablation of the sinus node
Sinus-node ablation would worsen slowing.
Coronary angiography solely for the pauses
No ischemic features are provided to justify angiography on this basis alone.
Routine permanent pacing based only on the nocturnal rate
Nocturnal bradycardia alone is not a permanent pacing indication.
Exercise restriction without further assessment
Avoidance of activity does not address the likely sleep-related mechanism.
