Frequent ectopy and follow-up

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A 69-year-old has frequent PACs on ambulatory monitoring. No atrial fibrillation is captured. He asks whether this means he already has atrial fibrillation and must start anticoagulation. Which explanation is most accurate?

  1. PACs are distinct from atrial fibrillation and may justify further rhythm assessment
  2. Any PAC proves atrial fibrillation is present
  3. PACs alone always require anticoagulation
  4. PACs eliminate the possibility of future atrial fibrillation
  5. PACs are a form of complete heart block
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

PACs are distinct from atrial fibrillation and may justify further rhythm assessment

Frequent atrial ectopy can be a marker of atrial disease or future arrhythmia, but it does not itself diagnose AF.

Understanding the options

PACs are distinct from atrial fibrillation and may justify further rhythm assessment
Frequent atrial ectopy can be a marker of atrial disease or future arrhythmia, but it does not itself diagnose AF.
Any PAC proves atrial fibrillation is present
An isolated premature impulse is not sustained fibrillatory atrial activity.
PACs alone always require anticoagulation
Anticoagulation requires an appropriate clinical indication, not PACs alone.
PACs eliminate the possibility of future atrial fibrillation
PACs do not exclude future AF.
PACs are a form of complete heart block
The mechanism is ectopic atrial firing, not complete conduction failure.
