Electric-shock facial pain

A 59-year-old man has recurrent seconds-long bursts of unilateral electric-shock pain in the cheek, triggered by shaving and chewing. Neurologic examination is otherwise normal. Carbamazepine is prescribed. Which condition explains this use outside epilepsy?

  1. Bell palsy
  2. Temporal arteritis
  3. Myasthenia gravis
  4. Parkinson disease
  5. Trigeminal neuralgia
Answer and explanation

Trigeminal neuralgia

Brief triggered paroxysms in a trigeminal distribution are characteristic of trigeminal neuralgia.

Option explanations

Trigeminal neuralgia: Brief triggered paroxysms in a trigeminal distribution are characteristic of trigeminal neuralgia.

Bell palsy: Bell palsy causes facial weakness rather than triggered shock-like pain.

Temporal arteritis: This usually causes persistent headache and ischemic symptoms rather than this stereotyped pattern.

Myasthenia gravis: Myasthenia causes fatigable weakness, not electric-shock pain.

Parkinson disease: The hallmark is a movement disorder, not paroxysmal facial pain.
