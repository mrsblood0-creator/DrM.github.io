A magnesium-related complication

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A patient taking a PPI for a prolonged period develops muscle cramps and palpitations. Laboratory testing shows marked hypomagnesemia without an obvious acute gastrointestinal loss. Which medication-related problem should be considered in the evaluation?

  1. PPI-associated hypomagnesemia
  2. PPI-induced magnesium overload
  3. Direct calcium chelation by an aluminum antacid
  4. Isolated vitamin K deficiency
  5. Acute hyperprolactinemia
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

PPI-associated hypomagnesemia

Rare but significant hypomagnesemia is reported during prolonged PPI therapy and may require reassessing treatment.

Understanding the options

PPI-associated hypomagnesemia
Rare but significant hypomagnesemia is reported during prolonged PPI therapy and may require reassessing treatment.
PPI-induced magnesium overload
The finding is depletion, not overload.
Direct calcium chelation by an aluminum antacid
The patient is taking a PPI, not an aluminum-binding product.
Isolated vitamin K deficiency
Vitamin K deficiency explains bleeding rather than magnesium depletion.
Acute hyperprolactinemia
Prolactin does not explain this electrolyte disorder.
