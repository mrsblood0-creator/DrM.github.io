CNS pharmacology foundations: 1

A patient receives a medicine that strengthens inhibitory neurotransmission and reduces neuronal excitability. Sedation and anticonvulsant effects are expected consequences. Which broad CNS mechanism best connects these effects in this introductory pharmacology example?

  1. Additive CNS depression
  2. Enhanced inhibitory GABA signaling
  3. Dopamine receptor blockade
  4. Mu-opioid receptor activation
  5. Blood-brain barrier penetration
Answer and explanation

Enhanced inhibitory GABA signaling

Increasing GABA-mediated inhibition can produce sedation and anticonvulsant effects.

Option explanations

Enhanced inhibitory GABA signaling: Increasing GABA-mediated inhibition can produce sedation and anticonvulsant effects.

Dopamine receptor blockade: Dopamine antagonism can reduce some psychotic symptoms but also affect movement and prolactin. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Mu-opioid receptor activation: Mu-receptor activation contributes to analgesia, respiratory depression and dependence. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Blood-brain barrier penetration: A CNS medicine must reach its central target; physicochemical and transport properties affect access. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Additive CNS depression: Combining sedating agents can impair alertness and breathing more than expected from one drug alone. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
