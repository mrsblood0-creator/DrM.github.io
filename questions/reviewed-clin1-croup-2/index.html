Narrow airway physiology

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A toddler develops substantial stridor from modest mucosal edema during croup, whereas an adult with comparable upper-airway inflammation has only hoarseness. Which anatomic factor best explains the child’s greater tendency toward airflow limitation?

  1. Smaller baseline airway caliber
  2. Greater baseline airway radius
  3. Lower airway resistance before inflammation
  4. A higher density of alveolar surfactant
  5. Greater hemoglobin affinity for oxygen
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Smaller baseline airway caliber

A given reduction in radius occupies a greater fraction of a small pediatric airway and substantially increases resistance.

Understanding the options

Smaller baseline airway caliber
A given reduction in radius occupies a greater fraction of a small pediatric airway and substantially increases resistance.
Greater baseline airway radius
A larger radius provides more reserve against an equivalent thickness of edema.
Lower airway resistance before inflammation
The vulnerable narrow airway, not intrinsically low resistance, explains the disproportionate effect.
A higher density of alveolar surfactant
Surfactant affects alveolar stability rather than the caliber of the inflamed upper airway.
Greater hemoglobin affinity for oxygen
Hemoglobin affinity does not explain the local mechanical narrowing causing stridor.
