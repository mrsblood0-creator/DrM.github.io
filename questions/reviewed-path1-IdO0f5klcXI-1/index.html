Renal infarct architecture

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A patient with atrial fibrillation develops an embolus in a renal arterial branch. Two days later, a wedge-shaped pale lesion contains eosinophilic cells with absent nuclei but recognizable tubular outlines. Which tissue response best explains this appearance?

  1. Coagulative necrosis
  2. Liquefactive necrosis
  3. Caseous necrosis
  4. Fat necrosis
  5. Fibrinoid necrosis
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Coagulative necrosis

Protein denaturation initially preserves the framework of an ischemic solid-organ infarct.

Understanding the options

Coagulative necrosis
Protein denaturation initially preserves the framework of an ischemic solid-organ infarct.
Liquefactive necrosis
This destroys the architecture through enzymatic digestion.
Caseous necrosis
Caseation produces amorphous granular debris, often within a granuloma.
Fat necrosis
Fat necrosis primarily damages adipocytes and may produce calcium soaps.
Fibrinoid necrosis
Fibrinoid change is centered in injured vessel walls.
