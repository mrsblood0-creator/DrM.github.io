Encephalopathy with normal transaminases

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A 25-year-old patient treated with valproate becomes lethargic and confused and begins vomiting after topiramate is added. Glucose is normal and liver transaminases are not elevated. Which laboratory measurement is most important for evaluating a recognized drug complication?

  1. Serum troponin
  2. Thyroid receptor antibodies
  3. Urine porphobilinogen as the only test
  4. Plasma ammonia
  5. Serum urate
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Plasma ammonia

Valproate-associated hyperammonemic encephalopathy can occur despite normal liver tests and is associated with concomitant topiramate.

Understanding the options

Plasma ammonia
Valproate-associated hyperammonemic encephalopathy can occur despite normal liver tests and is associated with concomitant topiramate.
Serum urate
Urate does not identify hyperammonemic encephalopathy.
Serum troponin
There is no myocardial injury presentation.
Thyroid receptor antibodies
These do not evaluate this characteristic interaction.
Urine porphobilinogen as the only test
The medication pattern first raises concern for ammonia elevation.
