Recognizing disease outside the visible throat

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A 45-year-old presents with rapidly worsening odynophagia and difficulty handling saliva. Oral examination shows no tonsillar abscess, yet his voice is muffled and breathing is becoming noisy. What is the best interpretation of the mild oral findings?

  1. A dangerous supraglottic process may be hidden from routine oral inspection
  2. Mild visible tonsillar findings make an airway-threatening infection unlikely
  3. A normal oral examination establishes a noninfectious cause
  4. Symptom severity must correlate with the size of the visible tonsils
  5. Progressive noisy breathing can be followed routinely because there is no abscess
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

A dangerous supraglottic process may be hidden from routine oral inspection

A routine oral examination may not show the epiglottis; severe symptoms out of proportion to visible findings require urgent assessment.

Understanding the options

A dangerous supraglottic process may be hidden from routine oral inspection
A routine oral examination may not show the epiglottis; severe symptoms out of proportion to visible findings require urgent assessment.
Mild visible tonsillar findings make an airway-threatening infection unlikely
The diseased region may lie below the area seen on routine examination.
A normal oral examination establishes a noninfectious cause
Oral inspection alone cannot determine whether deeper laryngeal inflammation is infectious.
Symptom severity must correlate with the size of the visible tonsils
Supraglottic disease can cause severe symptoms without large tonsils.
Progressive noisy breathing can be followed routinely because there is no abscess
Worsening airway symptoms require urgent assessment regardless of a visible abscess.
