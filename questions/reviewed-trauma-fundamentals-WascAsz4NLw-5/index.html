Soft-tissue injury: 5

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A shearing injury separates skin and subcutaneous tissue from underlying fascia. The surface initially looks intact, but perfusion and a fluid collection are concerning. Which soft-tissue injury pattern best explains the hidden tissue-plane separation?

  1. Contusion
  2. Hematoma
  3. Fracture blister
  4. Threatened skin from tenting
  5. Degloving injury
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Degloving injury

Skin and subcutaneous tissue separate from deeper fascia, potentially disrupting perfusion even when the skin initially appears intact.

Understanding the options

Degloving injury
Skin and subcutaneous tissue separate from deeper fascia, potentially disrupting perfusion even when the skin initially appears intact.
Contusion
Blunt trauma can damage tissue and small vessels without a discrete skin opening. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
Hematoma
A localized collection of blood differs from diffuse edema. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
Fracture blister
Severe swelling can separate skin layers and produce blisters over an injury. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
Threatened skin from tenting
A sharp displaced fragment may compromise overlying skin and requires urgent reassessment. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
