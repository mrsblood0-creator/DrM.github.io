Vaccination does not exclude the diagnosis

A fully Hib-immunized adult has severe sore throat, dysphagia and progressive muffling of the voice. The visible tonsils are only mildly inflamed. A colleague dismisses epiglottitis because of vaccination. Which statement best corrects this reasoning?

  1. Other organisms remain possible causes of supraglottitis
  2. The vaccine protects against all encapsulated respiratory bacteria
  3. A mild tonsillar examination excludes infection below the visible oropharynx
  4. Adult epiglottitis requires a history of childhood Hib disease
  5. A previous Hib vaccine series makes airway assessment unnecessary
Answer and explanation

Other organisms remain possible causes of supraglottitis

Vaccination reduces type b H influenzae disease but does not eliminate all infectious causes of supraglottitis.

Option explanations

Other organisms remain possible causes of supraglottitis: Vaccination reduces type b H influenzae disease but does not eliminate all infectious causes of supraglottitis.

The vaccine protects against all encapsulated respiratory bacteria: The Hib vaccine is organism- and capsule-type-specific, not a universal respiratory bacterial vaccine.

A mild tonsillar examination excludes infection below the visible oropharynx: Routine oral inspection does not adequately show the supraglottic structures.

Adult epiglottitis requires a history of childhood Hib disease: Adults can develop supraglottitis without that history.

A previous Hib vaccine series makes airway assessment unnecessary: Current symptoms and airway status determine urgency regardless of vaccination.
