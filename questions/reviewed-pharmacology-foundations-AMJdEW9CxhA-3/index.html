Drug properties affecting response: 3

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A patient switches from an immediate-release tablet to a modified-release product containing the same active ingredient. Peak timing and duration change. Which property best explains the altered concentration-time profile without changing the molecular target?

  1. Molecular size limiting passive membrane passage
  2. Additive pharmacodynamic effects
  3. Complex formation reducing absorption
  4. Formulation-dependent release
  5. Lipid solubility and membrane passage
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Formulation-dependent release

Immediate- and modified-release products can have different concentration-time profiles despite containing the same drug.

Understanding the options

Formulation-dependent release
Immediate- and modified-release products can have different concentration-time profiles despite containing the same drug.
Lipid solubility and membrane passage
Physicochemical properties influence membrane penetration, distribution and sometimes CNS entry. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
Molecular size limiting passive membrane passage
Large molecules often cross lipid membranes poorly without specialized transport or delivery. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
Additive pharmacodynamic effects
Two medicines with similar physiological actions can increase the combined effect or toxicity. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
Complex formation reducing absorption
Binding of some medicines to polyvalent cations can reduce gastrointestinal absorption. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
