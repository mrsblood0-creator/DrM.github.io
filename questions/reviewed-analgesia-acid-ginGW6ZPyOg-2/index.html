NSAID principles: 2

A patient with previous peptic ulcer bleeding is considered for an NSAID. Which class-related complication is especially important when weighing this history, even if the planned medicine has previously relieved the patient’s pain?

  1. Cardiovascular thrombotic risk
  2. Gastrointestinal ulceration or bleeding
  3. Cyclooxygenase inhibition
  4. Reduced prostaglandin-supported renal perfusion
  5. Sodium and water retention
Answer and explanation

Gastrointestinal ulceration or bleeding

Loss of protective mucosal prostaglandins contributes to NSAID-related ulceration and bleeding.

Option explanations

Gastrointestinal ulceration or bleeding: Loss of protective mucosal prostaglandins contributes to NSAID-related ulceration and bleeding.

Cyclooxygenase inhibition: NSAIDs reduce prostaglandin synthesis by inhibiting cyclooxygenase enzymes. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Reduced prostaglandin-supported renal perfusion: NSAIDs can impair afferent vasodilation, especially during volume depletion or reduced effective circulating volume. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Sodium and water retention: NSAIDs can cause fluid retention, worsen blood pressure and aggravate heart failure. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Cardiovascular thrombotic risk: Nonaspirin NSAIDs can increase myocardial infarction and stroke risk; selection considers baseline risk and exposure. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
