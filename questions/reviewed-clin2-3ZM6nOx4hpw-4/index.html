Extrication device versus ongoing care

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A patient with possible cervical injury reaches the emergency department on a rigid long backboard. After the initial resuscitation, trained staff and an appropriate bed are available. What is the best next approach to the backboard while spine assessment continues?

  1. Arrange a coordinated transfer off the board while maintaining indicated precautions
  2. Leave the patient on the board for several days until every study is finalized
  3. Remove all precautions because transport is complete
  4. Have one person pull the patient sideways by the shoulders
  5. Require the patient to stand independently to leave the board
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Arrange a coordinated transfer off the board while maintaining indicated precautions

An extrication device need not remain indefinitely; prompt supported transfer reduces device-related harm.

Understanding the options

Arrange a coordinated transfer off the board while maintaining indicated precautions
An extrication device need not remain indefinitely; prompt supported transfer reduces device-related harm.
Leave the patient on the board for several days until every study is finalized
Prolonged board use increases discomfort and pressure-related risk.
Remove all precautions because transport is complete
Arrival does not itself clear the spine.
Have one person pull the patient sideways by the shoulders
Uncoordinated movement risks unnecessary spinal displacement.
Require the patient to stand independently to leave the board
Potential injury has not yet been excluded.
