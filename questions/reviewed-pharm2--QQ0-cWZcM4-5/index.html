Cardiac history before prescribing

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A 15-year-old with ADHD is being assessed before stimulant treatment. He reports fainting during exertion, and his father died suddenly at age 34. Which next step is most appropriate before prescribing amphetamine?

  1. Evaluate for potentially serious cardiac disease
  2. Start treatment because a normal resting pulse excludes cardiac risk
  3. Use a higher starting dose to test tolerance
  4. Ignore the family history unless the patient has hypertension
  5. Screen only for appetite loss
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Evaluate for potentially serious cardiac disease

Exertional syncope and a family history of sudden death are important warning features requiring further cardiac assessment.

Understanding the options

Evaluate for potentially serious cardiac disease
Exertional syncope and a family history of sudden death are important warning features requiring further cardiac assessment.
Start treatment because a normal resting pulse excludes cardiac risk
A normal pulse does not exclude structural or inherited cardiac disease.
Use a higher starting dose to test tolerance
A drug challenge is not an appropriate investigation of these warning features.
Ignore the family history unless the patient has hypertension
Inherited risk can be relevant even with normal blood pressure.
Screen only for appetite loss
Growth and appetite matter, but they do not address this immediate risk signal.
