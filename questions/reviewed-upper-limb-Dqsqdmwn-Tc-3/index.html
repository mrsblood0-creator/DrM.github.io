Proximal humerus and Neer classification: 3

Imaging shows a displaced surgical-neck fracture and a separately displaced greater tuberosity. The lesser tuberosity remains attached to the humeral head. Which classic Neer part pattern best describes the three major displaced components present?

  1. Three-part fracture
  2. One-part fracture
  3. Two-part fracture
  4. Four-part fracture
  5. Displacement greater than 1 cm or angulation greater than 45 degrees
Answer and explanation

Three-part fracture

A displaced shaft and one displaced tuberosity, with the other tuberosity attached to the head, form a three-part pattern.

Option explanations

Three-part fracture: A displaced shaft and one displaced tuberosity, with the other tuberosity attached to the head, form a three-part pattern.

One-part fracture: In the classic Neer system, fragments not exceeding displacement criteria remain a one-part fracture even if several fracture lines exist. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Two-part fracture: One segment displaced relative to the others creates a two-part pattern in the classic Neer system. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Four-part fracture: The head, shaft and both tuberosities are separated as displaced parts in a four-part pattern. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Displacement greater than 1 cm or angulation greater than 45 degrees: These are the classic Neer displacement thresholds; specific tuberosity treatment thresholds can differ. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
