Minimal change disease: 1

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A child develops edema and heavy proteinuria with preserved renal function. Biopsy shows little light-microscopic change and diffuse podocyte foot-process alteration. Which glomerular disorder best explains this classic childhood nephrotic presentation?

  1. Membranous nephropathy
  2. IgA nephropathy
  3. Infection-related glomerulonephritis
  4. Minimal change disease
  5. Focal segmental glomerulosclerosis
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Minimal change disease

Childhood nephrotic syndrome with little change on light microscopy and diffuse foot-process effacement suggests minimal change disease.

Understanding the options

Minimal change disease
Childhood nephrotic syndrome with little change on light microscopy and diffuse foot-process effacement suggests minimal change disease.
Focal segmental glomerulosclerosis
FSGS is a pattern of segmental scarring affecting only some glomeruli; primary, adaptive and genetic causes must be distinguished. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
Membranous nephropathy
Subepithelial immune deposits and capillary-wall thickening characterize membranous nephropathy. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
IgA nephropathy
Mesangial IgA deposition commonly presents with hematuria rather than the specified classic nephrotic patterns. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
Infection-related glomerulonephritis
An inflammatory nephritic presentation after infection differs from a purely nephrotic podocyte pattern. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
