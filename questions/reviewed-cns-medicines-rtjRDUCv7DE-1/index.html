Suvorexant: 1

An adult with insomnia receives suvorexant. The clinician explains that the drug reduces a wake-promoting signal rather than directly enhancing GABA-mediated chloride conductance. Which receptor action best explains the intended effect of this medication?

  1. Dopamine D2 receptor antagonism
  2. Orexin receptor antagonism
  3. GABA-A positive allosteric modulation
  4. Melatonin receptor signaling
  5. 5-HT1A partial agonism
Answer and explanation

Orexin receptor antagonism

Suvorexant blocks wake-promoting orexin signaling at OX1 and OX2 receptors.

Option explanations

Orexin receptor antagonism: Suvorexant blocks wake-promoting orexin signaling at OX1 and OX2 receptors.

GABA-A positive allosteric modulation: Benzodiazepines and Z-drugs enhance GABA-A-mediated inhibition through the benzodiazepine site. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Melatonin receptor signaling: Melatonin acts on circadian signaling, including MT1 and MT2 receptors, rather than acting as a benzodiazepine. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

5-HT1A partial agonism: Buspirone’s anxiolytic action involves serotonergic modulation, especially partial agonism at 5-HT1A receptors. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Dopamine D2 receptor antagonism: D2 blockade contributes to antipsychotic action and can cause motor and prolactin-related adverse effects. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
