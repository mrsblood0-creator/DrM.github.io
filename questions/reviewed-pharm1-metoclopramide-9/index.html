Recognizing akathisia

Soon after starting metoclopramide, a patient feels unable to sit still, repeatedly paces and describes distressing inner restlessness. There is no elevated mood, grandiosity or sustained muscle spasm. Which adverse effect best matches this presentation?

  1. Oculogyric crisis
  2. Peripheral neuropathy
  3. Akathisia
  4. Acute mania
  5. Tardive orofacial dyskinesia
Answer and explanation

Akathisia

Akathisia is motor restlessness with subjective inner distress and can follow dopamine receptor blockade.

Option explanations

Akathisia: Akathisia is motor restlessness with subjective inner distress and can follow dopamine receptor blockade.

Acute mania: The absence of mood and behavioral features argues against mania.

Tardive orofacial dyskinesia: Tardive dyskinesia produces involuntary movements rather than primarily inner restlessness.

Oculogyric crisis: An oculogyric crisis is sustained eye deviation.

Peripheral neuropathy: Neuropathy causes sensory symptoms or weakness, not this urge to move.
