Transporter interaction

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A 54-year-old transplant recipient takes cyclosporine and is newly prescribed rifaximin for an appropriate intestinal indication. The pharmacist checks interactions before the first dose and notes that systemic rifaximin exposure can rise substantially. Which mechanism best explains this interaction despite the antibiotic’s usual poor absorption?

  1. Acceleration of intestinal transit causing increased clearance
  2. Inhibition of transport processes including P-glycoprotein
  3. Chelation by divalent metal ions in cyclosporine
  4. Induction of bacterial beta-lactamase
  5. Irreversible inhibition of renal proton pumps
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Inhibition of transport processes including P-glycoprotein

Cyclosporine can substantially increase exposure through transporter inhibition.

Understanding the options

Inhibition of transport processes including P-glycoprotein
Cyclosporine can substantially increase exposure through transporter inhibition.
Chelation by divalent metal ions in cyclosporine
This is not the recognized interaction between these agents.
Induction of bacterial beta-lactamase
Rifaximin is not a beta-lactam, and bacterial enzymes do not explain this systemic interaction.
Irreversible inhibition of renal proton pumps
Renal proton pumps are not the relevant disposition pathway.
Acceleration of intestinal transit causing increased clearance
Faster transit does not explain the observed increase in exposure.
