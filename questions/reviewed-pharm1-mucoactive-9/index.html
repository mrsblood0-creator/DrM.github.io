Selecting a mucolytic with an active ulcer

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A 65-year-old man with COPD has troublesome viscous sputum. Oral carbocisteine is considered as an adjunct, but endoscopy performed yesterday showed an active gastric ulcer after a recent upper gastrointestinal bleed. Which feature most directly argues against starting carbocisteine now?

  1. Stable oxygen saturation on room air
  2. Active peptic ulceration
  3. Use of a maintenance inhaled bronchodilator
  4. A history of smoking without current smoking
  5. Presence of a productive cough
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Active peptic ulceration

Active peptic ulceration is a contraindication to carbocisteine.

Understanding the options

Active peptic ulceration
The active ulcer is a product-specific contraindication.
Use of a maintenance inhaled bronchodilator
A maintenance bronchodilator is not, by itself, a contraindication.
A history of smoking without current smoking
Past smoking does not establish a contraindication to this mucolytic.
Presence of a productive cough
Viscous productive sputum is the symptom being targeted.
Stable oxygen saturation on room air
Normal oxygen saturation does not preclude symptomatic mucus treatment.
