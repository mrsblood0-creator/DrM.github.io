Post-influenza destructive pneumonia

An 8-year-old initially improves from influenza, then develops high fever, respiratory distress and rapidly progressive pulmonary consolidation. Imaging shows cavitation and thin-walled air-filled spaces. Which pathogen is a major concern?

  1. Pneumocystis jirovecii
  2. Staphylococcus aureus
  3. Mycoplasma pneumoniae
  4. Bordetella pertussis
  5. Chlamydia trachomatis
Answer and explanation

Staphylococcus aureus

Severe post-influenza pneumonia with tissue destruction and pneumatoceles raises concern for S. aureus.

Option explanations

Staphylococcus aureus: Severe post-influenza pneumonia with tissue destruction and pneumatoceles raises concern for S. aureus.

Mycoplasma pneumoniae: Mycoplasma is more often associated with a less destructive atypical presentation.

Bordetella pertussis: Pertussis typically causes prolonged paroxysmal cough rather than this necrotizing pattern.

Chlamydia trachomatis: Chlamydia trachomatis pneumonia is classically a young-infant illness.

Pneumocystis jirovecii: Pneumocystis is most associated with immunocompromise and diffuse interstitial disease.
