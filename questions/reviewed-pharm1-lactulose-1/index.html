Ammonia trapping in the colon

A 60-year-old man with cirrhosis and overt hepatic encephalopathy receives a poorly absorbed disaccharide. After treatment, stool pH falls and intestinal ammonia absorption decreases. Which chemical change in the bowel lumen most directly accounts for the reduced passive absorption?

  1. Conversion of NH3 to NH4+
  2. Conversion of NH4+ to NH3
  3. Oxidation of ammonia to urea within the colon
  4. Binding of ammonia to albumin in the intestinal lumen
  5. Conversion of glutamine directly to bilirubin
Answer and explanation

Conversion of NH3 to NH4+

The charged ammonium form crosses membranes less readily than uncharged ammonia.

Option explanations

Conversion of NH3 to NH4+: The charged ammonium form crosses membranes less readily than uncharged ammonia.

Conversion of NH4+ to NH3: This favors the more readily absorbed uncharged form.

Oxidation of ammonia to urea within the colon: The hepatic urea cycle, not colonic acidification, performs urea synthesis.

Binding of ammonia to albumin in the intestinal lumen: Protein binding is not the principal trapping mechanism.

Conversion of glutamine directly to bilirubin: These metabolites are not linked by the proposed pathway.
