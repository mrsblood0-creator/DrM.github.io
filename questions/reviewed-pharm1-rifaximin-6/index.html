Severe hepatic dysfunction

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A clinician reviews rifaximin for a patient with Child-Pugh class C cirrhosis. Another clinician argues that intestinal action means liver disease cannot affect its safety. Which correction is most appropriate?

  1. Systemic exposure can rise substantially in severe hepatic impairment
  2. Severe hepatic impairment prevents any intestinal antibacterial effect
  3. All patients with cirrhosis must receive intravenous rifaximin
  4. Rifaximin requires normal hepatic activation to become antibacterial
  5. Renal dialysis reliably prevents the exposure increase
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Systemic exposure can rise substantially in severe hepatic impairment

Poor absorption under usual conditions does not mean exposure is unaffected by severe disease.

Understanding the options

Systemic exposure can rise substantially in severe hepatic impairment
Poor absorption under usual conditions does not mean exposure is unaffected by severe disease.
Severe hepatic impairment prevents any intestinal antibacterial effect
The concern is altered exposure, not proven abolition of local activity.
All patients with cirrhosis must receive intravenous rifaximin
There is no routine intravenous substitution for this indication.
Rifaximin requires normal hepatic activation to become antibacterial
Its activity is not dependent on activation of an inactive hepatic prodrug.
Renal dialysis reliably prevents the exposure increase
Dialysis is not an established solution to this prescribing concern.
