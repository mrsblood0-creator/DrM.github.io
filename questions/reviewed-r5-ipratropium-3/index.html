Urinary symptoms

An older man with bladder outlet obstruction begins an inhaled antimuscarinic bronchodilator. He subsequently has increasing difficulty passing urine. Which recognized adverse effect should be considered when reviewing his ipratropium treatment and other medicines?

  1. Direct stimulation of bladder contraction
  2. Urinary retention
  3. Osmotic diuresis
  4. Nephrogenic diabetes insipidus
  5. Acute uric acid overproduction
Answer and explanation

Urinary retention

Antimuscarinic effects can worsen urinary retention, especially with outflow obstruction.

Option explanations

Urinary retention: Antimuscarinic effects can worsen urinary retention, especially with outflow obstruction.

Osmotic diuresis: This would increase urine output rather than obstruct voiding.

Nephrogenic diabetes insipidus: This causes high-volume dilute urine.

Acute uric acid overproduction: This does not explain the antimuscarinic association.

Direct stimulation of bladder contraction: Muscarinic blockade inhibits rather than stimulates detrusor contraction.
