A dry ischemic digit

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A patient with advanced peripheral arterial disease develops a black, dry, shriveled toe. There is a clear border between affected and viable tissue, without purulent drainage or surrounding infection. Which pathologic process predominates in the affected digit?

  1. Caseating granulomatous inflammation
  2. Enzymatic fat necrosis
  3. Physiologic apoptosis alone
  4. Ischemic coagulative necrosis
  5. Suppurative liquefaction as the initiating process
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Ischemic coagulative necrosis

Dry gangrene is a clinical description of extensive ischemic tissue death, usually dominated by coagulative necrosis.

Understanding the options

Ischemic coagulative necrosis
Dry gangrene is a clinical description of extensive ischemic tissue death, usually dominated by coagulative necrosis.
Suppurative liquefaction as the initiating process
Suppurative infection is more characteristic of wet gangrene.
Caseating granulomatous inflammation
There is no granulomatous infectious context.
Enzymatic fat necrosis
Adipocyte lipase injury is not the dominant process.
Physiologic apoptosis alone
Apoptosis alone does not explain gross ischemic gangrene of a digit.
