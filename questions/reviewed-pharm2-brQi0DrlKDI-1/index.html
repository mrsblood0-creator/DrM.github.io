Numb mouth after a cough capsule

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

An adult with a troublesome dry cough bites a prescribed benzonatate capsule because swallowing pills is difficult. Within minutes, the tongue feels numb and swallowing becomes uncomfortable. Which mechanism most directly explains this new problem?

  1. Local anesthesia of oral sensory nerves
  2. Histamine-mediated gastric acid secretion
  3. Inhibition of pulmonary surfactant synthesis
  4. Activation of central opioid receptors
  5. Suppression of bone marrow precursors
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Local anesthesia of oral sensory nerves

Released benzonatate anesthetizes oral tissues and can impair protective swallowing.

Understanding the options

Local anesthesia of oral sensory nerves
Released benzonatate anesthetizes oral tissues and can impair protective swallowing.
Histamine-mediated gastric acid secretion
Acid secretion does not explain immediate oral numbness.
Inhibition of pulmonary surfactant synthesis
Surfactant changes would affect alveolar stability rather than oral sensation.
Activation of central opioid receptors
Benzonatate acts peripherally and is not an opioid agonist.
Suppression of bone marrow precursors
Marrow suppression cannot produce this immediate local symptom.
