Regular narrow complexes without sinus P waves

An asymptomatic adult’s ECG shows a regular rhythm at 48/min with narrow QRS complexes. Small inverted P waves appear immediately after each QRS in the inferior leads. Which rhythm best explains this pattern?

  1. Junctional escape rhythm
  2. Atrial fibrillation
  3. Sinus tachycardia
  4. Ventricular escape rhythm
  5. Atrial flutter with variable conduction
Answer and explanation

Junctional escape rhythm

The slow regular narrow-complex rhythm with retrograde atrial activation is consistent with a junctional escape focus.

Option explanations

Junctional escape rhythm: The slow regular narrow-complex rhythm with retrograde atrial activation is consistent with a junctional escape focus.

Atrial fibrillation: Atrial fibrillation typically produces an irregularly irregular ventricular rhythm.

Sinus tachycardia: Sinus tachycardia is faster and has normal sinus P waves preceding QRS complexes.

Ventricular escape rhythm: A ventricular escape is generally slower with broad QRS complexes, absent special conduction circumstances.

Atrial flutter with variable conduction: Flutter usually shows organized rapid atrial activity rather than one retrograde P wave per beat.
