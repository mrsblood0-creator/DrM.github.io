Membranous nephropathy: 2

Electron microscopy in membranous nephropathy identifies deposits outside the glomerular basement membrane, on the podocyte side. Which anatomical deposit location best distinguishes this lesion from mesangial or predominantly subendothelial immune-complex disease?

  1. Podocyte foot-process effacement
  2. Near-normal glomeruli on light microscopy
  3. No substantial immune deposits on immunofluorescence
  4. Frequent corticosteroid responsiveness in typical childhood disease
  5. Subepithelial immune deposits
Answer and explanation

Subepithelial immune deposits

Membranous nephropathy deposits lie on the epithelial side of the glomerular basement membrane.

Option explanations

Subepithelial immune deposits: Membranous nephropathy deposits lie on the epithelial side of the glomerular basement membrane.

Podocyte foot-process effacement: Electron microscopy detects loss of normal podocyte foot-process architecture in many proteinuric diseases. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Near-normal glomeruli on light microscopy: Minimal change disease can have striking proteinuria despite little visible light-microscopic change. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

No substantial immune deposits on immunofluorescence: Classic minimal change disease lacks the immune-complex staining pattern of membranous nephropathy. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Frequent corticosteroid responsiveness in typical childhood disease: Typical childhood minimal change disease often responds to corticosteroids; atypical or resistant cases require reassessment. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
