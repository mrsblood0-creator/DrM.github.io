ORIGINAL USMLE-STYLE PRACTICE

New behavioral symptoms

A 14-year-old taking montelukast develops disturbing nightmares and new behavioral changes. The symptoms began after treatment was started. The family asks whether they should wait until the next routine visit. Which recommendation best reflects the drug’s boxed warning?

  1. Replace all inhalers with montelukast alone
  2. Stop montelukast and contact the treating clinician promptly
  3. Double the dose to treat the nightmares
  4. Ignore the symptoms because asthma drugs cannot affect behavior
  5. Continue unchanged until suicidal thoughts develop
Answer and explanation

Stop montelukast and contact the treating clinician promptly

New neuropsychiatric symptoms require prompt action and reassessment of montelukast treatment.

Option explanations

Stop montelukast and contact the treating clinician promptly: New neuropsychiatric symptoms require prompt action and reassessment of montelukast treatment.

Double the dose to treat the nightmares: Increasing exposure is not a treatment for this adverse effect.

Ignore the symptoms because asthma drugs cannot affect behavior: Serious neuropsychiatric events have been reported.

Continue unchanged until suicidal thoughts develop: Waiting for severe deterioration is inappropriate.

Replace all inhalers with montelukast alone: This does not address the adverse reaction and may impair asthma control.
