Predominant coagulation target

Choose the single best answer, then reveal the explanation.

A patient with a newly diagnosed proximal deep venous thrombosis receives subcutaneous enoxaparin. Compared with unfractionated heparin, this medicine has relatively greater activity against factor Xa than thrombin. Which structural feature best explains that difference?

  1. Shorter polysaccharide chains
  2. A direct vitamin K antagonist group
  3. A fibrin-binding catalytic domain
  4. An irreversible platelet-binding group
  5. A direct thrombin-binding peptide
Reveal answer and explanation
Correct answer

Shorter polysaccharide chains

Shorter chains often cannot bridge antithrombin to thrombin but retain antithrombin-mediated Xa inhibition.

Understanding the options

Shorter polysaccharide chains
Shorter chains often cannot bridge antithrombin to thrombin but retain antithrombin-mediated Xa inhibition.
A direct vitamin K antagonist group
Enoxaparin does not inhibit vitamin K recycling.
A fibrin-binding catalytic domain
Fibrin-binding catalytic activity describes fibrinolytic drugs, not heparin fragments.
An irreversible platelet-binding group
Platelet receptors are not the main anticoagulant target.
A direct thrombin-binding peptide
Enoxaparin works through antithrombin rather than directly binding thrombin as a peptide inhibitor.
