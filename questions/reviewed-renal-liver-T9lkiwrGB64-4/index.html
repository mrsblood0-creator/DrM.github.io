Diabetes insipidus and SIADH: 4

A patient with diabetes insipidus cannot obtain enough water and develops hypernatremia. Which fluid-balance problem best explains the rising sodium when the kidneys continue producing large volumes of inappropriately dilute urine?

  1. Arginine vasopressin resistance
  2. SIADH
  3. Osmotic diuresis
  4. Free-water loss
  5. Arginine vasopressin deficiency
Answer and explanation

Free-water loss

Inadequate antidiuresis produces dilute polyuria and can cause hypernatremia if water intake does not keep pace.

Option explanations

Free-water loss: Inadequate antidiuresis produces dilute polyuria and can cause hypernatremia if water intake does not keep pace.

Arginine vasopressin deficiency: Central diabetes insipidus results from inadequate vasopressin secretion and can respond to desmopressin. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Arginine vasopressin resistance: Nephrogenic diabetes insipidus reflects reduced renal response to vasopressin. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

SIADH: Hypotonic hyponatremia with inappropriately concentrated urine can suggest SIADH after excluding relevant alternatives. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.

Osmotic diuresis: Glucosuria or another poorly reabsorbed solute can cause polyuria without primary vasopressin deficiency. This does not best answer the specific question in this case.
