Bronchodilators

  • Beta 2 adrenergic agonists: salbutamol
  • Muscarinic receptor antagonists: ipratropium
  • Methylxanthines: theophylline

Mechanism of action

  • Muscarinic receptor antagonists (inhaled Ipratropium) work by competitively blocking the muscarinic M3 receptors in the airways smooth muscles leading to indirect bronchodilatation through unopposed B2 adrenergic action
  • Ipratropium also work to decrease bronchial secretions
  • Ipratropium also blocks M2 receptors (M2 receptors function as feedback regulators, inhibiting acetylcholine release) so once inhibited, there is increase in acetylcholine release and reduction in extent and duration of ipratropium blockage on M3 receptors; this may lead to paradoxical bronchoconstriction
  • Ipratropium is not sufficient alone for asthma treatment. It is usually combined with Beta 2 agonists to get synergistic effect

Pharmacokinetics

  • Available formulations: nasal spray, metered dose inhaler, and nebulized solution
  • Duration of action is around 4 hours

Therapeutic uses

  • Asthma: according to GINA guidelines, ipratropium is used in acute severe asthma, particularly if the patient is having altered mental status or silent chest; and ipratropium is given with SABA, oxygen and system steroids
  • COPD: according to GOLD guidelines, SABA with/without ipratropium is the initial bronchodilator for acute treatment of COPD exacerbation along with supportive measures

Adverse effects

Inhaled ipratropium formula may lead to the following:

  • Paradoxical bronchospasm
  • Dry mouth
  • Skin flushing
  • Nausea
  • Dyspnea
  • Dizziness
  • Sinusitis
  • Bronchitis
  • Tachycardia, arrhythmia
  • Difficulty urinating and urinary retention
