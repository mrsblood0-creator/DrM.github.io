Airway management

  • Airway management is not well covered in textbooks
  • Airway comes first (ABC) in resuscitation, whatever you do in resuscitation is useless if the airway is blocked

Anatomy of the airway

  • Airway starts from nasal orifices and ends in alveoli, but we manage the upper airway in resuscitation which is supraglottic , (everything above glottis) also everything in neck and above , not thoracic (extra thoracic)
  • Now let’s talk a little bit about anatomy, so we have nasal cavity, pharynx, larynx, trachea, laryngeal opening, epiglottis , vallecula (groove)

Characteristics of the normal airway

  • Patent: open
  • Protected: by neurological reflexes like cough reflex and swallow reflex
  • Maintained: airway stays open if patient position changed or if time passed

Maintained airway

  • In case patient GCS is less than 8 =severe depression in consciousness, This means that other brain functions also severely depressed
  • One of these functions is pharyngeal muscles tone (certain brain neurons is continuously discharging to keep pharyngeal muscle tone) , if depressed then tongue will prolapse posteriorly and airway get blocked (airway is not maintained)
  • In case of angioedema to lips and tongue , this also means airway is not maintained because airway might get blocked in the future when angioedema reaches the pharynx and larynx

When to manage the airway

  • Any problem in patency, protection or maintenance , this will require airway management by creating an artificial airway that has all characteristic of normal airway (patent, protected and maintained), this is called definitive airway
  • Definitive airway is a tube that connect the external environment with the trachea and has a cuff inflated below vocal cords (to prevent secretions from entering the trachea)

Examples of definitive airways

  • ETT tube: tube inserted nasally or through the mouth and enters the trachea and it has a cuff inflated below the vocal cords
  • ETT tube is patent (made of relatively hard material resistant to kinking) , protected by the cuff that prevents secretions from entering the trachea and frequent suctioning and it is maintained because it is open regardless of time and position of the patient
  • Cricothyroidotomy: you open cricothyroid membrane (between thyroid cartilage and cricoid cartilage) you go in trachea , it also has a cuff inflated below vocal cords
  • Tracheostomy: in suprasternal notch we open into the trachea, also has a cuff
  • In emergency department, first choice is ETT tube and if failed you can put a cric or a tracheostomy
  • Cric is more preferred to tracheostomy in emergency settings, because it is faster to make , in tracheostomy you have to dissect through muscles you might hit a bleeder while in cric you go through a thin membrane into the airway
  • Tracheostomy can be permanent while cric can’t be left for more than 72 hours because of risk cartilage necrosis due to pressure from the tube
