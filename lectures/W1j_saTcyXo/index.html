Treatment Regimens

According to the American college of gastroenterology (ACG), we have multiple treatment regimens for H. pylori infection that will be explained in the next slides:

  • Clarithromycin triple therapy
  • Bismuth quadruple therapy
  • Concomitant therapy
  • Sequential therapy
  • Hybrid therapy
  • Levofloxacin triple therapy
  • Fluoroquinolone sequential therapy
  • Rifabutin triple therapy

Clarithromycin Triple Therapy

This is a 14 day regiment and it consist of:

  • PPI: standard or double the standard dose BID
  • Clarithromycin: 500 mg BID
  • Amoxicillin 1g BID or metronidazole 500 mg TID
  • This is the treatment of choice if the strain is susceptible to clarithromycin and metronidazole; and the patient should have no previous history of macrolide exposure for any reason

Bismuth Quadruple therapy

This is a 10- 14 day regiment and it consist of:

  • PPI: standard dose BID
  • Bismuth Subsalicylate: 300 mg BID
  • Amoxicillin (in children < 8) 1 g BID or tetracycline (in children > 8 and adults) 500 mg QID
  • Nitroimidazole (metronidazole or tinidazole) 500mg BID
  • bismuth quadruple therapy is used when resistance to both clarithromycin or metronidazole is not known; and it is used in patients with any previous macrolide exposure or who are allergic to penicillin (older than 8)

Concomitant therapy

This is a 10- 14 day regiment and it consist of:

  • PPI: standard dose BID
  • Clarithromycin: 500 mg BID
  • Amoxicillin: 1 g BID
  • Nitroimidazole: 500 mg BID

Sequential therapy

This is a 10- 14 day regiment and it consist of:

  • PPI (standard dose BID) with amoxicillin (1 g BID) for 5-7 days followed by PPI with nitroimidazole 500 mg BID and clarithromycin 500 mg BID for 5-7 days
  • Sequential therapy should not be given if the strain is resistant to metronidazole or clarithromycin

Hybrid therapy

This is a 10- 14 day regiment and it consist of:

  • PPI (standard dose BID) and amoxicillin 1g BID for 7 days followed by a PPI, amoxicillin, clarithromycin 500mg BID and a nitroimidazole 500mg BID for 7 days

Levofloxacin triple therapy

This is a 10- 14 day regiment and it consist of:

  • PPI: standard dose BID
  • Levofloxacin: 500 mg QD
  • Amoxicillin : 1 g BID

Fluoroquinolone sequential therapy

This is a 10- 14 day regiment and it consist of:

  • PPI standard or double the standard BID and amoxicillin 1 g BID for 5-7 days followed by PPI, fluoroquinolone 500 mg QD and nitroimidazole 500mg BID for 5-7 days

Rifabutin triple therapy

This is a 10 day regiment suggested as a salvage treatment if first line treatment fails and it consist of:

  • PPI: standard dose BID
  • Amoxicillin: 1 g BID
  • Rifabutin: 300 mg QD

LOAD regimen

  • Levofloxacin: 350 mg QD
  • PPI: double standard dose
  • Nitazoxanide: 500 mg BID
  • Doxycycline: 100 mg QD

Choosing a first line regimen

  • Choosing a regimen depends on patient previous antibiotic exposures and if patient has penicillin allergy or no
  • The best regiment for a specific patient is the regiment that the patient had minimal exposure to its antibiotics

If treatment fails

  • Testing to prove eradication should be performed using urea breath test, fecal antigen test or biopsy based testing at least 4 weeks after completion of therapy, if positive result then treatment is failed
  • Bismuth quadruple therapy or levofloxacin therapy is preferred treatment options for patient received clarithromycin containing treatment first; while clarithromycin or levofloxacin containing treatments are preferred if patient received bismuth quadruple therapy first

Who should receive H. pylori treatment

  • All patients tested positive for H. pylori active infection should receive H. pylori treatment

Indications for H. Pylori testing

  • All active PUD patients and patients with history PUD
  • MALT lymphoma patients
  • History of endoscopic resection of early gastric cancer
  • Patients with uninvestigated dyspepsia
  • GERD patients
  • Patients taking long term low dosse aspirin
  • Patients on NSAIDs
  • Patients with unexplained IDA
  • Adults with ITP

Important notes

  • Esomeprazole and rabeprazole are preferred to be used in Asian and Caucasian population because the other options are rapidly metabolized in these patients due to CYP2C19 polymorphism
