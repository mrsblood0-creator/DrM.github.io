Overview

  • Buprenorphine is synthetic opioid, it works as a mixed agonist/antagonist (partial agonist)
  • It was synthesized in late 1960s, it is a derivative of thebaine
  • It is famous for its use in treatment of pain and opioid misuse disorder

Pharmacokinetics

  • Available in sublingual, buccal, transdermal, IV, IM formulas
  • Bioavailability of sublingual is 30%, and buccal is 65%
  • Metabolized by the liver by CYP3A4 and CYP2C8

Onset and elimination

  • Onset of action is after 30 minutes of administration, elimination half life is around 37 hours and duration of action is about 20 hours
  • Excreted through kidney and liver

Mechanism of action

  • Buprenorphine act as a potent partial agonist at mu opioid receptor and antagonist on kappa and delta receptors (mixed agonist antagonist), compared to morphine for equivalent dose, buprenorphine sublingual is around 50 times more potent than morphine and 100 times more potent if used as a transdermal patch
  • Buprenorphine can displace full mu agonists (e.g. morphine) because it is more potent, leading to withdrawal symptoms in opioid physically dependent patients

Pharmacologic effect

  • Buprenorphine provides analgesia that is good for moderate to severe pain, but it can antagonize the analgesic effects of agonist opioids (e.g. morphine) if it used when they are being used on a patient because it produces less effect when it bind to the opioid receptors compared to the full agonist opioids
  • Buprenorphine provides less euphoric effects thus it provides less abuse potential than agonist opioids

Receptor dissociation

  • The risk of respiratory depression is lower when compared to agonist opioids
  • It slowly dissociates from the receptors its occupying so that is why it has long duration of action and mild withdrawal symptoms once withdrawn

Therapeutic uses

  • Used in treatment of chronic pain (as transdermal patch), it has a long duration (24 hours)
  • Also used in treatment of opioid addiction (same with methadone) because of its long duration of action, providing less withdrawal symptoms when withdrawn and blocking the effects of any agonist opioid if they are used during the buprenorphine effect period

Combination with naloxone

  • It is proven that it has shorter and less severe withdrawal symptoms compared to methadone
  • Buprenorphine when used in treatment of opioid misuse disorder, it is used in combination with naloxone and used sublingual or buccal, because this combination if used intravenously, the naloxone would be absorbed and it would bind into opioid receptors leading to acute withdrawal (so it can’t be used intravenously, thus the patient can’t abuse it)

Adverse effects

  • Buprenorphine doesn’t cause respiratory depression unless it is very high dose but once it causes Respiratory depression, it can’t be reversed by naloxone
  • The risk of respiratory depression is higher if buprenorphine is injected IV with benzodiazepines
  • Increased blood pressure because it increase systemic and pulmonary vascular resistance leading

Other adverse effects

  • Prolongation of QT interval (risky in cardiovascular diseases)
  • Psychotomimetic effects such as hallucinations, nightmare and anxiety have been reported after use of drugs with mixed agonist antagonistic actions
  • Mu make the most euphoria and since they don’t activate mu that much so addiction is low

Gastrointestinal and neurologic effects

  • Nausea and vomiting
  • Constipation
  • Headache, mild amnesia, cognitive and neural inhibition
  • Pruritus
  • Risk of seizures if used in epilepsy patients

Pupillary and urinary effects

  • Miosis
  • Urinary retention
