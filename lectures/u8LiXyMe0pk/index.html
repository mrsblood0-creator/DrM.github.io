Physiological Fibrinolysis

  • When a pathogenic fibrin clot is formed inside the human body, plasminogen gets in contact with this clot
  • Then Plasminogen becomes activated into plasmin by the help of tissue plasminogen activators (tPAs) released from the tissues around the clot
  • Plasmin causes the clot to dissolve
  • These endogenous tissue plasminogen activators, bind selectively to the fibrin in the formed thrombus and avoid systemic activation of plasminogen
  • This process is slow, it takes several days to weeks until the clot fully dissolved

Fibrinolytic Drugs

  • In some situations, like in acute MI, we need rapid plasminogen activation to dissolve the clots blocking the coronary arteries and improve blood flow to save the heart from dying
  • And that is when the fibrinolytic drugs came into use
  • Fibrinolytic drugs cause rapid activation of plasminogen to form plasmin and clot will dissolve in few minutes
  • BUT these medications activate both fibrin bound plasminogen and circulating plasminogen
  • This lead to dissolution of both pathogenic thrombi and protective hemostatic thrombi, leading to high risk of bleeding

Fibrinolytic (Thrombolytic) Drugs

  • Streptokinase
  • Urokinase
  • Alteplase
  • Tenecteplase

Streptokinase

  • Streptokinase is a protein that is isolated from streptococci bacteria, it works by converting plasminogen into plasmin indirectly
  • It works by binding to circulating plasminogen, to form a complex that convert additional plasminogen into active plasmin
  • It has lower efficacy compared to alteplase
  • It may cause allergy, fever and hypotension in some people
  • It is relatively cheap and it is the most widely used fibrinolytic med

Urokinase

  • Urokinase is a protease that is formed normally inside the kidney to prevent clotting inside the kidney tubules, it is prepared as a recombinant for medical use
  • It has less incidence of allergy in comparison to streptokinase
  • It works on both fibrin bound plasminogen and circulating plasminogen leading to high risk of bleeding associated with its use

Alteplase and Tenecteplase

  • Those are human tPAs obtained by recombinant DNA technology
  • Alteplase and Tenecteplase have a low affinity for circulating plasminogen in the plasma, but it rapidly activates plasminogen bound to fibrin in a thrombus or a hemostatic plug
  • Alteplase has a very short half life (30 minutes), that is why a portion is injected as bolus then the rest is given by continuous infusion; while Tenecteplase have long half life, this permits administration as a bolus IV injection rather than by continuous infusion

Therapeutic uses of Fibrinolytics

  • Acute MI, ischemic stroke, massive PE, and arterial thrombosis
  • Before giving them, check for any contraindications that prevent giving them; so there would be no liability for bleeding
  • They are not used in DVT and most of the PE treatment due to the high bleeding risk

Contraindications

  • Absolute
  • Recent intracranial hemorrhage (ICH)
  • Structural cerebral vascular lesion
  • Intracranial neoplasm
  • Ischemic stroke within three months
  • Possible aortic dissection
  • Active bleeding or bleeding diathesis (excluding menses)
  • Significant head injury or facial trauma within three months
  • Recent Intracranial or spinal surgery
  • Severe uncontrolled hypertension
  • For streptokinase, previous treatment within six months
  • Relative
  • History of severe and poorly controlled hypertension
  • Severe hypertension at presentation (systolic blood pressure >180 mmHg or diastolic blood pressure >110mmHg)
  • Prolonged (>10 minutes) cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR) or major surgery within three weeks.
  • History of ischemic stroke.
  • Dementia
  • Internal bleeding within 2 to 4 weeks
  • Noncompressible vascular punctures
  • Pregnancy
  • Active peptic ulcer
  • Concurrent therapy of anticoagulants is associated with an elevated international normalized ratio (INR) higher than 1.7 or a prothrombin time (PT) longer than 15 seconds

Adverse effects

  • Bleeding: risk is higher with streptokinase and urokinase
  • Streptokinase may lead to allergy, fever and hypotension
  • Alteplase may cause angioedema, especially if combined with ACE
  • Reperfusion arrhythmia if used in MI

Monitoring

  • Patient receiving fibrinolytic therapy should undergo constant neurologic and cardiovascular evaluation and blood pressure every 15 minutes during treatment infusion and after for at least 2 hours
  • Then half hourly for 6 hours and hourly for 16 hours after injection
  • Treatment should stop when there is signs of neurologic deterioration or bleeding

Treatment of bleeding

  • FFP or cryoprecipitate can help refurbish clotting factors
  • Antifibrinolytic drugs like aminocaproic acid or tranexamic acid can be used to reverse the fibrinolytics effects
