General facts

Liver is the major site of metabolism in the body, metabolism also can occur in the kidney, lung and other body tissues

Some drugs are not metabolized at all and excreted unchanged

Metabolism of drugs may lead to four possibilities

the drug is already active and when get metabolized it gets inactive

the drug is active and when get metabolized it still active

the drug is inactive and when metabolized it gets active

drug converted from non toxic to toxic compound

Phases of hepatic metabolism

Liver metabolizes drugs in two phases

Phase I: convert lipophilic drugs into more polar molecules (water soluble) by oxidation, reduction, hydrolysis

Phase II: conjugation of the drug to water soluble molecule and excretion occurs

The drug enters phase 1 to be excreted as water soluble compound, if not liable to conversion into water soluble by phase 1 then it must enter phase 2 to increase solubility and enhance elimination

Phase I

Most drugs are metabolized by oxidation (phase I)

Inside the hepatocyte, there is vesicles attached to the smooth endoplasmic reticulum and inside the vesicles, there is a lot of different enzymes that catalase phase 1 reactions including CYP450, alcohol dehydrogenase, deaminases, esterases…

Majority of phase 1 reactions done by the CYP 450 family (mixed function oxygenases) located inside membranous vesicles (microsomes) on the surface of smooth endoplasmic reticulum of parenchymal liver cells, CYP450 also present in other tissues like kidney , testis, ovaries and GIT, it works by oxidizing drugs to make it water soluble

Some of phase 1 reactions are non microsomal (they don’t occur inside the vesicles) and examples include xanthine oxidase that convert xanthine to uric acid and monoamine oxidase which oxidize catecholamines and serotonin

CYP450 enzymes

Enzymes involved in oxidation of the drugs (phase I)

Class of enzymes that has more than 50 different enzymes, five of them metabolize 90% of drugs

All of them involved in oxidation of drugs and the most important is the CYP3A4 which is responsible for oxidizing 50% of drugs

CYP450 system is important for metabolism of many endogenous compounds (such as steroids and lipids) and the biotransformation of exogenous substances (drugs, carcinogens and environmental pollutants)

Genetic polymorphism of several clinically important CYP450 enzymes is a source of variability of drug metabolism in humans

Drugs may be metabolized by only one CYP450 enzyme (e.g. metoprolol by CYP2D6) or by multiple enzymes (e.g. warfarin).

CYP enzymes inducers and inhibitors

Some drugs when oxidized by microsomal enzyme (CYP) , they increase the activity of the CYP and those are called microsomal enzyme inducers , they have short half lives and shorten the half lives of drugs that is taken with them and metabolized by CYP

Some drugs when oxidized by CYP, they decrease the activity of the CYP and those are called microsomal enzyme inhibitors,

But most drugs don’t induce or inhibit CYP

Comparison: CYP inducers and CYP inhibitors

CYP inducers CYP inhibitors
increase rate of metabolism of some drugs leading to decrease their serum levels and therapeutic failuredecrease rate of metabolism of some drugs leading to increase their serum levels and toxicity
CYP enzyme Induction requires prolonged exposure to the inducing drug
CYP Enzyme inhibition occur in short period of exposure to the inhibiting drug
examples include phenobarbital, phenytoin, carbamazepine, rifampicin , smoking, saint john wort herbal med
examples include cimetidine , ciprofloxacin , erythromycin , grapefruit , ritonavir (gives powerful inhibition), ketoconazole, omeprazole
Rifampicin (antibiotic) accelerates metabolism of contraceptive pills leading to failure of contraception.
Omeprazole inhibits metabolism of warfarin (anticoagulant) leading to accumulation of warfarin and bleeding.
Phenytoin (anti seizure med) accelerates metabolism of cyclosporine-A leading to graft rejection.
Erythromycin inhibits metabolism of theophylline (bronchodilator) leading to toxicity of theophylline (cardiac arrhythmia).

Phase II

If drug can’t be metabolized by phase I then liver conjugate it with water soluble molecule and excrete it through phase II

The idea of conjugation is to bind drug with another molecule that’s water soluble and the water soluble molecule is mostly glucuronic acid which get conjugated to drugs and excreted through bile (most drugs that is metabolized by conjugation is excreted through bile) , kidney can’t excrete conjugates

Glucuronyl transferase is a set of enzymes that is responsible for the majority of phase II reactions. This set of enzymes is also located inside liver microsomes and also liable to induction by drugs and is a possible site of drug interactions , e.g phenobarbital induces glucuronidation of thyroid hormone and reduces their plasma levels

Some glucuronide conjugates secreted in bile can be hydrolyzed by intestinal bacteria and the free drug can be reabsorbed again by enterohepatic circulation and this extend the action of some drugs

Example: oral contraceptive pill failure

Liver uses another molecules for conjugation other than glucuronic acid and those are the acetic acid, amino acid, glycine , sulfate, glutathione
