According to extent of fracture line

  • Complete fracture and incomplete fracture
  • Complete fracture: The bone split into two fragments or more
  • Incomplete fracture: incomplete split of the bone, periosteum remains continuous in part of the bone.

According to fracture displacement

  • Undisplaced fracture: bone fragments still aligned and in their normal place
  • Displaced fracture: bone fragments out of alignment and shifted from original position

According to fracture pattern

  • Linear fracture: could be transverse, short oblique, long oblique (spiral)
  • Comminuted fracture: more than 2 fracture fragments
  • Segmental fractures: fracture broken to segments, segments longer than fragment
  • Bone loss: part of the bone or all of the bone is lost

According to relation with external environment

  • Closed fractures: overlying skin and soft tissue are intact
  • Open fracture: overlying skin and soft tissue are injured making bone exposed to the external environment
  • Internally open: sharp fractured bone pierces the skin to communicate with external environment
  • Externally open: object from outside causing laceration to skin and soft tissue causing bone to get exposed

According to complexity of treatment

  • Simple fracture: fracture in two pieces, requires reduction and casting (easy to treat)
  • Complex fracture: fracture with multiple pieces (comminuted), requires surgery (harder to treat)

According to force causing the fracture

  • High energy fracture: occur due to high impact force such as severe trauma, road traffic accidents and fall from height
  • Low energy fracture: occur due to low impact force such as ground level falls
