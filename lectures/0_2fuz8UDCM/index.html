Infectious Mononucleosis

Overview

  • it is called infectious mononucleosis because infectious means the disease can spread to other people, mononucleosis because there is increase in mononuclear cells count (reactive lymphocytes
  • It is also called glandular fever and kissing disease
  • Glandular because it is associated with swollen lymph nodes
  • Kissing because it spread through saliva
  • It is primarily spread through saliva but can rarely be spread through semen or blood. Incubation period is about 4 weeks and infected people spread the disease during this period before symptoms develop
  • IM not highly contagious thus isolation of cases is not necessary

Presentation

  • Fever which resolves after 2 weeks
  • Pharyngitis: with tonsillar exudates
  • lymphadenopathy: non tender cervical anterior and posterior, inguinal and axillary lymphadenopathy
  • Splenomegaly
  • Palatal petechiae
  • Periorbital edema
  • Fatigue
  • Death is rare but can occur due to respiratory obstruction, hemorrhage from splenic rupture, thrombopenia and encephalitis
  • In children under 10 years disease is mild, in adults over 30 disease is severe and prlonoged

Causes

  • Epstein Barr virus: about 90% of cases of IM are caused by that
  • Cytomegalovirus: 5-7% of cases

Pathophysiology

  • Virus replicates in epithelial cells in the pharynx leading to pharyngitis
  • Virus infect B cells through CD21 => the body respond to this by activating the cytotoxic T cells which attack the infected B cells resulting in enlarged atypical lymphocytes (downey cells)

Diagnosis

  • Medical history: Highest risk in ages 10-30, close contact with other infected people, fever and sore throat
  • Physical examination by lymph node examination looking for enlargement , and abdominal examination looking for enlarged spleen
  • Heterophile antibody test (AKA mononuclear spot test is positive in IM

Differential diagnosis

  • Acute cytomegalovirus infection and toxoplasmosis present with similar symptoms as IM but differentiated using Heterophile antibody test which will be negative in both of these conditions and positive in IM

Treatment

  • Self limiting
  • Symptomatic treatment
  • Avoid contact sports and weight training to decrease the risk of splenic rupture (because some cases of IM there would be splenic enlargement)
  • If pharyngeal edema is severe, short course of glucocorticoids is indicated
  • Almost all people given amoxicillin or ampicillin antibiotic eventually develop generalized, itchy maculopapular rash because of the immune response triggered by the interaction of the antibiotic with the virus
  • So avoid these antibiotics

Complications

Splenic rupture

  • Autoimmune hemolytic anemia

Lymphoma

  • A lot more, but rare
