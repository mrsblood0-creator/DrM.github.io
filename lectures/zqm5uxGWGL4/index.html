Definition

  • Decrease blood supply to part of the heart, causing damage to heart muscle
  • Mechanism: Atherosclerosis: gradual buildup of cholesterol and fibrous tissue in plaques in the walls of coronary arteries => plaques become unstable, rupture and promote formation of a blood clot that blocks the artery => MI
  • Prinzmetal’s angina: spasm of coronary arteries
  • Other causes: anemia, hyperthyroidism...
  • Cardiac enzymes are the gold standard for diagnosing Myocardial infarction
  • Anatomy: Left anterior descending artery; Left Circumflex artery; Right coronary artery

ACS

  • Unstable angina: worsening of already existing angina. Chest pain at rest or with previously tolerated exertion. Cardiac enzymes: negative
  • STEMI: transmural infarct involves the permanent occlusion of LAD artery or LCx or RCA. Cardiac enzymes: positive
  • NSTEMI: non transmural infarct involves the transient/partial obstruction of coronary artery, or small intramural vessel obstruction. Cardiac enzymes: positive

Patient presentation — classic presentation, STEMI and NSTEMI

  • Chest pain (tightness, squeezing, pressure, crushing), retrosternal, poorly localized
  • Anginal chest pain lasts from 2 to 10 minutes and brought on by exertion and relieved by rest
  • Unstable angina pain lasts from 10 to 30 minutes and it is chest pain at rest
  • MI pain lasts longer than 30 minutes
  • Chest wall tenderness
  • Radiation of the pain to the arms, neck, jaw
  • Sweating, dyspnea, nausea or vomiting

Patient presentation — non classic presentation, STEMI and NSTEMI

  • Occur more frequently in females, racial minorities, diabetics, elderly, patients with altered mental status
  • Chest pain lasting for seconds, or constant pain lasting for 12 to 24 hours without change in intensity
  • Dyspnea at rest or with exertion without chest pain
  • Shoulder, arm or jaw discomfort without chest pain
  • Nausea
  • Generalized weakness
  • Diaphoresis

Watch Out!

  • Do not exclude ACS if the ECG is normal, examination is normal or patient is < 30 years old
  • Epigastric or upper abdominal discomfort, even when relieved with antacids, raises suspicion for ACS, especially for patient > 50 years old or those with known CAD

Diagnosis

  • ECG
  • Cardiac enzymes
  • Blood tests: Electrolyte: derangements predispose to arrhythmia
  • CBC: to identify secondary causes of NSTEMI like anemia and blood loss
  • Liver function tests: useful if patient take drugs that undergo hepatic metabolism
  • BUN and creatinine: for patients taking drugs renally excreted and to asses kidney function in patient with CKD

ECG (STEMI) — hyperacute changes

  • In the first few hours after the myocardial infarction
  • Tall, widened T waves
  • Upward sloping ST segment: ST segment lose normal upward concavity, then straightens and slopes upward before becoming elevated
  • Increase ventricular activation time: interval between the start of the QRS and the apex of the R wave more than > 0.045 sec, because the infarcting myocardium is slower to conduct electrical impulses
  • Increase height of R wave in inferior leads in inferior MI

ECG (STEMI) — acute and chronic changes

  • Acute changes: Start few hours after the MI and continue for hours or days
  • ST elevation: significant if elevated > 1mm in 2 limb leads, or > 2mm in 2 adjacent chest leads, this is the most important ECG change
  • Reciprocal ST depression may occur on the opposite leads
  • Pathological Q waves: reflect electrically inert necrotic myocardium
  • Conduction problem may develop, like LBBB, RBBB
  • Chronic changes: Permanent changes that start few weeks after MI; Q waves remain; Everything else goes back to normal

ECG (STEMI localization)

  • Location — Leads — Coronary artery
  • Inferior — II, III, aVF — RCA 80%, LCx 18%
  • Anterior — V2–V4 — LAD
  • Anteroseptal — V1–V4 — LAD
  • Lateral — I, aVL, v5, v6 — LCx
  • Posterior — ST depression in V1, V2; ST elevation in V7, V8 — RCA
  • Anterior — LBBB — LAD

ECG (NSTEMI)

  • It could be normal
  • Horizontal or down sloping ST depression > 0.5 mm
  • Symmetrically inverted T waves > 2 mm
  • Diffuse precordial ST depression more pronounced in leads V4–V6 may indicate a stenosis in the mid LAD artery and if changes evident in leads V2–V3 it is more suggestive of stenosis of the LCx artery
  • Diffuse ST depression including both precordial and extremity leads associated with ST elevation > or = to 1mm in lead aVR may indicate stenosis in the left main coronary artery, or proximal occlusion in the LAD

Cardiac enzymes

  • Gold standard in diagnosing STEMI and NSTEMI
  • Elevated in STEMI and NSTEMI
  • Troponin I: begins to elevate approximately 6 hours after the onset of chest pain and remains elevated for 1 week
  • CK-MB: specific for cardiac muscle, begins to elevate 6 hours after the onset of chest pain and peaks within 24 hours, normalize in 72 hours post MI

Treatment (STEMI)

  • PCI (percutaneous intervention) angioplasty with stenting is the treatment of choice for STEMI
  • If PCI cannot be performed within 90 minutes of diagnosis then thrombolytic therapy is an alternative, benefits reduce with time
  • Thrombolytic therapy risk exceeds benefits after 12 hours of diagnosis
  • Thrombolytic therapy (tPA, streptokinase)
  • Absolute contraindications: hemorrhagic strokes, non hemorrhagic strokes with 3 months, active internal bleeding, aortic dissection, head and facial trauma in last 3 months
  • Relative contraindications: Hx of severe hypertension, BP of 180/110, major surgery or trauma within month, allergy to streptokinase, active peptic ulcer disease, current use of anticoagulation

Treatment (STEMI, NSTEMI, unstable angina) — beta blockers

  • Beta blockers: slow heart rate
  • Decrease mortality associated with myocardial infarctions
  • Atenolol: 5 mg slowly IV over 5min, repeated once after 15 min
  • Metoprolol: initial dose 5 mg IV every 2 minutes as tolerated for 3 doses then if patient tolerated the first 3 doses then give 50mg orally every 6 hours starting 15 minutes after the last IV dose and if patient intolerant of full IV dose then you give 25 to 50mg according to their clinical condition
  • Contraindicated in uncontrolled heart failure, hypotension, bradyarrhythmias, COPD

Treatment — nitrates and morphine

  • Nitrates: pain relief and decrease afterload
  • Three doses of sublingual nitrate 0.4mg every 5 minutes if no pain relief then
  • IV nitroglycerin: dose should be titrated upwards until symptoms are relieved, and in hypertensive patients until blood pressure normalized
  • Careful blood pressure monitoring while giving nitrate
  • When Side effect like hypotension and headaches occur nitrates should be stopped
  • Nitrate contraindicated if patient took phosphodiesterase type 5 inhibitor within 24 hours, it cause severe hypotension
  • Morphine: pain relief and decrease afterload
  • Dose of 2–8 mg repeated at intervals of 5 to 15 minutes until pain relieved or side effects occur

Treatment — oxygen, aspirin and heparin

  • Oxygen: Only in patients who are hypoxemic (arterial oxygen saturation < 90%) or who have respiratory distress
  • Aspirin: make blood thinner
  • Decrease mortality associated with myocardial infarction
  • Give 160–325mg per oral aspirin within minutes of presentation if patient can’t take oral medications then give 300–600mg per rectum
  • Then continue with maintenance dose of 81–325mg everyday
  • IV heparin: prevent further thrombus formation
