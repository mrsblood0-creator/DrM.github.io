Cough Suppressants (Antitussives) Classification & Mechanism of Action

Definition

  • Cough suppressants (Antitussives) are drugs that reduce the frequency or intensity of cough
  • They are used in patients who have dry cough
  • Mucolytics and expectorants is what is used in treatment of productive cough

Classification

  • Central antitussives: e.g. Dextromethorphan and Codeine
  • Peripheral antitussives: e.g. Benzonatate, camphor, menthol, water steam inhalation
  • Dextromethorphan is what most commonly used out of all the antitussives

Mechanism of action

  • Cough is produced through stimulation of sensory receptors of the glossopharyngeal and vagus nerves, which innervate the mucous membranes in the lower pharynx, larynx, trachea and smaller airways of the respiratory system
  • These sensory receptors transmit the signal to the cough center in the brain which triggers the cough reflex which result in contraction of vocal cords to close the glottis and contraction of the respiratory muscles => this result in sudden increase in intrathoracic pressure followed by relaxation of the vocal cords resulting in fast expulsion of air
  • Centrally acting agents such as dextromethorphan and opioids work by inhibiting the cough center in the brain
  • Peripheral acting agents work by anesthetizing the sensory nerve endings in the throat, trachea and small airways

Dextromethorphan

  • FDA approved over the counter cough suppressant that works centrally by inhibiting the cough center by unknown mechanism
  • Dextromethorphan is a synthetic analog of codeine but it does not have direct action on the opioid receptors, so it doesn’t produce the classic opioid CNS effects or side effects
  • Available as a syrup and liquid filled capsules
  • Dextromethorphan may lead to nausea and GIT discomfort, hallucinations and nystagmus and dizziness
  • In high doses it may lead to euphoria

Codeine

  • Codeine is an opioid that is FDA approved for treatment of pain only; and its use as a cough suppressant is an off label use
  • Codeine work as an agonist on the opioid receptors and it inhibit the cough center
  • It is available as tablets taken orally
  • It has a lot of adverse effects same as with the other opioids; those include constipation, nausea, vomiting, sedation, euphoria, CNS depression (especially in children, that is why it has FDA warning not to be used for cough in patients < 12), urinary retention, bronchospasm, tremor, weakness …
  • For its use as a cough suppressants it is given in lower doses than those required for analgesia so it produces less side effects

Benzonatate

  • It is peripherally acting cough suppressant; there is multiple studies that found that there is inadequate evidence to support effectiveness of safety of this medication in treatment of cough
  • It is thought to work by inhibiting the stretch receptors in the lungs leading to suppression of the cough reflex, it is structurally related to procaine
  • Benzonatate may lead to sleepiness, dizziness, headache, GIT discomfort, hallucinations and allergic reactions
