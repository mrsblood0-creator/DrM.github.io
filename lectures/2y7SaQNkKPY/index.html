Overview

  • The term depression is widely used to describe low mood (sad, empty or irritable)
  • Mood is state of mind, predominant emotion and feeling; e.g. angry, irritable, sad and happy
  • Depression also affects person’s thoughts, behavior and sense of well being
  • Medical term for depression is depressive disorder

Depressive Disorder Types

  • Depressive disorder classified into many types according to ICD-11
  • Single episode depressive disorder (unipolar depression)
  • Recurrent depressive disorder
  • Persistent depressive disorder
  • Mixed depressive and anxiety disorder
  • Other specified depressive disorders
  • Unspecified depressive disorders
  • All these disorder share the presence of sad, empty or irritable mood
  • And they are also accompanied by changes in person’s capacity to function
  • What is different is duration, timing and etiology

Depression vs Mania

Diagnosis

  • In order to diagnose depressive episode, there is a number of symptoms (ICD criteria) that must be present for at least 2 weeks

ICD Criteria

  • Criteria A
  • Persistent low mood
  • Anhedonia: loss of interest or pleasure
  • Anergia: fatigue or low energy
  • Criteria B
  • Reduced concentration and attention
  • Reduced self esteem and self confidence
  • Feeling of guilt and worthlessness
  • Hopelessness about the future
  • Suicidal thoughts
  • Disturbed sleep: insomnia and hypersomnia
  • Appetite and weight changes
  • Mild: ≥ 2 from criteria A & ≥ 2 from criteria B
  • Moderate: ≥ 2 from criteria A & ≥ 3 from criteria B
  • Severe: All of criteria A & ≥ 4 from criteria B

Other Symptoms

  • Psychomotor retardation: slowed movements and thinking, low voice
  • Psychomotor agitation: excessive movements, feeling of inner tension
  • Loss of libido
  • Constipation
  • Amenorrhea

Recurrent Depressive Disorder

  • If more than two episodes lasting more than 2 weeks then it would be called recurrent depressive disorder

Persistent Depressive Disorder

  • Also called dysthymic disorder
  • It is Longstanding mild depressive symptoms lasting 2 years or more
  • Dysthymia often occur with other psychiatric and physical illnesses

Mixed Anxiety & Depressive Disorder

  • Symptoms of both disorders are present for more than 2 weeks but neither meet the criteria for a diagnosis of any of them
  • Very common

Psychotic Depression

  • Most severe type of depression
  • Worries and misdemeanors become beliefs (delusions)
  • The person may also believe that part of them is dead, they also experience derogatory auditory hallucinations
  • Suicide risk is high

Seasonal Affective Disorder

  • Recurrent depression that is related to a certain season
  • Typically during late fall and early winter and the patient goes back to normal the rest of the year

Note

  • Depressive disorder should not be diagnosed in individuals who had a manic, hypomanic or mixed episode before because in this case the diagnosis is bipolar disorder

Epidemiology

  • Globally, 5% of adults suffer from depression according to WHO
  • Females are two times more affected than males
  • Mean age of onset is late twenties

Risk Factors

  • In depression there is no specific cause but there is risk factors
  • Neurotransmitter imbalance: alteration in serotonin, norepinephrine and dopamine and that is what we try to correct with antidepressant drugs
  • Neuroendocrine factors: HPA axis dysfunction due to chronic stress
  • Neuroanatomical changes in structural changes of the brain, especially changes in hippocampus, amygdala and prefrontal cortex due to stress, inflammation and neurotrophic factors
  • Genetic factors: there is 4 genes identified to be related to depression
  • Neuroplasticity: Changes in brain ability to adapt
  • Psychological trauma
  • Cognitive factors: negative thought patterns and distorted cognitive processes

Lines of Treatment

  • First line: Psychotherapy (CBT and other methods)
  • Second line: Antidepressant drugs
  • Other lines:
  • Brite light therapy
  • Electro convulsive therapy
  • Transcranial magnetic stimulation
  • Vagus nerve stimulation
  • Deep brain stimulation

Antidepressant Drugs Classification

  • Monoamine oxidase inhibitors (MAOIs)
  • Tricyclic antidepressants (TCA)
  • Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs)
  • Serotonin norepinephrine reuptake inhibitors (SNRIs)
  • Atypical antidepressants
