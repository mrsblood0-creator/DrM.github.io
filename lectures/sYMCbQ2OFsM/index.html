Intro

  • Ibuprofen is an anti-inflammatory, analgesic and antipyretic
  • Here we will talk about what ibuprofen does to your body

Administration

  • Ibuprofen when taken orally, it would go through the mouth, pharynx, esophagus, stomach and it would get absorbed in the intestine , in the intestine it would get absorbed into the blood, once it is in the blood, then it would get distributed everywhere in the body

Mechanism of action

  • Ibuprofen work as a COX enzyme inhibitor
  • To simplify the COX
  • COX enzyme help produce prostaglandins, prostaglandins mediate pain sensation, inflammation and fever
  • So by inhibiting COX , ibuprofen suppress prostaglandins and achieves its analgesic, antipyretic and anti-inflammatory properties

Side effects

  • Now what could go wrong during all of this? (side effects)
  • It may lead to GIT bleeding
  • To really understand this we have to go back to the prostaglandins, we mentioned that prostaglandins mediate pain inflammation and fever, they also mediate the production of gastroprotective mucous (help protect the staomach against gastric acid)
  • Since ibuprofen inhibit COX => inhibit prostaglandins => inhibit production of gastric mucus => gastric bleeding

Renal side effects

  • Ibuprofen express nephrotoxic properties
  • Prostaglandins has vasodilatory effects on the kidney , they increase renal blood flow
  • This effect is worse during dehydration especially in dehydration

Skin rash (allergic)

  • Headache
  • High blood pressure (prostaglandins also control blood pressure)
  • Bronchospasm (prostaglandisn help in respiratotion )
  • Hepatotoxicity
  • Increase risk of heart attack
