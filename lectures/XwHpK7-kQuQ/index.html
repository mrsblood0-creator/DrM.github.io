Overview

Trade name: Tegretol

Carbamazepine is one of the most commonly used antiepileptic drugs

It was discovered in 1953 by the swiss chemist Walter Schindler

Mechanism of action

Blocks voltage gated Na+ channels in the brain leading to inhibition of the generation of repetitive action potentials that lead to seizures

Therapeutic uses

Carbamazepine used in treatment of focal onset seizures of most types and generalized onset tonic clonic seizures

It can exacerbate absence and myoclonic seizures

It is effective in treatment of Trigeminal and glossopharyngeal neuralgia

It is also used as a mood stabilizer in treatment of bipolar disorder

It is used off label for refractory schizophrenia and it improves both positive and negative symptoms in schizophrenia, also used off label in treatment of neuropathic pain and fibromyalgia

Adverse effects

Mild gastrointestinal discomfort

Mild maculopapular Rash, if appeared then drug should be stopped

SJS is rare, Asians are more susceptible to SJS due to presence of HLA-B*1502 allele which is highly associated with SJS

CNS: diplopia which occur firstly in comparison with other CNS symptoms caused by this medication

CNS: nystagmus

CNS: ataxia

Liver: microsomal enzyme induction (more on that later)

Blood:

Megaloblastic anemia (due to increase folic acid metabolism)

Benign leukopenia

Rarely it may cause agranulocytosis and aplastic anemia (baseline CBC is recommended)

Pregnancy: teratogenic lead to neural tube defects (spina bifida) and craniofacial anomalies (e.g. cleft palate), risk is lower in comparison with phenytoin or valproic acid

Increase ADH secretion: hyponatremia and edema

Pharmacokinetics

Carbamazepine available in oral, and intravenous formulations

Well absorbed with almost 100% bioavailability

Distribution is slow, Vd is around 1 L/kg, and plasma protein binding is 70%

Carbamazepine is metabolized in the liver mainly by CYP3A4

Carbamazepine is a CYP enzyme inducer, specifically CYP3A4 and CYP2B6 and this increase rate of metabolism of other antiepileptic drugs including primidone, phenytoin , ethosuximide, valproic acid and clonazepam and other drugs

Carbamazepine Induces its own metabolism (autoinduction) resulting in lower blood concentrations at higher doses

Its half life is around 36 hours

Excreted through urine

Contraindications

Bone marrow suppression

Hypersensitivity to it or to tricyclic antidepressants (e.g. amitriptyline)

Carbamazepine toxicity

Doses higher than 24 grams in adults are fatal

Toxicity appears after 2 hours of intake and present as impaired consciousness which progresses to coma, also tremor, athetoid movements, dizziness, mydriasis and nystagmus

With higher doses cardiac symptoms appear like ECG changes, tachycardia and shock
