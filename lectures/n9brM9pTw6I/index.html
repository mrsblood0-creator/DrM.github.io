Oxidative stress

Cellular damage induced by accumulation of reactive oxygen species (ROS), a form of free radical

Free radicals are Chemical species with unpaired electron in outer orbit

Physiologic generation

Oxidative phosphorylation

Cytochrome c oxidase transfers electrons to O2 during oxidative phosphorylation

Partial reduction yields O2-, H2O2 and OH radicals

Pathologic generation

Ionizing radiation

Inflammation

Metals (e.g., copper and iron)

Drugs and toxins (e.g., acetaminophen)

Free radical damage

Peroxidation of lipids

Crosslinking of proteins

Oxidation of DNA

Comparison: Free radical and Mechanism of production and Mechanisms of removal and Pathologic effects

Free radical Mechanism of production Mechanisms of removal Pathologic effects
Superoxide (O2-) Incomplete reduction of O2 during oxidative phosphorylation; by phagocyte oxidase in leukocytes Conversion to H2O2 and O2 by superoxide dismutase Direct damaging effects on lipids (peroxidation), proteins, and DNA
Hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) Mostly from superoxide by action of SOD Conversion to H2O and O2 by catalaseCan be converted to OH and CIO- , which destroy microbes and cells
Hydroxyl radical (OH) Produced from H2O, H2O2, and O2- by various chemical reactions Conversion to H2O by glutathione peroxidase Direct damaging effects on lipids, proteins, and DNA
