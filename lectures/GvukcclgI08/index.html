In Pneumothorax, look for:

  • Visible lung edge
  • No vascular markings beyond the lung edge
  • Expiratory phase radiograph shows pneumothorax better

Tension pneumothorax

  • In addition to the lung edge and the loss of vascular markings, you also see:
  • Mediastinal shift
  • Tracheal deviation
  • Depressed diaphragm
  • Life threatening

Fluid alone in the pleural space = curved line (meniscus sign)

  • Air + fluid in the pleural space = a perfect horizontal line

Supine pneumothorax

  • In supine patient the air is not going to be in the lung apex, that is only in the erect patient
  • That is why when patient supine the air will be anterior and posterior in the pleural cavity making it harder to detect pneumothorax
  • In this lead to hyper lucency in the lung bases producing the deep sulcus sign

In supine patient, look for:

  • deep sulcus sign
  • Transparent and sharp diaphragm (because air sitting near the diaphragm making it transparent)
  • Double diaphragm sign

Skin folds can trick you into thinking of pneumothorax

  • To exclude: lung blood vessels run beyond them, skin folds don’t show as a thin white line but it would show as a thick line
