SIADH vs DI

William-Adolphe Bouguereau, Public domain, via Wikimedia Commons · Phee, CC0, via Wikimedia Commons

Overview

SIADH and DI are both disorders affecting the ADH secretion

ADH hormone secreted from pituitary gland and work on the V2 receptor in the collecting duct to absorb more water from the duct into the circulation

SIADH is excessive release of ADH from the pituitary or other sources (such as tumors), this leads to more water being absorbed thus leading to dilutional hyponatremia

Diabetes insipidus is deficiency or inadequacy of ADH secretion leading to decrease water absorption from collecting duct, thus leading to polyuria and polydipsia

DI on two types: nephrogenic (the V2 receptors doesn’t work), or central (the pituitary doesn’t secrete ADH)

Syndrome of inappropriate ADH: the inappropriate in SIADH, make students confused, thinking it could be increase or decrease in the secretion of the hormone, but in reality it is increase but it is variable increase that is why it is called inappropriate

Diabetes insipidus is a different disease than the diabetes mellitus, it is called diabetes because it shares some symptoms with the DM like polyuria and polydipsia, but otherwise it is quite different

Causes

SIADH caused by:

Tumors secreting ADH such as lung cancer

Head injuries

Medications

Any disease affecting pituitary gland or hypothalamus

DI caused by:

Central caused by head trauma, tumors affecting pituitary or hypothalamus

Nephrogenic could be genetic or acquired due to kidney disorders or medications

Presentation

SIADH present with nausea, vomiting, malaise, headache, lethargy and seizures from the cerebral edema caused by hyponatremia

Diabetes insipidus present with polyuria, polydipsia, dehydration and seizures

Urine output

SIADH: urine output reduced and urine is concentrated because body is absorbing more water from the urine

DI: urine output is increased and urine is diluted because body losing more water

Thirst

In SIADH, patients doesn’t feel thirsty as much as normal because they are retaining water

In DI, patients feel extremely thirsty and drink a lot of water because they are losing water

Serum sodium levels

SIADH lead to dilutional hyponatremia (low sodium because of the excessive water in circulation)

DI: normal or high sodium levels

Treatment

SIADH: treat the cause and restrict fluid intake to prevent further load on circulation

DI: treat according to the type and cause, and increase fluid intake
