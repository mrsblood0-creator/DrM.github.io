Mechanism of action

Mepolizumab and Reslizumab are humanized monoclonal antibodies and they are recombinant

They both work by binding to IL-5 and preventing it from binding to its receptor on the surface of the eosinophils => leading to inhibition of the signal for eosinophils proliferation, activation and survival in the bone marrow

Pharmacokinetics

Mepolizumab is available as SC only while reslizumab is available as IV only

Mepolizumab SC absorption is slow, it reaches peak plasma conc in 4-8 days after administration

Mepolizumab is metabolized by the proteolytic enzymes with a half life of 20 days

Reslizumab peak plasma conc are reached after the infusion (since it given IV)

Reslizumab is metabolized by proteolytic enzymes with a half life of 24 days

Therapeutic uses

Mepolizumab is FDA approved for treatment of severe asthma and high eosinophils levels at 150 cell/mcL or higher (before treatment) in patients aged 12 and older, Meplizumab also FDA approved for treatment of eosinophilic granulomatosis with polyangiitis and hypereosinophilic syndrome

Reslizumab is FDA approved for for treatment of severe asthma and high eosinophils levels at 400 cell/mcL or higher (before treatment) with at least one asthma exacerbation in the previous year in patients aged 18 and older

Adverse effects

Both of these medications are targeted therapy, that is why it is considered to have an excellent safety profile

Mepolizumab may lead to:

Injections site reactions

Hypersensitivity reactions

Headache, back pain, abdominal pain and muscle spasms

Fatigue

Influenza, UTI

Pruritus, eczema

Reslizumab may lead to:

Elevated creatine phosphokinase

Oropharyngeal pain

Muscle pain

Hypersensitivity reactions

Malignancy

Contraindications

Previous hypersensitivity reactions to Mepolizumab and reslizumab

Patients must be closely observed for several hours after Mepolizumab/ reslizumab administration due to risk for anaphylaxis
