Mucoactive Drugs: N-Acetylcysteine, Guaifenesin and Carbocysteine

Definition

  • Mucoactive medications are medications that aid in the clearance of mucus from the airways

Classification

Mucoactive drugs are classified into:

  • Mucolytics: reduce the viscosity of the mucus by breaking down the chemical bonds between molecules of the mucus; e.g. N acetylcysteine and bromhexine
  • Expectorants (secretolytics) increase volume of airway secretions making them easier to expel through coughing; e.g. guaifenesin
  • Mucokinetics: increase ciliary transport of mucus. E.g. bronchodilators and surfactant
  • Mucoregulators: suppress underlying mechanisms of mucus hypersecretion. E.g. Carbocysteine
  • Some of these medications work by multiple mechanisms

Therapeutic uses

  • These medications are used in treatment of productive cough in respiratory diseases that are complicated by over secretion or higher viscosity of mucus such COPD, Asthma and cystic fibrosis
  • Mucoactive agents are used in treatment of productive cough while antitussives are used in treatment of dry cough
  • It is important to confirm the diagnosis before giving these medications; because most common cause for productive cough is infections and these medications may aggravate infections
  • Because the mucus layer entraps and clear bacteria and inhibit bacterial growth and biofilm formation . It also protect the airway from inhaled irritants and fluid loss

N- Acetylcysteine

  • Acetylcysteine is a mucolytic, it has free sulfhydryl (-SH) groups that break disulfide bonds that connect the mucin oligomers in the mucus leading to reduction in mucus viscosity; it also has anti inflammatory and antioxidant properties
  • N acetylcysteine is FDA approved for treatment of abnormal viscid or inspissated mucous secretions in pneumonia, bronchitis, cystic fibrosis, postoperative pulmonary complications, tracheostomy patients…; it is also FDA approved for treatment of paracetamol toxicity
  • N acetyl cysteine is available as oral, IV and inhalational formulations
  • According to the American college of chest physicians; oral NAC is recommended for patients with moderate to severe COPD to prevent exacerbations
  • NAC adverse effects: GIT symptoms with oral NAC, anaphylactic reactions (e.g. bronchospasm) with IV NAC because it triggers histamine release; treat with anti histamine meds

Bromhexine

  • Bromhexine acts on the mucus at the formative stages within the mucus secreting cells. It disrupts the structure of acid mucopolysaccharide fibers in mucoid sputum and produces a less viscous mucus which is easy to expel
  • Because bromhexine can disrupt the gastric mucosal barrier, it should be avoided in patients with peptic ulcer
  • Bromhexine also may lead to headache, vertigo, sweating and allergic reactions

Guaifenesin

  • Guaifenesin act as an expectorant by relaxing bronchial smooth muscle tone and by irritating the respiratory tract leading to increasing the volume of fluid secreted in there => more fluid inside the mucus => mucus easier to expel
  • Guaifenesin available as capsules and syrup used in treatment of productive cough
  • Guaifenesin may lead to headache, drowsiness, diarrhea and insomnia

Carbocysteine

  • Carbocysteine suppress underlying mechanisms of mucus hypersecretion and increases fluid volume inside the mucus
  • It is very effective and safe; it is used especially in Asthma and COPD
  • Carbocysteine may lead to allergic side effects
