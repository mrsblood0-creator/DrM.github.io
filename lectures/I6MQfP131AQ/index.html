Overview

  • Trade name: Zarontin
  • The least toxic antiepileptic drug
  • It is mainly used in treatment of absence seizures
  • Ethosuximide was first synthesized in 1957 and was a big help to children with absence seizures

Mechanism of action

  • Ethosuximide work by blocking the T type Ca+2 channels in thalamocortical neurons

Therapeutic uses

  • First line drug for treatment of absence seizures
  • It can be used as monotherapy for absence seizures, unless generalized tonic clonic seizures are associated with the absence seizures and in that case valproate is preferred

Dosing

  • In children
  • Starting dose is 10- 15 mg/kg/day
  • Maintenance dose is 15- 40 mg/kg/day
  • In adults
  • Starting dose is 250 mg/day and titrated up until response occur
  • Max dose is 1500 mg/day

Adverse effects

  • Ethosuximide is well tolerated and less toxic than other antiepileptics
  • GIT symptoms: abdominal pain, nausea and vomiting
  • Headache and dizziness
  • Euphoria
  • It may rarely lead to psychosis, depression and lupus

Pharmacokinetics

  • Ethosuximide is available as oral formulations in form of capsules and oral suspension
  • Absorption is almost complete with bioavailability of 90% and peak plasma levels reached within 3 hours after oral administration
  • Distribution: ethosuximide doesn’t bind to plasma proteins and readily crosses the BBB to exert its effects
  • 80% of Ethosuximide dose is metabolized by the liver mainly by CYP3A4 to inactive metabolites
  • 20% is excreted unchanged in the urine
  • Its half life is around 40 hours

Drug interactions

  • Valproic acid inhibit ethosuximide metabolism and decrease ethosuximide clearance leading to higher concentrations of the drug in the blood

Contraindications

  • Hypersensitivity reactions to the medications itself or to the succinimides family of medications
