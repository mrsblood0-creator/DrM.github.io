Overview

  • Famous trade name: Neurontin
  • Gabapentin is an analog of GABA, but it doesn’t work on the GABA receptors
  • It was discovered in 1975 by Satzinger & Hartenstein and got FDA approval for treatment of epilepsy in 1993

Mechanism of action

  • it doesn’t work at the GABA receptors
  • The precise mechanism is not fully understood, but it seem to be related to inhibiting the alpha 2 delta voltage gated calcium channels => that lead to decrease the excitatory neurotransmitters release into the synapse

Therapeutic uses

  • It is FDA approved for treatment of:
  • Postherpetic neuralgia (type of neuropathic pain)
  • Add on therapy for focal onset seizures
  • Moderate to severe restless leg syndrome
  • Off label uses:
  • Other types of neuropathic pain: e.g. diabetic neuropathy and trigeminal neuralgia, it is NOT used for sciatica
  • Fibromyalgia, bipolar disorder, anxiety, mood disorders, depression, IBS, alcohol withdrawal, headache …

Adverse effects

  • CNS: ataxia
  • CNS: nystagmus
  • Headache, tremor and dizziness
  • Fatigue and sleepiness
  • Most of these adverse effects occur initially in the start of the treatment and go away with time
  • Rarely may lead to suicidal ideation, Steven Johnson syndrome, respiratory depression and rhabdomyolysis
  • Withdrawal symptoms occur after abrupt stopping of this medications, patient agitated, confused and disoriented followed by GI complaint and sweating, tremor tachycardia, hypertension and insomnia also occur
  • Dependence also occur with this medication

Pharmacokinetics

  • Gabapentin is absorbed via LAT2 amino acid transporter in the upper intestine, this absorption system is saturable, this lead to bioavailability of 80% if only 100 mg is given per dose and 60% bioavailability if 300mg given and so on
  • Gabapentin is not bound to plasma proteins, the full dose get distributed to body tissues including the brain
  • Gabapentin undergoes minimal metabolism
  • Eliminated renally with short Elimination half life of around 6 hours

Dosing

  • Initially start with 300 mg/ day and titrate up to 3 times per day of the same dose, max dose is 4800 mg/day
  • In renal impairment dose should be reduced

Gabapentin toxicity

  • Drowsiness
  • Sedation
  • Blurred vision
  • Slurred speech
  • Anxiety
  • Respiratory suppression
  • Coma and death
