Definition

MAS is neonatal respiratory distress that occurs when neonate pass meconium in utero leading to meconium stained amniotic fluid and aspirate it during labor resulting in respiratory distress

MAS is diagnosed only if the respiratory distress can not be attributed to another cause like pneumonia, sepsis, TTN, RDS, CHD

MAS symptoms can range from mild RS distress to severe RS distress with respiratory failure

Epidemiology

Meconium stained amniotic fluid is seen in 10-20% of all deliveries

5% of those infants have meconium stained amniotic fluid develop MAS

Mortality is about 2% of MAS infants

MAS is common in infants with breech presentation

Causes

Fetal distress and hypoxia: hypoxic stress on the fetus => enhance intestinal peristalsis and relax anal sphincter => passage of meconium into the amniotic fluid

Fetal maturity: intestinal parasympathetic innervation and myelination increases in later pregnancy => parasympathetic increase intestinal peristalsis => passing of meconium into amniotic fluid

Pathophysiology

Baby passes meconium in utero due to the causes mentioned before

During the delivery process, hypoxia cause the fetus to start breathing and gasping which lead to aspiration of the meconium stained amniotic fluid into the lungs

Meconium is thick material (contains cellular debris of skin, GI tract and hair, also contains GI secretions, blood, and mucus) when aspirated, it blocks the respiratory airways (same with foreign bodies), so meconium can cause lung atelectasis when it completely obstruct the right or left bronchi, or it can cause partial obstruction

Meconium triggers inflammation, in 24-48 hours after birth the airways get inflamed in form of chemical pneumonitis and respiratory distress occurs

Inflammation lead to inactivation of surfactant => surfactant deficiency lead to increase surface tension of the alveoli => alveoli collapsing => which furtherly aggravate the respiratory distress

Blood supply to the Obstructed airways and collapsed alveoli would be decreased due to vasoconstriction, as an attempt from the lungs to shunt more blood towards the open airways to maintain oxygenation

If the previous process occurs extensively in the lungs, this will lead to substantial increase vascular resistance in the lungs which lead to a condition called persistent pulmonary hypertension of the newborn (PPHN), PPHN may cause right heart failure

All of these factors lead to ventilation- perfusion mismatch => hypoxemia

Meconium obstruct airways leading to air trapping, overdistension of the lungs, extra alveolar air leaks and subsequently pneumothorax

Risk factors of MAS

Fetal distress during labor

Post term pregnancy: the greater infant’s gestational age, the greater the risk of passing meconium in utero

Prolonged labor: increase chance of fetal hypoxia

maternal related risk factors: diabetes, hypertension => Placenta problems => increase resistance to blood flow through the placenta => fetal hypoxia

IUGR

Maternal substance abuse: tobacco, amphetamines or cocaine

Clinical features

A term or post term infant present with respiratory distress and meconium stained amniotic fluid

Respiratory distress present as tachypnea (> 60/m), intercostal and subcostal retraction, nasal flaring, and use of accessory muscles

On physical exam, there might be signs of post maturity like peeling skin, long fingernails and vernix

Diagnosis

Chest x ray shows:

Multiple patchy densities bilaterally

Hyperinflation

Flattening of diaphragm

Atelectasis

Collapsed lung due to pneumothorax

Oxygen saturation

Arterial blood gas analysis: to evaluate for respiratory failure and acid-base balance

Echocardiography to look for right ventricular dysfunction due to PPHN

Blood cultures looking for sepsis, empirical antibiotic should be considered

Treatment

Infant born to meconium stained amniotic fluid should have routine postnatal care with monitoring for signs of respiratory distress, endotracheal suctioning is no longer recommended

If infant developed respiratory distress then management is supportive

Oxygen supplementation is needed if oxygen saturation drops below 95%

Assisted ventilation techniques are used if the hypoxemia is refractory to oxygen therapy

Blood glucose

Surfactant maybe needed

Nitric oxide is pulmonary vasodilator that can be used in persistent pulmonary hypertension of the newborn

Glucocorticoids can be used to suppress inflammation

Antibiotics are used if cultures positive

Complications

PPHN

Pneumothorax

Neonatal hypoxic-ischemic encephalopathy

Prevention

If meconium stained amniotic fluid is seen by obstetrician then obstetrician should suction the infant oropharynx before delivering the rest of their body
