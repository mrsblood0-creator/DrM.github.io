Intro

  • LMWH are classified as anticoagulant medications
  • Unfractionated heparin is a mixture of different molecular weight fractions (5000- 40000 Da) that can work on multiple coagulation factors and produce multiple side effects (e.g. thrombocytopenia)
  • LMWH is the fractions of heparin that works on factor X only, molecular weight of these fractions is less than 8000 Da; this lead to minimal effects on PLT and other clotting factors

LMWH examples

  • Dalteparin
  • Enoxaparin (Levonex, Clexan)

Pharmacokinetics

  • LMWH available as SC formulas
  • SC bioavailability is better than the unfractionated heparin (UFH binds to subcutaneous tissue more than LMWH)
  • Half life of LMWH is long enough to allow for once daily dosing while UFH half life is short, that’s why it is giving 3 times daily

Mechanism of action

  • LMWH bind to antithrombin III and activate it to inhibit factor Xa

Therapeutic uses

  • Prophylaxis against DVT in medium and high risk groups
  • Treatment of venous thromboembolism in pregnancy
  • Used in the treatment of DVT, PE
  • Treatment of STEMI
  • Unstable angina
  • Prevention of clotting in extracorporeal circuits

Monitoring

  • LMWH doesn’t require monitoring but if needed (in patients at extremes of weight or in renal dysfunction), it is by anti factor Xa assay
  • In comparison with the UFH, which need continuous monitoring with the APTT

Adverse effects

  • Bleeding: risk for bleeding with LMWH is much less compared to the UFH
  • Thrombocytopenia: much less common in LMWH compared to UFH
  • Osteoporosis and spontaneous fractures especially on long term use
  • Hypersensitivity reactions
  • Hypoaldosteronism

Antidote

  • Protamine sulfate work as an antidote but it is weaker in comparison with its work on UFH

Contraindications

  • Trauma
  • Hemorrhagic disorders
  • Peptic ulcer disease
  • Recent cerebral hemorrhage
  • Severe hypertension
  • Recent surgery to the eye or the brain
  • History of heparin induced thrombocytopenia
